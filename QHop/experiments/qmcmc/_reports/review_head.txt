1. VERIFIED FACTS FROM THE BASIS PAPERS (Wave 1, reports A1 and A2)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
P1 (arXiv:2203.12497, Nature 619, 282). Read: arXiv v1 with the 40-page supplement (SI page N = PDF page N+9).
- H = (1-gamma)*alpha*H_prob + gamma*sum X, alpha = sqrt(n)/sqrt(sum J^2 + sum h^2); gamma ~ U[0.25,0.6], t ~ U[2,20],
  ranges "ad hoc ... trial and error" [P1 Alg. S1, Eqs 5-8]. H_prob is not divided by T: the proposal ignores temperature.
- Symmetry needs real symmetric H (U = U^T) or a palindromic H(t) [P1 Eq 4, S40-S46]. Bare hardware circuits "clearly
  break" symmetry; twirling restored it statistically [P1 SI p.17, p.22].
- Exact simulation n=3-10, 500 SK-like instances per n, T=1: k_quantum 0.264(4), k_local 0.94(4), k_uniform 0.948(7) [P1 Fig 2c].
- Temperature: advantage claimed only for T <~ 1; at high T the uniform gap goes to 1 and the quantum gap stays near 0.3
  [P1 Fig 2a, S2, p.4]. With unscaled N(0,1) couplings, T=1 is far below the glass transition (T_c ~ sqrt(n)), so P1's
  regime is LOW in this review's definition [CALC].
- Hardware: ibmq_mumbai (27-qubit device), three 1D instances n=8,9,10; chains run offline from cached shots because the
  control stack was "impractically slow" [P1 SI p.20]. (An earlier project note "hardware to n=27" was wrong.)
- Baselines: local, uniform; Swendsen-Wang/Wolff/Houdayer only on the 3 hardware instances; parallel tempering explicitly
  excluded [P1 SI p.14]. No wall-clock, cost-per-step or autocorrelation numbers anywhere in P1.
P2 (arXiv:2405.04247 v2; Phys. Rev. Research 7, 013231 (2025), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.7.013231).
- Effective field h~_j = h_j + sum_{i not in g} J_ji s_i, group couplings = J restricted to g [P2 Eq 13-14].
- Only three grouping strategies, all consecutive index blocks at a random start [P2 Sec IV].
- Gap exponents T=1, n=4-10: uniform 0.97, local 0.92, full QeMCMC 0.335, single group 0.64-0.77, sequential multi-group
  0.50 [P2 Fig 3]; at T=0.1 single groups are worse than uniform [P2 Table I]; T=10 exponents QeMCMC 0.087, local 0.26,
  uniform 0.341 [P2 Table I, App B].
- Detailed balance is asserted, not proved [P2 p.11]. Own result: single-group strategies are symmetric; the fixed-order
  sequential sweep is NOT [CALC, SIM B7 V02: TV bias 0.061]; random forward/reverse order or per-group accept fixes it.
- Noise untested; "Concrete analysis for the level and types of noise under which we maintain the quantum scaling
  advantage is an important open question" [P2 p.10-11]. Small-group advantage is "quantum inspired" [P2 footnote 12].


2. TEMPERATURE REGIMES (definitions used everywhere)
----------------------------------------------------
Defined per instance family by beta_c = specific-heat peak of the exact Boltzmann distribution, cross-checked by where the
local-proposal gap starts to collapse:
  HIGH   beta <= 0.5 beta_c (single-spin Metropolis mixes in a few sweeps)
  MEDIUM 0.5 beta_c < beta <= 2 beta_c (critical / crossover region)
  LOW    beta > 2 beta_c (local moves trapped; P1 and P2's regime)
Measured boundaries [SIM D1, D8, D9]: SK n=10 (J,h ~ N(0,1) unscaled) beta_c = 0.41 [0.32-0.55]; 2D EA 3x3 beta_c = 0.65;
2D ferromagnet 3x3/3x4 beta_c = 0.40/0.41; fixed-composition n=12: CuAu-like Tc 2.88-3.43, Coulomb Tc 0.40-0.50, SK
Tc 0.68-0.84 (D8 used LOW=0.5 Tc, MEDIUM=1.1 Tc, HIGH=3 Tc). Planted inference posteriors: the specific-heat peak is NOT a
glass marker (it tracks collapse onto the planted vector); there the matched temperature beta_match = 1 is the reference.
Bayesian posteriors sampled at the matched temperature lie on the Nishimori line, where no spin-glass phase exists
(Zdeborova and Krzakala, arXiv:1511.02476, Sec II.7) [LIT]; confirmed numerically in D6 [SIM].


3. BREAK-EVEN MODEL (convention for all rows)
---------------------------------------------
Wall-clock speedup = (tau_C/tau_Q) x (t_C/t_Q); S* = t_Q/t_C.
t_C: best classical step; cheap dense Ising ~30 ns per proposal at n<=100 (measured on this laptop, A5 bench,
  QHop/experiments/qmcmc/bench/sk_bench.c); sparse lattices 0.15 ns/flip CPU (Isakov et al. arXiv:1401.1084), ~2-3 ps GPU/FPGA.
t_Q (A4 hardware scan, sources in A4-hardware.txt; D4 compilation):
  optimistic  best published per-step latency of the best-fitting device: D-Wave 0.03 s (16 ms QPU access + 10-20 ms
              overhead, D-Wave timing docs); IBM 2 s (documented ~2 s per sub-job); Quantinuum Helios in-program loop: D4
              estimate ~8 s per step for SK n=10 at t=6 (gate time dominated), not the 0.1-0.5 s first estimated in A4.
  realistic   IBM Heron session ~3 s per step (queue excluded; derived from Marshall et al. arXiv:2602.06171v3), D-Wave 0.5 s.
  pessimistic realistic t_Q and the step advantage eroded by noise: required ideal S = S*_realistic / F, F = D4 proxy (1-e2)^N_2q (1-e1)^N_1q (1-ro)^n
              with e2 = 2e-3 (Heron median) and N_2q from D4 (e.g. SK n=10 at t=6: 2,160 CZ on heavy-hex, F ~ 3e-3).
Cheap dense Ising: S* = 7e7 (IBM optimistic) / 1e8 (IBM realistic) / ~3e10 (pessimistic, SK n=10); D-Wave 1e6 / 1.7e7.
Expensive energy t_E: S* = (t_Q + t_E)/t_E -> 1 as t_E grows (e.g. t_E = 1 s: 3 / 4 / 4/F).
Batching many independent chains per IBM job (only when the application needs many chains): ~0.85 ms per chain-step,
S* ~ 3e4 for cheap energies [CALC, B7].
Note: C1-C4 master-table rows computed the optimistic S* with the superseded Helios 0.1 s figure (e.g. 3e6 for 30 ns
energies, 28 rows) and pessimistic F as (1-2e-3)^N_2q; with the D4 values optimistic S* rises (IBM 7e7, D-Wave 1e6);
no verdict changes (F1 check).
