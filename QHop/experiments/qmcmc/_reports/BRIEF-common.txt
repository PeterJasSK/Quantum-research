ALGORITHM (P1, Layden et al., Nature 619, 282 (2023), arXiv:2203.12497). Each MCMC step: prepare basis state |s>, apply
U=exp(-iHt), H=(1-gamma)*alpha*H_prob + gamma*sum_j X_j, alpha = sqrt(n)/sqrt(sum J^2 + sum h^2), gamma~U[0.25,0.6], t~U[2,20]
drawn per step ("ad hoc, trial and error"); measure s'; classical Metropolis accept min(1, pi(s')/pi(s)). H real symmetric -> U=U^T
-> proposal symmetric -> exact Boltzmann stationarity for ANY H_prob (even a wrong one); noise breaks exact symmetry (P1 SI p.17,22:
needed twirling; small bias possible). H_prob is not divided by T: the proposal is temperature-independent.
P1 results: exact sim only n=3..10, SK instances, T=1: gap ~ 2^(-k n), k_quantum=0.264(4), k_local=0.94(4), k_uniform=0.948(7)
(Fig 2c). Advantage only for T <~ 1; at high T uniform gap -> 1 while quantum gap stays ~0.3 (Fig 2a). Hardware: ibmq_mumbai, n=8-10,
1D instances, chains run OFFLINE from cached shots because the control stack was "impractically slow"; hardware gap ~3x below ideal.
Baselines compared: local, uniform; Swendsen-Wang/Wolff/Houdayer only on 3 instances; NO parallel tempering, NO wall-clock numbers.
P2 (Ferguson & Wallden, Phys. Rev. Research 7, 013231 (2025), arXiv:2405.04247): coarse-grained QeMCMC. Pick a group g of spins,
rest frozen as effective field h~_j = h_j + sum_{i not in g} J_ji s_i, quantum proposal on the group only (~sqrt(n) qubits). Groups =
consecutive index blocks at random start. Gap exponents at T=1 (n=4..10): uniform 0.97, local 0.92, full QeMCMC 0.335, single group
0.64-0.77, sequential multi-group sweep 0.50. Single-group strategies are symmetric; the fixed-order sequential multi-group sweep is
NOT symmetric in general (no proof in P2) and needs random order or per-group accept. Noise untested; "quantum inspired" at small
group size because the group proposal is classically simulable. Evidence = noiseless statevector, n<=10 fits, 1 instance at 16/25/36.

TEMPERATURE REGIMES (defined per instance, not by raw number):
LOW = below glass/ordering transition, local proposals trapped (P1/P2 claim advantage here).
MEDIUM = around transition/crossover, critical slowing down; classical tools = cluster moves (Wolff, Swendsen-Wang) where they
apply, parallel tempering.
HIGH = well above transition, single-spin Metropolis already mixes fast (gap near max). Here a quantum case can only come from:
very expensive energy evaluation, need for decorrelated samples per step, mixing across hard constraints, or many-temperature ensembles.

BREAK-EVEN MODEL: wall-clock speedup = (tau_C/tau_Q) x (t_C/t_Q); t_C = t_propose_C + t_energy;
t_Q = t_latency + t_compile/amortized + t_shot + t_readout + t_energy. Energy is evaluated in the accept step in BOTH chains, so if
t_energy dominates, per-step time ratio -> 1 and step advantage S = tau_C/tau_Q becomes wall-clock. Break-even S* = t_Q/t_C.
Rough hardware context (to be replaced by the team's sourced hardware scan): classical single-spin flip ~1-100 ns on CPU/GPU, p-bit
and FPGA faster; a sequential QeMCMC step on cloud gate-based hardware costs ~0.1 s to seconds or more (latency dominated; shot itself
~1e-4 to 1e-3 s on superconducting; ms-to-s on ions and neutral atoms); P1 had to run offline. So for cheap energies S* ~ 1e6-1e9.
