======================================================================================================
0. BOTTOM LINE
======================================================================================================
1. Real-time physical-layer use is dead, and not narrowly. A 5G NR slot is 1 ms at 15 kHz subcarrier spacing and
   0.125 ms at 120 kHz. Every resource element carries its own detection problem, so a 100 MHz carrier needs about
   1e8 detection problems per second [OWN, section 2]. The fastest documented quantum round trip is a D-Wave QMI at
   about 16 ms of QPU access plus 10-20 ms overhead (A4). Gate-based cloud steps take seconds (A4). That is 6 to 9
   orders of magnitude too slow, before any MCMC chain length is counted. Every "detect/decode/schedule/precode
   inside the frame" idea is KILLED cleanly (B4-01, 02, 06, 09, 12, 13, 16, 18, 19).
2. The temperature physics does not match the P1/P2 evidence. P1, P2 and the pilot measured unplanted SK spin glasses
   at low T. A telecom posterior sampled at the matched temperature (T set by the noise variance) lies on the
   Nishimori line, where Bayes-optimal inference shows NO replica-symmetry breaking [LIT: Nishimori, comment on
   Tanaka, arXiv:cond-mat/0111003; Zdeborova & Krzakala, arXiv:1511.02476, section "no RSB in Bayes-optimal
   inference"]. Telecom's hard regimes are of a different kind:
   (a) a first-order (spinodal) metastability window at intermediate SNR in overloaded systems [LIT: Arai, Ohzeki,
       Tanaka, arXiv:2004.11066];
   (b) T -> 0 maximum-likelihood search at high SNR (an optimization task, not a sampling task).
   Whether the quantum proposal's gap advantage survives on planted, Nishimori-line instances is untested anywhere.
   It is cheap to test with the team's existing code. This is the single most useful thing B4 can recommend (B4-04).
3. The offline survivors fall into three cases where energy evaluation is expensive, the landscape is multimodal and
   no deadline applies:
   (a) error-floor rare-event estimation, where the "energy" is a full BP decoder run (B4-07);
   (b) low-autocorrelation binary sequence (LABS) design for radar and sonar, a famously glassy offline problem with
       a quantum precedent (B4-15);
   (c) overloaded NOMA/CDMA detection in the first-order window, studied as science, not as a product (B4-03).
   None of them is BUILD-NOW on hardware. The best case for all three is SIMULATE MORE, ending as QUANTUM-INSPIRED
   ONLY for any group size that can be simulated classically.

======================================================================================================
1. KEY PHYSICS: SNR -> TEMPERATURE, AND WHERE THE HARD REGIME IS
======================================================================================================
1.1 Mapping [OWN, standard]. Real-valued model y = Hx + w, w ~ N(0, sigma^2 I), x in a discrete alphabet.
    Posterior p(x|y) ~ exp(-||y - Hx||^2 / (2 sigma^2)). With E(x) = ||y - Hx||^2 = x^T G x - 2 z^T x + const,
    where G = H^T H and z = H^T y, the posterior is a Boltzmann distribution at T_eff = 2 sigma^2.
    Ising form with BPSK spins: J_ij = -2 G_ij (i<j), h_i = 2 z_i.
    Low SNR => large sigma^2 => HIGH T. High SNR => LOW T.
    The couplings come from the channel Gram matrix: dense, all-to-all, and changing once per channel coherence
    block. The fields come from y: they change with EVERY received vector, i.e. per subcarrier per OFDM symbol.
1.2 QAM -> spins [OWN, standard; used in QuAMax, LIT arXiv:2001.04014].
    One complex M-QAM symbol is 2 real PAM dimensions, giving log2(M) spins per user:
      QPSK 2, 16-QAM 4, 64-QAM 6, 256-QAM 8.
    16-QAM per real dimension: value = 2 s1 + s2. Within-symbol coupling weights scale 4:2:1, and 64-QAM reaches
    16:1. The coefficient dynamic range grows with M.
    For annealers this hurts directly: the precision error (ICE) moves the ground state.
    For QeMCMC it hurts only proposal quality, never correctness. Any real symmetric H_prob gives an exact
    Boltzmann chain [P1]. This is the one structural edge QeMCMC has over QuAMax-style annealing. It is real, but it
    buys correctness, not speed.
1.3 Where the hard regime is.
    LOW SNR (HIGH T): the posterior is broad and single-spin Gibbs mixes fast. MMSE is near-optimal for typical
      massive MIMO (N_r >> N_t). BRIEF's HIGH regime: no quantum case unless energy is expensive. Telecom energies
      are cheap: an incremental Delta-E costs O(d N) [OWN].
    MATCHED-T posterior, N_t <= N_r, iid channel: on the Nishimori line, no RSB [LIT above]. In the large-system
      limit AMP/BP is Bayes-optimal when the replica fixed point is unique [LIT: Montanari & Tse, arXiv:cs/0602028,
      proves Tanaka's formula for loading alpha <= alpha_s ~ 1.49 in a sparse-signature model]. That alpha_s marks
      the onset of multiple fixed points (a first-order transition) [LIT-recalled]. Conclusion: for ordinary MIMO
      loading at the matched temperature, mixing is NOT the bottleneck.
    MATCHED-T, overloaded (alpha above ~1.5: NOMA, overloaded CDMA, user-dense uplink), intermediate SNR: a
      first-order transition with metastable states. MCMC and BP get stuck in the wrong (low-overlap) state.
      Quantum annealing also suffers here: reverse annealing could not avoid the first-order transition in their
      practical test [LIT: arXiv:2004.11066]. This is the real "hard phase" of telecom inference. It is
      metastability between two macrostates, not an SK-like glass. P1/P2 evidence does not cover it.
    LOW T below matched (ML detection, T -> 0, square or overloaded, large N, high-order QAM, intermediate-to-high
      SNR): an off-Nishimori, rugged landscape. Sphere-decoding expected complexity is exponential in N at any
      fixed SNR [LIT: Jalden & Ottersten, IEEE TSP 53(4) 2005, abstract checked], against the polynomial claim of
      Hassibi & Vikalo [LIT]. Hassibi et al. found that running the MCMC detector at T = noise std makes mixing time
      unbounded as SNR grows when local minima exist. Their recommendation is T scaling like sqrt(SNR) for mixing,
      while an optimality bound caps T at O(sqrt(SNR)/ln N) for SNR > 2 ln N [LIT: arXiv:1310.7305,
      IEEE TSP 62(17) 2014]. This is the regime where the P1-type low-T advantage could apply. But it is an
      optimization regime, and at high SNR an MMSE warm start already lands in the right basin for most
      realizations. The hard part is the tail of ill-conditioned channel draws. [OWN/SPEC: the fraction of hard
      realizations is not quantified here.]
    WHAT IT MEANS: the pilot's 25x-39x gap ratios at beta >= 1 [PILOT] are for the wrong ensemble. Telecom needs a
      planted test (B4-04) before any telecom idea gets compute.

======================================================================================================
2. LATENCY AND THROUGHPUT ARITHMETIC (why real-time dies)
======================================================================================================
2.1 Deadlines. NR numerology mu: 14 OFDM symbols per slot, 2^mu slots per 1 ms subframe, so the slot is 1, 0.5, 0.25
    or 0.125 ms for 15/30/60/120 kHz [LIT: 3GPP TS 38.211 numerology; slot/symbol structure confirmed via patent
    text in search, exact N1/N2 processing tables in TS 38.214 not fetched]. HARQ processing budgets are fractions
    of a slot to a few slots [LIT-recalled: LTE ~3 ms eNB processing in the 8 ms HARQ loop].
2.2 Problem rate [OWN]. 100 MHz carrier at 30 kHz: 273 PRB x 12 = 3,276 subcarriers x 14 symbols = 45,864
    detection problems per 0.5 ms slot, about 9e7 problems/s per carrier, each with a different field vector h.
    Per-problem time budget on one device: ~10 ns.
2.3 Quantum step times (from A4). D-Wave QMI: ~16 ms QPU access (15.9 ms programming) plus 10-20 ms unbilled
    overhead. IBM Heron: ~2 s per sub-job, 2-4 s in-session 1-shot round trip (A4 estimate). Helios: 55 ms per
    depth-1 layer. QuAMax's headline "10 us" counts anneal time only [LIT: arXiv:2001.04014]. Overheads were in the
    hundreds of ms per the team's own D-Wave talk (search snippet, not re-verified). X-ResQ (reverse-annealing MIMO)
    reports anneal-level BER for 4x6 16-QAM on 240 qubits, not end-to-end latency [LIT: arXiv:2402.18778].
2.4 Hypothetical best case [OWN/SPEC]. Run the whole chain on the controller: measure s', compute Delta-E on an FPGA,
    accept with an on-board RNG, conditionally re-prepare s by feedforward. IBM FF is ~600 ns (A4). Helios has
    real-time arithmetic (A4). A step then costs at least the circuit time. For n = 32 dense spins one Trotter layer
    is ~1.5k CNOT, depth ~100 two-qubit layers [OWN, 2.5], ~10-30 us per step on superconducting. 100 steps take
    1-3 ms for ONE problem, against a ~10 ns per-problem budget. Still ~1e5x short even with perfect fidelity, and
    the fidelity is not there (2.5).
2.5 Embedding cost [OWN]. Dense n-spin ZZ evolution on a line or heavy-hex via a swap network: about 3 CNOT per pair
    per Trotter layer, i.e. ~1.5 n^2 CNOT and ~3n two-qubit depth per layer:
       n=16 (8x8 QPSK):          ~360 CNOT/layer
       n=32 (16x16 QPSK):        ~1.5k
       n=64 (16x16 16-QAM):      ~6k
       n=256 (64x64 16-QAM):     ~98k
    t ~ U[2,20] with alpha-normalized H (||alpha H_prob|| ~ sqrt(n)) needs on the order of 10-100 Trotter layers
    [SPEC range; not measured]. A4 puts the Heron budget at ~2-5k two-qubit gates with mitigation. Ideal-shaped
    proposals are therefore impossible beyond n ~ 16-24 spins on present hardware. Coarse-grained groups of 8-16
    spins fit, but P2 notes small groups are classically simulable, so that is quantum-inspired [P2].
2.6 Energy per step [OWN]. Cache the residual r = y - Hx. A move flipping d spins costs O(d N) to update, a full
    evaluation O(N^2). For N = 32-256: ~1e2-1e5 flops, i.e. ~10 ns-10 us on a CPU core, faster batched on GPU.
    Cheap, so S* = t_Q/t_C ~ 1e6-1e9 as in BRIEF. Exception: when the "energy" is a decoder run (B4-07).
2.7 Verdict rule applied below: any idea that needs its answer inside a frame, slot, or channel-coherence block is
    KILLED. No foreseeable device closes 6-9 orders of magnitude in throughput per watt against ASIC detectors
    [SPEC, but the gap is big enough that the conclusion does not depend on the exact numbers].

3. IDEAS

B4-01  Real-time hard-output (near-ML) MIMO detection with a QeMCMC proposal
 Payoff: higher spectral efficiency than MMSE in square/overloaded uplinks (ParaMax-type gains, ~1.4x throughput
   vs a linear detector in 12x24 16-QAM [LIT: arXiv:2103.10561]).
 Regime: LOW T (T -> 0 or Hassibi-tempered T ~ sqrt(SNR) scale), mid-high SNR. Why: ML is the ground state; the chain
   is used as stochastic search.
 Mapping: BPSK/QAM -> N_t log2 M spins; J from the dense Gram matrix (all-to-all); h from H^T y per received vector.
   Embedding ~1.5 n^2 CNOT per Trotter layer [OWN 2.5].
 Energy/step: O(dN) incremental, 10 ns-1 us.
 Spins: 32-512 full; group 8-16.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Partly, only for the ill-conditioned tail. An MMSE warm start puts most chains in the right
   basin at high SNR [OWN/SPEC].
 Best classical: MMSE-SIC, K-best/sphere decoding for small N, ParaMax parallel tempering [LIT arXiv:2103.10561],
   RI-MIMO/TRIM Ising-machine detectors [LIT arXiv:2105.10535], deep-unfolded detectors, all on ASIC/GPU.
 Sketch: none worth building. If forced: on-controller loop (2.4).
 Detailed balance: holds in the ideal case [P1]. Irrelevant here, since this is search.
 Break-even: ~9e7 problems/s needed vs >= 16 ms per quantum call. Missed by >1e6 even at zero chain length.
 Kill risk: certain (deadline + throughput).
 VERDICT: KILL. Likelihood of any value: <1%.

B4-02  Real-time soft-output MIMO detection (LLRs from posterior samples at T = 2 sigma^2)
 Payoff: soft LLRs into the LDPC decoder are worth ~1-3 dB in coded systems [LIT-recalled: list sphere decoding
   literature]. This is the one telecom task that is genuinely SAMPLING.
 Regime: matched T (Nishimori line). LOW SNR = HIGH T (easy). Intermediate SNR + overloaded = first-order window.
 Mapping/embedding/spins: as B4-01.
 Energy/step: O(dN), ~10 ns-1 us.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Usually no for N_t <= N_r (no RSB; AMP/EP near-optimal for iid channels). Only in the
   overloaded first-order window.
 Best classical: list sphere decoding, soft MMSE-PIC, EP detectors, MCMC with optimized temperature
   [LIT arXiv:1310.7305], ParaMax soft variant [LIT arXiv:2103.10561].
 Detailed balance: required here, since the samples must be the posterior. Holds ideally [P1]. Hardware noise biases
   LLRs unless twirled [P1 SI].
 Break-even: same throughput wall as B4-01.
 Kill risk: certain.
 VERDICT: KILL (real-time). The offline variant is B4-05.

B4-03  Overloaded NOMA/CDMA multiuser detection in the first-order hard window (offline science)
 Payoff: a scientific answer to "does a quantum proposal escape a first-order metastable state that traps Gibbs and
   BP?". If yes, it is the first QeMCMC result on a planted inference problem with a known phase diagram. Product value
   is nil (offline).
 Regime: matched T at intermediate SNR, loading alpha ~1.5-3 (NOMA overloading). Why: coexistence of a high-overlap
   and a low-overlap macrostate. Barriers are extensive, so local moves need exponential time [LIT: 2004.11066 for
   QA; LIT-recalled for the classical spinodal].
 Mapping: BPSK users, J = -2 G with G = S^T S from random spreading S (dense), h = 2 S^T y.
 Energy/step: O(dN).
 Spins: 8-16 per instance for exact gaps (n <= 12-14 statevector with common/qmc.py); full chains to ~20 in sim.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Yes, by construction inside the window.
 Best classical: BP/AMP with damping and restarts, parallel tempering across SNR, population annealing. Also known
   trick: start from a decoded/oracle partial solution, i.e. reverse-annealing analogue [LIT 2004.11066].
 Sketch: generate planted CDMA instances; sweep alpha and SNR; compute exact spectral gaps of quantum_Q vs local_Q,
   uniform_Q and PT (common/qmc.py: quantum_Q, gap, parallel_tempering). Measure the time for a chain started at
   the paramagnetic state to hit overlap > 0.9.
 Caveat: finite n <= 14 blurs any first-order transition. The window is visible only as a bimodal overlap
   histogram at n ~ 12-20 [SPEC].
 Detailed balance: exact ideal [P1]; checked numerically by qmc.asymmetry.
 Break-even: n/a (science). Any hardware run is n <= 10 and offline.
 Kill risk: finite-size smearing makes "first-order" untestable at n <= 14. PT across SNR closes the gap anyway.
 VERDICT: SIMULATE MORE. Likelihood of a clean positive result ~15%.

B4-04  Nishimori-line transfer test: does the QeMCMC gap advantage survive on planted, matched-temperature posteriors?
 Payoff: decides the whole telecom/inference vertical in a few days of CPU. If the gap-ratio collapses to ~1x on the
   Nishimori line (as no-RSB suggests for typical loading), every telecom sampling idea dies with a clear reason.
   If it survives, B4-03/05/07/11 earn compute. Also a citable negative or positive result: nobody has published
   QeMCMC on planted posteriors [SPEC: no such paper found in this session's searches].
 Regime: matched T across SNR (= across T), plus off-matched low T (ML-like) as a control. Why: separates "glass"
   advantage (P1 regime) from "inference" hardness.
 Mapping: three planted ensembles at n = 6-14 (exact):
   (i) MIMO BPSK with Gaussian H at loading 0.5/1/1.5/2;
   (ii) Sourlas/LDPC-like p-spin codes, e.g. 3-body parity terms; any diagonal H_prob keeps U real symmetric, so
        symmetry holds;
   (iii) the SK control already in the pilot.
 Energy/step: trivial.
 Spins: 6-14.
 Mixing the bottleneck? That is exactly the measured quantity.
 Best classical: local, uniform, PT, and for MIMO the Hassibi-tempered chain (T scaled with sqrt(SNR)).
 Sketch: reuse pilot/pilot_sk_gap_vs_beta.py. Replace sk_instance with a planted generator: x0 random, y = Hx0 + w,
   J/h as 1.1; beta_matched = 1/(2 sigma^2). Report the median gap ratio quantum/best-classical vs (SNR, loading)
   and vs (beta/beta_matched). Same 3+ instances per point as the pilot, ideally 20.
 Detailed balance: ideal-exact [P1]. The planted generator does not change the proof (diagonal H_prob).
 Break-even: n/a. Simulation only.
 Kill risk: small-n gaps at n <= 14 may not show the scaling exponent. Fit k as in P1 Fig 2c and say so.
 VERDICT: BUILD NOW (simulation only). Likelihood it yields a decisive answer ~70%. Likelihood the answer is "advantage
   survives on matched posteriors" ~20% [SPEC].

B4-05  Offline gold-standard posterior labels for training and benchmarking deep-learning MIMO detectors
 Payoff: exact LLR labels on hard channel draws (correlated, ill-conditioned, overloaded) for training and stress
   tests of learned detectors.
 Regime: matched T, intermediate SNR, hard channel tail.
 Mapping: as B4-01.
 Energy/step: cheap.
 Spins: 16-128.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Only in the overloaded window. For N <= ~24 exact enumeration of 2^N states is cheap on a
   GPU [OWN: 2^24 ~ 1.7e7 states x N ops], so labels need no sampling at all.
 Best classical: exact enumeration (N <= ~28), list sphere decoding, PT with many replicas on GPU.
 Detailed balance: required; ideal-exact; hardware bias needs twirling [P1 SI].
 Break-even: offline, but S* ~1e6-1e9 against GPU PT and the labels are cheap anyway.
 Kill risk: classical PT and enumeration already produce these labels.
 VERDICT: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (use a coarse-grained group proposal inside GPU PT if B4-04 is positive).
   Likelihood of quantum value ~3%.

B4-06  LDPC / Sourlas MAP decoding by Gibbs sampling at the Nishimori temperature
 Payoff: bitwise-MAP decoding between the BP threshold and the MAP threshold.
 Regime: matched T (channel noise). The hard window is the first-order region between the BP and MAP thresholds.
 Mapping: code bits as spins, checks as d_c-body Z terms (d_c = 6-20 typical), or penalty+ancilla to 2-body (adds
   qubits). Kasi & Jamieson embed up to 420-bit blocks on a 2,048-qubit annealer [LIT: arXiv:2007.11069], but not
   protocol-size codes.
 Energy/step: O(d * d_v) incremental, ~10 ns.
 Spins: 1e3-1e4 bits per codeword in 5G NR.
 Mixing the bottleneck? In the window, yes. But the window is a narrow SNR band of ~0.1-0.5 dB for good codes
   [LIT-recalled], and codes are designed so BP works at the operating point.
 Best classical: BP / min-sum on ASIC at Gbit/s; spatial coupling closes the BP-MAP gap classically
   [LIT-recalled: threshold saturation, Kudekar-Richardson-Urbanke].
 Detailed balance: ideal-exact for multi-body diagonal H_prob [P1 argument].
 Break-even: real-time throughput wall (2.2). Spatial coupling already harvests the MAP gain.
 Kill risk: certain.
 VERDICT: KILL. The science content is folded into B4-04 ensemble (ii).

B4-07  Error-floor rare-event estimation: quantum proposals from a surrogate Ising, exact accept with the true decoder
 Payoff: frame error rates of 1e-12 to 1e-19 in the error floor for optical/storage/5G-URLLC codes, where plain Monte
   Carlo is hopeless. Industrial value is real: code qualification for 400G/800G optics, flash, URLLC.
 Regime: multicanonical / tempered sampling over BSC-quantized error patterns e. Target
   pi(e) ~ p^w(e) (1-p)^(n-w(e)) x 1[decoder fails on e], or a flat histogram over weight w restricted to failures.
   The failure set is a union of disconnected islands (trapping sets / near-codewords): genuinely multimodal, a
   LOW-T-like problem where local flips rarely bridge islands [SPEC, consistent with the trapping-set literature].
 Mapping: e in {0,1}^n as spins. The proposal Hamiltonian H_prob is a SURROGATE: e.g. a 2-body Ising built from the
   parity-check graph that rewards patterns concentrated on small check-sharing clusters. P1's key property: the
   proposal is symmetric and exact for ANY real symmetric H_prob [P1], so a crude surrogate costs only efficiency,
   not correctness. The accept step evaluates the true target, i.e. runs the real BP decoder.
 Energy/step: one decoder run, 1-100 us per call on a CPU for n ~ 1e3 [OWN estimate: ~3e3-6e3 edges x 20-50
   iterations]. This is the rare telecom case where t_energy is not negligible.
 Spins: n = 1e2-1e4 full (infeasible). Coarse-grained group of 10-30 bits around the current failing pattern's
   support (P2-style), the rest frozen.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Yes: island-to-island moves.
 Best classical: DAIS / multicanonical MC, reaching BER ~1e-19 on a (96,50) LDPC code [LIT: Holzlohner et al., IEEE
   Comm. Lett. 9(2) 2005, via search]; trapping-set enumeration + importance sampling [LIT: arXiv:cs/0605051];
   Richardson's error-floor method [LIT-recalled]; GPU brute-force decoding at 1e9-1e10 frames/day.
 Sketch: (1) small QC-LDPC code n ~ 64-128, BSC. (2) Classically simulate the group proposal for groups of 10-12
   bits. (3) Compare integrated autocorrelation of the failure indicator and the island-switching rate vs local
   flip + swap-in/out moves and vs DAIS. (4) Only if the advantage is >~10x per step, consider a hardware run with
   cached shots per group configuration (P1 ran offline from cached shots).
 Detailed balance: holds if the group is chosen at random independent of the state and the group proposal is the
   symmetric P1/P2 single-group move [P2]. Choosing the group FROM the current support makes the proposal
   state-dependent, so a Hastings correction is needed (qmc.mh_matrix(hastings=True) supports it).
 Break-even [OWN]: t_C ~ 1-100 us (decoder dominated), t_Q ~ 0.1-4 s per step on cloud => S* ~ 1e3-4e6. Better than
   the 1e6-1e9 of cheap-energy problems, still far from any measured step advantage at n <= 30. Offline: no deadline.
 Kill risk: a group proposal of 10-12 bits is classically simulable [P2], and domain-informed classical moves
   (swap one trapping set for another) are already near-optimal.
 VERDICT: SIMULATE MORE (quantum-inspired). Likelihood of a quantum-hardware case ~5%; of a useful quantum-inspired
   move ~15%.

B4-08  Minimum-distance and trapping-set search for code design
 Payoff: certify d_min / dominant trapping sets of candidate codes (NP-hard in general).
 Regime: LOW T optimization over nonzero codewords. Energy = Hamming weight. Glassy for random-like codes [SPEC].
 Mapping: information bits as spins. Codeword weight is a sum of parities of generator columns, i.e. multi-body Z
   terms of weight up to the generator column weight (dense for LDPC generators). Alternative: code bits as spins
   with check penalties (d_c-body). High-order terms make the circuits ~O(n * d_c) multi-controlled phases per
   layer [OWN].
 Energy/step: O(n) or O(d_c d_v) incremental, ~10 ns.
 Spins: 1e2-1e4.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Yes, but the task is search, not equilibrium.
 Best classical: Brouwer-Zimmermann, Stern / information-set decoding, impulse methods [LIT-recalled].
 Detailed balance: ideal-exact (diagonal multi-body H_prob) [P1 argument]. Irrelevant for search.
 Break-even: S* ~1e6-1e9 (cheap energy). Hopeless at n of interest.
 Kill risk: ISD is very strong, and sizes are far beyond hardware.
 VERDICT: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. ~3%.

B4-09  List decoding (polar SCL / CA-SCL) replaced or assisted by sampling
 Payoff: lower FER at short block lengths (5G control channels).
 Regime: matched-T posterior over info bits; the list wants the top-L codewords, i.e. LOW T.
 Mapping: polar encoding gives high-order parity terms. Dense.
 Energy/step: O(N log N) re-encode, or O(N) incremental.
 Spins: 32-1024.
 Mixing the bottleneck? No. SCL is deterministic O(L N log N) and near-ML for L = 8-32 [LIT-recalled].
 Best classical: CA-SCL, SCL-flip, ordered statistics decoding.
 Detailed balance: ideal-exact; irrelevant.
 Break-even: deadline wall.
 Kill risk: certain.
 VERDICT: KILL. ~0%.

B4-10  Protograph lifting / circulant-shift assignment for QC-LDPC design (Potts)
 Payoff: codes with larger girth and fewer small trapping sets.
 Regime: LOW T (optimize), offline.
 Mapping: Potts variable per protograph edge with q = lifting size Z (up to 384 in 5G NR), i.e. ~9 qubits per
   variable in binary encoding, all 2^9 values valid when Z is a power of two, else invalid states are rejected.
   The cost (count of short cycles) is a high-order function of shift sums mod Z: not quadratic, so H_prob must be a
   surrogate.
 Energy/step: cycle counting, ~1-100 us.
 Spins: hundreds of edges x 9 qubits = thousands.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Unclear. Greedy PEG/ACE heuristics plus random restarts work well [LIT-recalled].
 Best classical: PEG, ACE, girth-aware greedy, SA.
 Detailed balance: binary-encoded Potts with rejection of invalid codes is still symmetric (pi = 0 outside the valid
   set) [OWN, from the P1 argument].
 Break-even: offline; S* ~1e3-1e5 with the costlier energy; problem too large.
 Kill risk: no evidence that mixing limits design quality.
 VERDICT: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. ~2%.

B4-11  Sparse support recovery with binary support (spike-and-slab) for sparse channel / delay-Doppler estimation
       and compressive sensing
 Payoff: sparse mmWave / OTFS channel estimation and radar grid detection with exact support posteriors.
 Regime: matched T on the support posterior after integrating out the Gaussian slab. Hard phase = the first-order
   region of AMP for spike-and-slab at low sparsity and intermediate measurement rate [LIT-recalled: Krzakala et
   al., seeded/spatially coupled CS]. Low SNR = HIGH T, easy.
 Mapping: support bits as spins. The marginal likelihood after integrating the slab is NOT quadratic in the support
   (log-det of a support-dependent matrix), so H_prob must be a quadratic surrogate (e.g. from the Gram matrix of
   the dictionary). Accept with the exact marginal (rank-1 updates, O(K^2) per flip).
 Energy/step: O(K^2) with K = support size; ~100 ns-10 us.
 Spins: 64-4096 grid points.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Only in the hard phase. AMP/VAMP otherwise.
 Best classical: OMP, VAMP/EM-BG-AMP, Gibbs with block moves, spatially coupled measurements.
 Detailed balance: exact (symmetric proposal, exact accept) [P1].
 Break-even: real-time variants hit the deadline wall. Offline radar/sonar post-processing tolerates ~seconds, but
   S* is still ~1e5-1e8.
 Kill risk: classical AMP is near-optimal outside a narrow window; deadlines.
 VERDICT: SIMULATE MORE only as a 4th ensemble in B4-04; otherwise QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. ~5%.

B4-12  Joint channel estimation and data detection with discrete priors (semi-blind, pilot contamination)
 Payoff: fewer pilots, better throughput.
 Regime: bilinear posterior: continuous channel times discrete data, matched T.
 Mapping: only the data part is Ising (conditional on H). Gibbs alternates continuous/discrete blocks. The quantum
   proposal applies to the discrete block only.
 Energy/step: O(N^2) per block update.
 Spins: 1e2-1e4 per frame.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Phase/permutation ambiguities are real multimodality, but they are resolved by a few pilots,
   not by better moves.
 Best classical: EM / turbo channel estimation, BiG-AMP.
 Detailed balance: holds blockwise (Metropolis-within-Gibbs with a symmetric discrete proposal) [OWN from P1].
 Break-even: deadline wall.
 Kill risk: certain.
 VERDICT: KILL. ~0%.

B4-13  1-bit / low-resolution ADC massive MIMO detection
 Payoff: detection with cheap, low-power ADCs.
 Regime: posterior with probit likelihood; matched T. No simple SNR->T map, because quantization noise dominates
   at high SNR.
 Mapping: the true energy is non-quadratic (sum of log Phi terms). A quadratic surrogate H_prob (Bussgang
   linearization) for proposals, exact accept.
 Energy/step: O(N_r) per flip with cached products.
 Spins: 16-256.
 Mixing the bottleneck? No evidence. Bussgang-LMMSE and GAMP work well [LIT-recalled].
 Best classical: GAMP, Bussgang LMMSE, learned detectors.
 Detailed balance: exact with exact accept [P1].
 Break-even: deadline wall.
 VERDICT: KILL (real-time). ~0%.

B4-14  Multi-target tracking data association (radar/sonar MCMCDA)
 Payoff: better association in dense clutter.
 Regime: posterior over association hypotheses, mostly HIGH-T-like for single scan.
 Mapping: one-hot assignment variables with row/column constraints, i.e. a constrained Potts / permutation. A
   quantum proposal from an unconstrained H plus measurement mostly lands outside the feasible set and is rejected.
   The penalty-term version used in the adiabatic MOT formulation [LIT: Zaech et al., CVPR 2022, arXiv:2202.08837]
   adds dense couplings.
 Energy/step: O(1)-O(K) per move.
 Spins: targets x measurements, 1e2-1e4.
 Mixing the bottleneck? NO for single scan: MCMCDA is a fully polynomial randomized approximation scheme for JPDA
   [LIT: Oh, Russell, Sastry, IEEE TAC 54(3) 2009]. Multi-scan track split/merge moves are where it gets hard, and
   those moves are already designed classically.
 Best classical: MCMCDA, JPDA/MHT with Murty k-best, GNN, PMBM filters.
 Detailed balance: symmetric if infeasible proposals are rejected [OWN from P1], but the acceptance rate would be tiny.
 Break-even: tracking update deadlines of ~10-100 ms, still far below a seconds-scale cloud step. S* ~1e6+.
 Kill risk: mixing is provably not the bottleneck in single scan; the constraint structure wastes proposals.
 VERDICT: KILL. ~1%.

B4-15  LABS: low-autocorrelation binary sequences for radar/sonar pulse compression and sync words (offline design)
 Payoff: better peak-sidelobe / merit-factor sequences for radar, sonar and sync codes. One-off offline design, so no
   deadline. An established hard benchmark with a quantum precedent: QAOA (plus quantum minimum finding) gave the
   best empirical scaling of any algorithm for LABS [LIT: Shaydulin et al., Sci. Adv. 10, eadm6761 (2024),
   arXiv:2308.02342, abstract checked; their classical comparison in the abstract is branch-and-bound]. The memetic
   tabu search baseline and its ~1.34^N scaling are LIT-recalled (Boskovic et al. 2017), not re-verified.
 Regime: LOW T. A notoriously glassy "golf-course" landscape [LIT-recalled]. This is the P1 regime.
 Mapping: E = sum_k C_k^2 with C_k = sum_i s_i s_{i+k}: 4-body and 2-body terms, ~N^3/12 four-body terms [OWN order
   of magnitude]. Each 4-body ZZZZ rotation is ~6 CNOT plus routing, so ~1e4-1e5 CNOT per Trotter layer at
   N = 30-40 [OWN]. Far beyond hardware. Option: a 2-body surrogate H_prob (keep only the 2-body part or a
   mean-field linearization) for the proposal; correctness is unaffected [P1].
 Energy/step: O(N) incremental per flip with tables (as in MTS), ~10-100 ns.
 Spins: N = 20-30 for sim studies; applications want N = 64-1000+.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Yes (exponential time-to-solution for all known methods).
 Best classical: memetic tabu search on GPU, PT, self-avoiding walks; branch-and-bound for proofs.
 Sketch: exact gap of the QeMCMC proposal at N <= 14 (2^14 states, qmc.quantum_Q with the true 4-body diagonal, which
   works since H_prob is diagonal) vs local, uniform, PT. Fit gap exponents like P1 Fig 2c. Then a time-to-ground-state
   comparison at N <= 24 using the sampled proposal inside PT. Report steps AND wall-clock.
 Detailed balance: ideal-exact for any diagonal H_prob [P1]. Optimization, so it matters only for
   tempering-correctness claims.
 Break-even: cheap energy, so S* ~1e6-1e9 on cloud hardware. Even offline, a step advantage must exceed that, or the
   device must run the chain on the controller (2.4). Realistic outcome: a QUANTUM-INSPIRED proposal kernel.
 Kill risk: MTS is excellent; the 4-body circuit cost is prohibitive; gap exponents at N <= 14 may not predict
   time-to-solution at N ~ 60+.
 VERDICT: SIMULATE MORE. Likelihood of a publishable gap-exponent result ~25%; of a hardware wall-clock win <1%.

B4-16  User scheduling, PRB allocation and beam selection (Potts / QUBO)
 Payoff: higher sum-rate / proportional-fair utility.
 Regime: optimization at LOW T, solved every TTI (0.125-1 ms).
 Mapping: one-hot users x PRBs/beams with interference couplings, i.e. constrained Potts. Proposals mostly infeasible
   (B4-14 issue).
 Energy/step: O(K) per move.
 Spins: 1e2-1e4.
 Mixing the bottleneck? No. Greedy proportional-fair and Hungarian/auction are near-optimal in practice
   [LIT-recalled].
 Best classical: greedy PF, Hungarian, WMMSE, learned schedulers.
 Detailed balance: irrelevant (search).
 Break-even: TTI deadline wall.
 VERDICT: KILL. ~0%.

B4-17  Interference-graph colouring: frequency assignment, PCI/PRACH planning (Potts, offline network planning)
 Payoff: fewer conflicts in network-planning tools, run hours or days offline.
 Regime: LOW T optimization; glassy near the colourability threshold [LIT-recalled: random colouring phase diagram];
   real interference graphs are geometric and usually far from the threshold [SPEC].
 Mapping: q-state Potts (q = 3-504 for PCI), log2 q qubits per cell, invalid codes rejected. Couplings sparse
   (neighbouring cells), so better embedding than MIMO.
 Energy/step: O(degree), ~10 ns.
 Spins: 1e3-1e5 cells x log2 q.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Rarely for real networks.
 Best classical: tabu, SA, DSATUR, ILP with commercial solvers.
 Detailed balance: symmetric with rejection of invalid encodings [OWN from P1].
 Break-even: offline, but S* ~1e6-1e9 and problem sizes are far beyond hardware.
 VERDICT: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. ~2%.

B4-18  Vector perturbation precoding (downlink integer lattice search)
 Payoff: near-capacity downlink with full diversity.
 Regime: LOW T (closest-lattice-point search), per channel realization and per symbol vector.
 Mapping: integer perturbation -> several spins per dimension; dense Gram couplings. A QA version exists (QAVP)
   [LIT: snippet from the Jamieson group's NSF record, attribution not fully verified].
 Energy/step: O(dN).
 Spins: 32-256.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Search problem. Sphere encoders and lattice reduction are competitive.
 Best classical: sphere encoding with LLL, THP, regularized ZF.
 Break-even: deadline wall.
 VERDICT: KILL. ~0%.

B4-19  Physical-layer security and secret-key reconciliation (Cascade / LDPC information reconciliation, CV-QKD)
 Payoff: higher reconciliation efficiency, longer key.
 Regime: decoding near capacity at low SNR (CV-QKD at SNR << 1), i.e. HIGH T for the posterior.
 Mapping: LDPC/polar decoding as B4-06.
 Energy/step: cheap.
 Spins: 1e4-1e6 bit blocks.
 Mixing the bottleneck? No. It is a deterministic syndrome-decoding task where BP on long multi-edge codes is
   near-optimal [LIT-recalled]. The task needs ONE correct decoded string, not samples. Detailed balance is irrelevant.
 Best classical: multi-edge-type LDPC + BP on GPU, Cascade for DV-QKD.
 Break-even: key-rate throughput wall.
 Kill risk: certain. Also the trusted-hardware question: a cloud QPU in the key-distillation loop leaks
   side-information [SPEC].
 VERDICT: KILL. ~0%.

B4-20  Many-SNR (many-temperature) ensembles for BER-curve generation with one compiled proposal circuit
 Payoff: amortize compilation across a whole SNR sweep. The QeMCMC proposal does not contain T (H_prob is not divided
   by T [P1/BRIEF]), so one circuit family serves every SNR point.
 Regime: all T; the useful part is the MEDIUM/LOW-T end of the sweep.
 Mapping: compile once per channel matrix H (couplings fixed), bind fields h = 2 H^T y as parameters per received
   vector (parametric Rz angles; Braket/IBM parametric compilation, A4).
 Energy/step: cheap.
 Spins: as B4-01.
 Mixing the bottleneck? Only at the hard end. Also, PT across SNR points is the natural classical answer, and it is
   free classically.
 Best classical: PT/replica exchange across SNR on GPU, multicanonical sweeps.
 Detailed balance: per-T chains exact ideal [P1]. Swap moves between SNR points standard.
 Break-even: removes t_compile from t_Q but leaves latency and shot time: S* still ~1e5-1e8 [OWN from A4 numbers].
 Kill risk: the amortization fixes the smallest term in t_Q.
 VERDICT: SIMULATE MORE only as an engineering note for any hardware run of B4-03/04/07. On its own: KILL. ~2%.

Strongest three:
 1. B4-04 Nishimori-line transfer test (BUILD NOW, simulation). Cheap (reuses pilot + common/qmc.py) and decisive for
    the entire inference/telecom vertical. It tests the physics question nobody has answered for QeMCMC: does the
    low-T glass advantage [P1, PILOT] carry over to planted matched-temperature posteriors, where theory says there is
    no RSB? Either answer is a clean result.
 2. B4-07 Error-floor rare-event sampling (SIMULATE MORE). The only telecom task combining:
      - genuine multimodality (trapping-set islands);
      - an expensive energy (a BP decoder call), which pulls S* down to ~1e3-1e6;
      - no deadline;
      - a direct use of P1's "any H_prob is exact" property: a crude surrogate Ising drives proposals while the true
        decoder decides acceptance.
    The likely end state is a quantum-inspired move. It is still a concrete, sellable offline use (code
    qualification).
 3. B4-15 LABS radar/sonar sequence design (SIMULATE MORE). Offline, LOW-T glassy (exactly P1's regime), a famous
    classical baseline, and a recent quantum-scaling precedent. Gap exponents at N <= 14 are computable this week. The
    4-body circuit cost kills hardware, so the honest deliverable is a proposal-kernel exponent study.
