METHOD / PROVENANCE
- Semantic Scholar citations API, pulled 2026-11-20: 111 citing records for P1 and 14 for P2.
  S2 coverage of P1 is clearly incomplete (it misses 2611.05883, 2506.01335 and 2604.20701), so I
  also ran arXiv API keyword searches and web searches.
- Every arXiv ID below was checked against arXiv: the export API (title, authors, date, abstract,
  journal_ref) for all of them, plus the full HTML text for the key papers, which I grepped for
  temperature, size and hardware facts.
- Rows marked "[S2-only]" or "[snippet-only]" take a detail (usually the journal DOI) from Semantic
  Scholar or a search snippet that I did not open on the journal site.
- Temperature convention: P1 and most follow-ups use the SK model with UNNORMALISED couplings,
  J_jk and h_j ~ N(0,1) (confirmed in the text of P1 and of Arai 2502.08060). The glass transition
  therefore sits near T_c ~ sqrt(n), which is about 3 for n=10. "T=1" in these papers is LOW T for
  n~10, and "T=10" is about 3*T_c, i.e. genuinely high T. Keep this in mind when reading "medium/high T".

ID CHECKS AGAINST EARLIER NOTES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
- 2408.07881 Orfi & Sels: CONFIRMED. The arXiv title is "Quantum enhanced Markov chains require
  fine-tuned quenches". The journal title differs: per S2 it appeared as "Barriers to efficient mixing
  of quantum-enhanced Markov chains", PRA 110, 052434 (2024) [S2-only DOI 10.1103/physreva.110.052434].
  A separate EARLIER Orfi & Sels paper exists, arXiv:2403.03087 "Bounding speedup of QeMCMC"; per S2
  it is PRA 110, 052414 (2024). Do not conflate the two.
- 2602.06171 Marshall et al.: CONFIRMED (IBM Quantum + Hartree Centre; v3 dated 17 Nov 2026).
  NUANCE: the problem is Maximum Independent Set with up to 117 variables on 117 qubits
  (ibm_boston / ibm_pittsburgh, plus ibm_aachen at 17 nodes). The method combines QeMCMC with
  warm-start QAOA and parallel tempering, all at T=0.1. Authors' own caveat: "the instances studied
  here are solved efficiently by CPLEX". The scaling claim is only "an indication of more favorable
  scaling" against the tested classical MCMC, on 5 instances.
- 2411.17821 Christmann et al.: CONFIRMED. Authors Christmann, Ivashkov, Chiurco, Mazzola;
  Phys. Rev. A 111, 042615 (2025).
- 2603.08395 Claudon et al.: CONFIRMED. Authors Claudon, Ramos-Calderer, Piquemal; run on Quantinuum
  H2 and Helios. NUANCE: this is NOT Layden-type QeMCMC. It is a Szegedy-style quantum-walk qMCMC
  with amplitude estimation, demonstrated on TWO-STATE Markov chains (toy), on physical qubits and
  with the Iceberg error-detection code.
- 2603.28076 Hsieh, Orfi, Sels "Adiabatic dressing of quantum enhanced Markov chains": CONFIRMED.
  S2 lists a journal DOI 10.1103/x255-fw2l [S2-only].
- 2606.23350 Cui et al. "Irreversibility Enhances Quantum-Enhanced MCMC": CONFIRMED (v2 18 Nov 2026,
  cond-mat.stat-mech).
- P1 hardware size: an earlier note said "IBM hardware to n=27". This is WRONG as a problem size.
  P1 ran n=10 instances (e.g. T=0.1) on ibmq_mumbai, which is a 27-QUBIT device. Exact-gap
  numerics covered n<=10.
- Not found despite targeted searches: "Nakano et al. QeMCMC for alloys"; any Szabo/Bauer QeMCMC
  paper; any Rydberg-hardware QeMCMC EXPERIMENT; any D-Wave HARDWARE QeMCMC run; any paper training
  RBMs/Boltzmann machines with Layden-type QeMCMC; any head-to-head paper showing the speedup
  vanishes against parallel tempering + cluster moves. The Nakano papers that do exist are
  QAOA-MC (2305.08789), a neural sampler trained on QAOA (2506.01335) and fair sampling (2512.14552);
  details below. "Arai et al." is Arai & Kadowaki 2502.08060 (quantum-annealing proposal,
  SIMULATION only).

2. THEORY OF ADVANTAGE (when does the gap enhancement exist?)
=========================================================================
- Orfi & Sels | 2024 | arXiv:2403.03087 (PRA 110, 052414 [S2-only]), https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03087 | no speedup on a worst-case unstructured sampling problem; upper bound on the Markov gap rules out speedup for ANY unital proposal there; in the low-T regime gap = O(2^-N) for any unitary proposal on that problem | low T; theory | general N | WEAKENS (no generic advantage; any advantage must come from problem structure)
- Orfi & Sels | 2024 | arXiv:2408.07881 (PRA 110, 052434 "Barriers to efficient mixing..." [S2-only]), https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07881 | long-time-limit gap is bounded by the IPR of classical states in the eigenstate basis, so NO advantage when quenching to an ERGODIC system; optimum needs delocalisation without too much entropy, which is a fine-tuned intermediate regime for SK and 3-spin | low T; sim + theory | small N (ED) | WEAKENS (advantage needs fine-tuned quench parameters; it disappears in the ergodic/long-t limit)
- Hsieh, Orfi, Sels | 2026 | arXiv:2603.28076, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28076 | replace the quench with a time-symmetric adiabatic ramp ("dressing"); suppresses high-energy proposals and improves gap scaling: SK k drops from 0.614 (quench) to 0.146 (peak) / 0.124 (kappa-averaged); 3-spin from 0.453 to 0.182 / 0.181 | low T (beta=5, h=1.5); sim (ED + bottleneck bound) | ED N=5-12; Ising-chain bound N=8-40 | STRENGTHENS (better scaling; same group as the bounds)
- Christmann, Ivashkov, Chiurco, Mazzola | 2024/2025 | arXiv:2411.17821 (PRA 111, 042615), https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17821 | finds an optimal mixing strength (gamma~0.45, just below critical) and fixed t=12; time-to-plateau of the gap is linear in n; large Trotter steps fine; reverse digitized annealing explored; MPS/TEBD proposals with chi=4 follow the ideal k~0.23 up to n=9 | T=1 main text (low T for these n); gap-vs-T at n=8 in App. D; sim | n<=~10 exact | WEAKENS the quantum case (a classical approximate emulation keeps the scaling advantage over local/uniform)
- Nakano, Hakoshima, Mitarai, Fujii | 2023 | arXiv:2305.08789 (PRR 6, 033105, 2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08789 | QAOA-type proposal satisfying the symmetric-proposal constraint; acceptance rate correlates with gap, so parameters can be tuned from a measurable; quadratic speedup in convergence | low T; sim | small n | STRENGTHENS (shallow circuits, tunable from data)
- Incudini & Mazzola | 2026 | arXiv:2607.22818, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22818 | puts the Layden Hamiltonian-simulation proposal inside a coherent Szegedy walk ("fully-quantum walk"); heuristic cubic gap gain over best classical proposal at low T; total ~6th-degree query speedup; full FT compilation; crossover vs CPU/GPU/FPGA cut from ~10^3 years to <1 day | low T; sim + FT resource estimate | dense Ising, extrapolated | STRENGTHENS the long-run case (FT route), WEAKENS the NISQ-QeMCMC case (they call standard QeMCMC a "classical sequential chain assisted by a quantum simulator")
- Shirai | 2026 | arXiv:2609.38943, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38943 | "quantum-echo" kernel (forward+backward QA/QAOA dynamics): Hamming-space delocalisation via operator spreading plus energy-space localisation; too much energy localisation hurts; greedy descent needed | optimisation; sim | small | NEUTRAL (mechanistic insight; agrees with Orfi/Sels tension)
- D'Arcangelo, Javanmard, Pearson (Pasqal) | 2025 | arXiv:2505.21255 (PRA 113, 052403, 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21255 | quantum Markov chain using an MBL Floquet Ising chain; thermal-to-MBL transition tunes the acceptance rate; aimed at neutral-atom/analog QPUs | not stated; sim | small | NEUTRAL (the localisation knob is the same lever as the IPR bound; neutral-atom-friendly)

3. TEMPERATURE-DEPENDENCE RESULTS (explicit)
=========================================================================
- Layden et al. 2203.12497: "substantially better delta on average than either classical alternative for T <~ 1". Fits at T=1 (k_q=0.264, k_local=0.94, k_uniform=0.948); "roughly cubic/quartic enhancement at low temperatures". No advantage claim at high T | sim n<=10.
- Ferguson & Wallden 2405.04247, App. B.3 Table 1 (k in delta ~ 2^-kn; header reads T = 0.01 / 1 / 10, while the main text says T=0.1 and T=10, an inconsistency inside the paper):
    T=1 : uniform 0.97, local 0.92, QeMCMC 0.335, CG improved-local-group 0.64, CG multi-sample 0.50
    T=10: uniform 0.341, local 0.26, QeMCMC 0.087, CG local-group 0.177, CG improved 0.171, CG multi-sample 0.10
  i.e. even at T=10 the fitted QeMCMC exponent is ~3x smaller than local. CAVEATS: gaps are large in
  absolute terms at T=10, n<=10 exact, and the fits are over a very small n range. Text: multi-sample
  CG "almost match[es] QeMCMC in high temperatures; performance is reduced in low temperatures".
  Main text also: 6 simulated qubits give an advantage for the magnetisation of a 36-spin system at T=0.1.
- Arai & Kadowaki 2502.08060 (Sci. Rep. 2025, s41598-025-07293-y [snippet-only]): QA-proposal gap vs T at N=10. At T>=10 the Boltzmann distribution approaches uniform, so they fix tau=0.01; "short annealing times ... yield a large spectral gap in the high-temperature region". For 1<=T<10 (intermediate) the QA update gives a larger gap than uniform/local; at T<1 the uniform/local updates fail and QA helps most. Size scaling at T=1 | QuTiP sim only, SK N<=~10.
- Cui et al. 2606.23350: gap vs T at n=6, with IQEMC > QEMC across the whole T range; scaling advantage at T=0.1; hardware toy run at T=10, n=3.
- Christmann et al. 2411.17821: all scaling at T=1; App. D Fig. 13 shows gap vs T at n=8 for Trotter/MPS proposals; MPS performance depends strongly on chi.
- Wilson et al. 2407.13830 (AQAM, Rydberg quench proposal, 12-atom sim): "large temperature regions where the AQAM has a larger MCMC spectral gap" than classical samplers | sim only.
- Pelofske 2510.10345 (direct sampling, not MCMC): at very high T a depth-1 Grover-mixer QAOA approximates the Boltzmann distribution better than the X-mixer QAOA; the quality depends heavily on the angles.
- Hsieh/Orfi/Sels 2603.28076 and both Orfi/Sels papers: analysed only at low T (beta=5 etc.); bottleneck bounds chosen for low T.
- Marshall 2602.06171: T=0.1 only. Nakano 2512.14552: ground-state fair sampling, i.e. T->0.

FACTS MOST RELEVANT TO MEDIUM AND HIGH TEMPERATURE
=========================================================================
1. P1 claims an advantage only for T <~ 1 with unnormalised SK (T_c ~ sqrt(n) ~ 3 at n=10), i.e.
   low T. No paper claims a practically useful advantage at high T.
2. The one quantitative high-T number is P2's App. B.3: at T=10, fitted k = 0.087 (QeMCMC) vs
   0.26 (local) vs 0.341 (uniform). Even at high T, the fitted gap DECAY rate is ~3x slower for
   QeMCMC. But (a) the absolute gaps are already large, (b) n<=10, (c) the table header (0.01)
   disagrees with the text (0.1) for the low-T column. Treat this as weak evidence; it needs replication.
3. Arai & Kadowaki: at T>=10 the target is near-uniform and the optimal anneal time ~0, so the
   quantum proposal degenerates toward a near-uniform proposal. The QA advantage is largest for T<1
   and still present for 1<=T<10 (intermediate) at N=10.
4. Cui et al.: IQEMC > QEMC across the whole T range at n=6; their hardware demo was at T=10 (n=3).
   That is a convenient regime for noisy hw, not evidence of high-T advantage over classical.
5. Wilson et al. (Rydberg-quench AQAM, 12 atoms): "large temperature regions" with a larger gap than
   classical samplers. Simulation only.
6. Orfi/Sels bounds and Hsieh dressing are all analysed at low T (beta=5). The IPR bound in the
   long-time limit is temperature-agnostic in form, but the papers focus on low T.
7. Inference (mine, not a paper claim): at high T the local Metropolis chain already mixes fast
   (O(n log n)-type), so a per-step quantum gain cannot offset the shot/latency cost. Medium T
   (around T_c, critical slowing down) is the only non-low-T regime where a gap gain could matter.
   I found NO paper that specifically studies QeMCMC at/near T_c, and none that compares against
   cluster algorithms there.

=========================================================================
STRONGEST CRITIQUE FOUND
=========================================================================
Combined Orfi & Sels line + Christmann et al.:
 (i) arXiv:2403.03087: no speedup for any unital proposal on a worst-case unstructured problem
     (gap O(2^-N) at low T).
 (ii) arXiv:2408.07881: in the long-time limit the gap is bounded by the eigenstate IPR, so quenching
     into an ergodic regime gives NO advantage; the advantage requires a fine-tuned intermediate
     quench.
 (iii) arXiv:2411.17821: the scaling advantage is reproduced by cheap, unconverged MPS emulation
     (chi=4, n<=9). The observed small-n "quantum" advantage is therefore not evidence of
     classical hardness.
 Supporting practical critiques:
 - Marshall's hw paper admits CPLEX solves its instances.
 - Mazumder & Towe (2607.09893): PT recovers almost all the ESS gap and wall-clock favours classical.
 - No QeMCMC paper benchmarks against tuned PT + ICM/Houdayer cluster moves.

Single strongest paper-level critique: Orfi & Sels arXiv:2408.07881 (PRA 110, 052434). It explains
WHEN the gap advantage exists (intermediate, non-ergodic, fine-tuned quench) and shows it vanishes
otherwise.
