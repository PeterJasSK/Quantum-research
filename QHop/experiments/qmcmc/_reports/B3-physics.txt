======================================================================================================================
PART 0. FOUR CROSS-CUTTING FINDINGS THAT DRIVE MOST VERDICTS BELOW (read these first)
======================================================================================================================

F1. The proposal is temperature-blind, so HIGH T is structurally the worst regime. [P1] the quantum gap stays ~0.3 at high T while
the uniform-proposal gap goes to 1 (P1 Fig 2a). [PILOT] median gap ratio vs best of local/uniform at beta=0.1 is only 1.29x, at the
critical beta=0.3 it is 2.8x. At high T any proposal that is not tuned to T wastes steps; single-spin Metropolis and heat-bath are
already near-optimal per sweep. A HIGH-T quantum case therefore needs one of: expensive energy, constraint mixing, or a many-T trick.

F2. "Expensive energy makes step advantage become wall-clock" is true but the right classical competitor then changes. [OWN] If a
cheap surrogate E_s (cluster expansion, CE) exists, the classical practitioner does not run local Metropolis with DFT. They run a
long, cheap, temperature-aware chain on E_s (PT on the CE if needed, nanoseconds per flip, 1e6-1e9 flips between expensive calls) and
use its output as a proposal, correcting with acceptance min(1, exp(-beta[(E(s')-E_s(s')) - (E(s)-E_s(s))])). This is the
surrogate-importance / delayed-acceptance family [LIT R15, R16]: Iftimie et al. report several orders of magnitude gain for DFT
sampling with a molecular-mechanics guiding potential [LIT R16]. Detailed balance: holds exactly (standard MH with a proposal that is
itself reversible w.r.t. pi_s; [OWN] derivation is the standard one). Consequence: putting the CE inside U and the DFT in the accept
step (the "surrogate-driven quantum proposal") competes against a classical proposal that is ALREADY an (almost) independent draw from
the surrogate Boltzmann distribution at the right T. The quantum proposal, being T-blind and only approximately Boltzmann-like, cannot
beat that on acceptance. It can only win when the surrogate itself is classically hard to equilibrate (glassy at LOW T), which puts us
back in P1's LOW-T cheap-energy regime with S* ~ 1e6-1e9. This kills most "DFT in the accept step" ideas at MEDIUM and HIGH T.

F3. Energy cost is NOT symmetric between local and global moves when the energy model is local. [OWN] With CE, short-range MLIPs or
lattice models, a single-site move needs only a local Delta E (O(neighbours)); a global quantum proposal that flips O(n) sites needs a
full O(N) energy, or a full relaxation. The BRIEF's model (same t_energy in both chains) is optimistic for the quantum side by a factor
~ N/(neighbourhood size) for local models. Only for genuinely non-local energies (DFT, Ewald) is t_energy ~ equal per move.

F4. Gate-model QeMCMC does not need auxiliary spins for multi-body clusters, an annealer does. [OWN] A k-body CE term c Z_i Z_j ... Z_k
is diagonal; exp(-i theta Z..Z) costs 2(k-1) CNOTs plus routing on a Trotterised gate device. Aux spins (Rosenberg-type reduction,
~1 aux per reduced product) are needed only for 2-local hardware (annealers, Rydberg analog). Conservation laws (fixed composition,
charge balance, ice rules) are best handled by a mixer that commutes with the conserved quantity: XY/SWAP mixers (Hamming weight),
charge-neutral hop terms (charge), ring-exchange terms (ice rules). All of these are real symmetric in the Z basis, so H = a*H_prob +
g*H_mix is real symmetric, U = U^T and the proposal is symmetric: detailed balance holds in the noiseless case by exactly P1's argument
[P1 + OWN]. Noise breaks it (leakage out of the sector is rejected because pi=0 there, which is safe; in-sector asymmetry is a bias,
needs twirling as in P1 SI) [P1 + OWN]. Postselection on the sector does not restore symmetry by itself under noise [OWN].

B3-01  Cold-end injection: quantum proposals only in the lowest replicas of a parallel-tempering ladder (SK / EA spin glasses)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: a spin-glass physicist gets equilibrated low-T replicas (overlap distribution P(q), finite-T phase diagram) with fewer sweeps at
  the bottleneck end of a PT ladder; this is the only fair way to test QeMCMC against the actual tool of the field.
Regime: MEDIUM to LOW. PT already makes the high-T end mix; the bottleneck is the lowest replicas. [PILOT] gap ratio grows from 2.8x at
  beta=0.3 to 25x at beta=1 vs weak baselines, so quantum steps are placed where their relative value is largest.
Mapping/embedding: native Ising. SK: dense, n(n-1)/2 ZZ terms; EA 3D: 3n ZZ terms, 3D grid onto heavy-hex needs SWAP routing [OWN].
Energy cost: cheap, local Delta E ~10-100 ns per flip on CPU [BRIEF range].
Spins: full n=20-60 on device near term; coarse-grained group (P2) of ~sqrt(N) for N=512-4096 EA samples [P2 + SPEC].
Mixing the bottleneck? Yes at the cold end; but PT + cluster moves already address it.
Best classical: PT + Houdayer (2D) / isoenergetic cluster moves (ICM, any dimension, >=10x thermalisation speedup) [LIT R10, R11];
  population annealing, about as efficient as PT [LIT R12]; dedicated FPGA (Janus) [LIT R17].
Implementation: kernel = mixture: with prob p apply quantum proposal + Metropolis at replica T_k (k in cold set), else standard sweep;
  replica swaps unchanged. Detailed balance: each component kernel is reversible w.r.t. its pi_k, a fixed-probability mixture of
  reversible kernels is reversible [OWN, standard]. Must NOT choose p adaptively from chain history without care. Device: simulator
  first (n<=24 statevector), then IBM Heron for n~20-30 with cached-shot offline chains as P1 did.
Break-even: cheap energy, S* ~ 1e6-1e9 [BRIEF]. Even with only the cold replicas quantum, the PT ladder wall-clock is dominated by the
  quantum steps unless p is tiny; with tiny p the benefit dilutes. Wall-clock win impossible on current latency [OWN].
Kill risk: the measured advantage vs PT+ICM (not vs local/uniform) is ~1x; P1 tested Houdayer on 3 instances only [P1].
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (as the decisive fair-baseline experiment for the whole programme, not as an application). Likelihood that it
  shows step advantage vs PT+ICM at n<=24: ~25% [SPEC]; wall-clock application: <2%.

B3-02  de Almeida-Thouless line: spin glass in a longitudinal field (SK and 3D EA)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: a long-standing open question (does a dAT line exist in 3D?) [LIT R17]: Janus 2014 saw no transition with standard analysis;
  2025-26 preprints argue for one (unreviewed) [LIT R17]. Answer is equilibrium P(q) at low T in field.
Regime: LOW and MEDIUM (along the putative line); field kills the Z2 symmetry.
Mapping: Ising + h terms; trivial. Embedding as B3-01.
Energy cost: cheap local.
Spins: L=8-16 3D EA = 512-4096 spins; only a coarse-grained group fits [P2-style, SPEC].
Mixing the bottleneck? Yes, extremely (Janus needed FPGA hardware). But sizes needed for finite-size scaling are far beyond full QeMCMC.
Best classical: PT on FPGA/GPU [LIT R17]; [OWN] Houdayer-type two-replica moves remain isoenergetic in a uniform field (field term
  summed over both replicas is unchanged by swapping cluster spins), but their efficiency in field is not established here.
Implementation: coarse-grained group proposals inside a PT/ICM code; group <= 30 qubits. Detailed balance: single random-group
  proposals symmetric [P2]; fixed-order sequential sweeps NOT proven symmetric [P2], use random order.
Break-even: S* ~ 1e6-1e9 vs Janus-class hardware (updates in the ~100 ps per spin class [LIT R17, order of magnitude not re-verified]).
Kill risk: group of ~30 spins in a 4096-spin glass is a local move with extra steps; [P2] single-group exponents 0.64-0.77 at n<=10
  (vs uniform 0.97) give no evidence at the sizes that matter. Also [P2] at small groups the move is classically simulable.
Verdict: WAIT FOR HARDWARE (science interest real; nothing buildable that would change the field). ~5%.

B3-04  Hardware-native heavy-hex / Pegasus spin glass as an exactly checkable calibration bed
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: the team gets an instance family with no embedding overhead (one ZZ per physical coupler) and, in zero field on a planar graph,
  an exact partition function (Pfaffian / Kasteleyn method, polynomial time) to validate stationarity and bias from noise [OWN; planarity
  of heavy-hex is [OWN] reasoning: it is a decorated hexagonal lattice].
Regime: all three; the point is calibration across T, including the high-T region where P1 predicts no gain.
Mapping: native, zero SWAPs on IBM heavy-hex; 1D chains as in P1 are a subset [P1].
Energy cost: cheap.
Spins: 20-127+ physical qubits.
Mixing the bottleneck? Not relevant; this is a measurement of the proposal, not an application.
Best classical: for planar zero field, exact sampling is classically polynomial [OWN, standard planar-Ising result]; so any "advantage"
  here is by construction not useful. That is the point: it is a fair-bias/noise testbed.
Implementation: random +-J on the coupling map; ideal U by Trotter; measure TV distance of the empirical chain distribution against the
  exact Boltzmann marginals; compare twirled vs non-twirled to quantify noise-induced bias [P1 SI noted bias]. Detailed balance:
  ideal yes [P1]; noisy measured directly against exact answer [OWN design].
Break-even: not applicable.
Kill risk: a reviewer calls it a toy; mitigate by stating clearly it is calibration.
Verdict: BUILD NOW (as calibration infrastructure, not as an application). ~80% that it yields a clean noise-bias measurement.

B3-05  Surrogate-in-U, DFT-in-accept for alloy order-disorder (CE inside H_prob, DFT energy in the Metropolis step)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: a materials engineer would get a DFT-exact (not CE-approximate) order-disorder temperature and SRO, removing CE fitting error.
Regime: MEDIUM (order-disorder transitions) and HIGH (SRO above T_c), as requested.
Mapping: CE with pairs, triplets, quadruplets -> diagonal multi-Z Hamiltonian; gate-model needs no aux spins (F4) [OWN].
Energy cost: CE ~ tens of ns per local Delta E [OWN est.]; DFT single point on a ~50-200-atom metallic cell ~ minutes to hours per
  configuration, plus relaxation [SPEC: practitioner range, not sourced here]; prior work stresses DFT cost becomes prohibitive for large
  HEA supercells [LIT R23, R24].
Spins: 32-256 sites per DFT cell (DFT limits the cell, so full QeMCMC on the cell is conceivable on 30-100 qubit devices) [OWN].
Mixing the bottleneck? NO. The bottleneck is the number of DFT calls per independent sample.
Best classical: surrogate-importance / delayed acceptance [LIT R15, R16]: run classical PT on the CE (free compared to DFT) and propose
  its decorrelated output; accept with the residual exp(-beta Delta(E_DFT - E_CE)). Also active-learning on-lattice NN potentials
  [LIT R27]. See F2.
Implementation (as asked): U from CE couplings with P1 alpha scaling; measured s' is DFT-evaluated; accept min(1, exp(-beta Delta E_DFT)).
  Detailed balance: holds for the DFT Boltzmann distribution because U is symmetric for any real H_prob [P1]; the CE only shapes the
  proposal. Latency is irrelevant here (DFT dominates), so S = tau_C/tau_Q is wall-clock [BRIEF model].
Break-even: S* ~ 1 (t_Q ~ t_C ~ t_DFT) [OWN]. This is the one place where the BRIEF's arithmetic is trivially satisfied.
Kill risk (decisive): the classical surrogate-importance chain proposes from pi_CE at the right T, so its acceptance depends only on the
  CE error; the quantum proposal is T-blind and accepts on the full Delta E_DFT, so at MEDIUM/HIGH T its acceptance is lower [OWN, F1+F2].
  Quantum can only win if PT on the CE fails to equilibrate, which does not happen for typical binary/ternary CE at order-disorder T.
Verdict: KILL (dominated by a free classical construction). Report it as a clear negative result. Likelihood of a real win: <3%.

B3-06  MLIP-in-the-loop with lattice relaxation: HEA short-range order where CE fails because of relaxations
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: alloy designer gets SRO / Warren-Cowley parameters in size-mismatched HEAs where relaxations shift transitions (shown with
  ML potentials for HEAs [LIT R23]); here a cheap rigid-lattice surrogate is poor, which weakens the F2 competitor.
Regime: MEDIUM-HIGH (SRO at processing/annealing temperatures, around order-disorder).
Mapping: k-component Potts on fixed lattice; one-hot (k qubits/site) with SWAP mixers, or binary ceil(log2 k) qubits/site with invalid
  codes rejected (pi=0) [OWN]. H_prob = a rigid-lattice CE or pair-only surrogate.
Energy cost: MACE-MPA-0 ~3 us per atom per optimisation step on H100 [LIT R22]. [OWN] 500-atom cell: ~1.5 ms per force call; a
  50-300-step relaxation ~0.08-0.5 s. Rigid-lattice MLIP (low-rank potentials) is "extremely fast" [LIT R23], far below that.
Spins: 100-1000 sites per cell; full QeMCMC infeasible; coarse-grained groups of 20-50 sites [P2-style, SPEC].
Mixing the bottleneck? Partly: swaps between unlike species are rejected often near ordering; but per-sample cost is dominated by
  relaxations.
Best classical: canonical swap MC + PT with MLIP on GPU at scale (SMC-X: billion-atom HEA, >3e6 swap steps, checkerboard parallel)
  [LIT R24]; surrogate-importance with an ML on-lattice surrogate retrained by active learning [LIT R27].
Implementation: group proposal (P2) on a block of ~20-50 sites with the rigid-lattice surrogate in U; full MLIP relaxation + energy in
  accept. Detailed balance: single random group = symmetric [P2] -> exact for the relaxed-MLIP Boltzmann distribution, provided the
  relaxation is deterministic (same s -> same relaxed energy) [OWN; nondeterministic relaxation would add noise to pi].
Break-even: t_C ~ t_Q ~ 0.1-1 s if every step relaxes [OWN] -> S* ~ 1-10. But F3: classical local swaps can use local relaxation
  windows (cheaper), and SMC-X parallelises thousands of independent local swaps on one GPU; per-sample cost comparison then favours
  classical by ~N_sites/block [OWN].
Kill risk: parallel local swaps on GPU (SMC-X) give O(N) updates per energy evaluation; one quantum group proposal gives one block move.
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE only if the team wants one expensive-energy case with S* ~ O(1-10); expectation low. ~8%.

B3-07  Fixed-composition (Kawasaki) alloy sampling with a Hamming-weight-conserving XY/SWAP mixer: CuAu-type order-disorder
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: algorithmic: the first QeMCMC gap study under a conservation law on a physical alloy model (CuAu L1_0, ordering ~ 680 K
  [SPEC: textbook value, not re-verified]); a materials engineer would get the canonical phase diagram.
Regime: MEDIUM (first-order order-disorder; latent heat, two-phase coexistence).
Mapping: binary site occupancy; X mixer would leave the composition sector and nearly every proposal would be rejected; XY mixer
  sum (X_iX_j + Y_iY_j)/2 on lattice edges (or all-pair) conserves total Z [OWN]; XX+YY is real in the Z basis [OWN check: YY has real
  entries], so H real symmetric -> symmetric proposal -> detailed balance [P1 argument + OWN]. Related prior art: QAOA with XY mixer as
  surrogate-trained proposal for Kawasaki dynamics, 20.3x / 7.6x autocorrelation speedups on 3-regular graphs vs classical Kawasaki
  [LIT R5]; QAOA-MC [LIT R6].
Energy cost: CE cheap.
Spins: 64-512 sites per cell; groups of 20-40 for near-term [SPEC].
Mixing the bottleneck? At a first-order transition yes (tunnelling between ordered and disordered phases), and that is what
  multicanonical/WL fix classically.
Best classical: canonical swap MC (any-pair swaps, not only nearest neighbours: non-local Kawasaki is free classically) + PT;
  Wang-Landau / multicanonical for first-order transitions [SPEC: standard methods; WL for alloys used widely]; independent swap
  proposals parallelised on GPU [LIT R24].
Implementation: Trotterised XY ring/all-to-all mixer; statevector up to 24 qubits, then group variant. Measure gap vs nonlocal swap MC,
  not vs nearest-neighbour Kawasaki (the latter is a weak baseline, a trap present in R5's framing [OWN reading of R5 abstract]).
Break-even: cheap energy, S* ~ 1e6-1e9.
Kill risk: at a first-order transition the barrier is a surface free energy ~ L^(d-1); a T-blind proposal does not reduce it [SPEC];
  WL/multicanonical does by design.
Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (as an algorithm paper on conserving QeMCMC; no application value). ~15% to show a clean step-count gain
  vs nonlocal swap + PT at n<=24.

B3-08  Multi-component high-entropy alloy (Potts, k=4-5 species) SRO across temperature
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: SRO maps for CrCoNi / MoNbTaW-type alloys; accuracy matters (EAM gives up to 25% error in Warren-Cowley parameters in CrCoNi

Energy cost: CE / rigid-lattice ML cheap; with relaxation see B3-06.
Spins: 100-1000 sites x 3-5 qubits = 300-5000 qubits [OWN]; only small groups feasible.
Mixing the bottleneck? At HIGH T no (F1). At ordering, moderately.
Best classical: canonical multi-species swap MC + PT, GPU checkerboard at billion-atom scale [LIT R24]; Wang-Landau.
Implementation: group of 6-8 sites x 5 qubits = 30-40 qubits with SWAP mixers; detailed balance as B3-07.
Break-even: S* ~ 1e6-1e9.
Kill risk: qubit overhead of the Potts encoding plus F1 at HIGH T.
Verdict: KILL. ~1%.

B3-09  Long-range Coulomb (Ewald) ionic ordering with charge balance: disordered rocksalt (DRX) cathodes and mixed-valence oxides
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: battery materials scientist gets Li/TM/vacancy ordering, SRO and voltage curves in charge-decorated CE + Ewald models where
  single-site moves are forbidden by charge balance [LIT R14] and Swendsen-Wang/Wolff-type cluster moves do not apply (frustrated,
  long-range, multi-species) [OWN].
Regime: MEDIUM (cation SRO/ordering at synthesis temperatures ~ 1000-1300 K [SPEC]) and LOW (glassy charge order). This is the
  physically most SK-like model in B3's scope: dense, frustrated, long-range couplings, which is exactly where P1 measured k=0.264 vs
  0.94 [P1] (SK, not Ewald: transfer is [SPEC]).
Mapping: charge-decorated site occupancies (Potts-like, several species and valences); Ewald term = dense pair couplings J_ij on all
  sites [LIT R14 uses Ewald with CE]. Constraint: total charge Q = sum q_i n_i must be conserved. Mixer must commute with Q: real
  symmetric "neutral hop" terms that swap species with equal charge, plus coupled pair moves that exchange charge compensatingly (the
  quantum analog of smol's table-exchange moves [LIT R14]) [OWN]. Embedding: dense ZZ on n qubits needs ~n(n-1)/2 two-qubit
  interactions per Trotter step; with a SWAP network depth ~n layers [OWN].
Energy cost: precomputed Ewald matrix: Delta E per single-site change O(N) multiply-adds -> ~1 us for N=1000 [OWN]; still cheap.
Spins: 100-1000 sites, 2-3 qubits per site.
Mixing the bottleneck? Yes, plausibly: charge balance restricts moves and long-range frustration produces many nearly degenerate states;
  the classical fixes (table exchange, square-charge bias) are recent and specific [LIT R14].
Best classical: canonical / charge-balanced semigrand MC with table exchange or square-charge bias, plus PT [LIT R14]; no general
  cluster algorithm known to us for this case [OWN, not exhaustively searched].
Implementation: (a) statevector study, n<=24 qubits (8-12 sites), measure gap vs table-exchange + PT; (b) group variant on device with
  Ewald effective fields from frozen sites (P2 effective-field construction applies directly to dense couplings [P2]). Detailed
  balance: real symmetric H with Q-commuting mixer -> symmetric within the charge sector [OWN]; noisy leakage to other sectors -> pi=0 ->
  rejected, safe [OWN]; in-sector bias needs twirling [P1].
Break-even: S* ~ 1e5-1e6 (dense O(N) energy raises t_C) [OWN]. No wall-clock path near term; dense ZZ circuits are also the worst case
  for heavy-hex routing [OWN].
Kill risk: the step advantage disappears once table-exchange + PT is the baseline; circuit depth for dense couplings.
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE. Best scientific case in B3 for a genuine MEDIUM-regime step advantage over the BEST classical method, because
  the best classical method here is comparatively weak. ~20% step win vs TE+PT at small n; wall-clock ~1%.

B3-12  Pyrochlore spin ice with an ice-rule-conserving quantum mixer (hexagon ring exchange = quantum spin ice as the proposal)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: a striking physics link: the proposal dynamics is quantum spin ice (U(1) spin liquid, emergent QED) [LIT R19], used to propose

  leaves the ice rules.
Regime: LOW and MEDIUM (inside the ice manifold, approaching the dipolar ordering transition, which single-spin flips miss [LIT R8]).

  (S+S-S+S-S+S- + h.c.): real 0/1 matrix elements in the Z basis -> real symmetric -> U=U^T -> symmetric proposal, and it commutes with
  every tetrahedron's ice-rule operator [OWN, follows from R19's construction]. Detailed balance holds in the noiseless case [OWN + P1].
  Cost: each 6-body ring term needs a multi-controlled construction, tens of CNOTs per hexagon per Trotter step [OWN est.].
Energy cost: nearest-neighbour cheap; dipolar (Ewald) O(N) per flip.
Spins: 16-site cubic cell already contains hexagons; 128-1024 for physics [SPEC].
Mixing the bottleneck? Within the ice manifold, classical loop moves (short/long loops) already work well [LIT R8, R9]; at the dipolar
  ordering transition, loop moves enabled the first observation of the first-order transition [LIT R8].
Best classical: Barkema-Newman / Melko-Gingras loop algorithms [LIT R8, R9]; spin-ice cluster algorithm (arXiv:1411.3807, seen in search,
  not read).
Implementation: statevector, 16-32 spins; compare to loop + PT. Annealers cannot do ring exchange directly (only transverse field;
  ring exchange appears perturbatively) [LIT R18 context + SPEC].
Break-even: S* ~ 1e6-1e9; no application path.
Kill risk: loop moves are already near-ideal in the manifold; 6-body circuits are deep.
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE as a physics/demo paper (conserving mixers are the honest way to handle hard constraints); application KILL.
  ~30% for a clean "constraint-preserving QeMCMC" simulation result; <1% for useful advantage.

B3-13  Proton (H-bond) ordering in ice with DFT/MLIP energies: ice Ih -> ice XI ordering and proton order in high-pressure ices
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: proton-ordering transition temperatures and ordered structures, where energy differences between proton configurations are
  small and need DFT-level accuracy; a hard constraint (ice rules) AND an expensive energy in one problem.
Regime: LOW-MEDIUM (ordering transitions at tens of K [SPEC: ice XI ~72 K, not re-verified]).
Mapping: bond-variable Ising (proton position per H-bond), ice rules = 2-in-2-out per oxygen; mixer = ring exchange on hexagonal rings
  as in B3-12 [OWN]; energy = graph-invariant expansion fitted to DFT (cheap) or DFT/MLIP directly.
Energy cost: graph-invariant surrogate cheap; DFT minutes-hours per cell [SPEC]; MLIP ms-s (R22 scaling).
Spins: 96-384 bonds per DFT cell [SPEC].
Mixing the bottleneck? Classical loop moves (Rahman-Stillinger, Barkema-Newman) sample the ice manifold efficiently [LIT R9];
  mixing within the constraint is solved.
Best classical: loop moves + surrogate (graph invariants) + surrogate-importance correction (F2).
Implementation: B3-12 circuit with DFT in accept.
Break-even: as B3-05: S* ~ 1 but dominated by classical surrogate-importance with loop moves.
Kill risk: F2 + loop moves.
Verdict: KILL (as application). ~2%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3-14  HP lattice protein density of states with Wang-Landau acceptance and a symmetric quantum proposal
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: folding thermodynamics (specific heat, collapse and folding transitions) of HP sequences across all T from one g(E).
Regime: all T (WL gives every temperature at once); medium-T collapse transition is the physics target.
Mapping: turn encoding (2-3 qubits per bond) with self-avoidance and contacts as multi-body penalties; resource-efficient lattice folding
  Hamiltonians scale O(N^4) terms, 22 qubits for 10 residues [LIT R20]. Self-avoidance as a penalty in H_prob is allowed (P1: wrong H_prob
  still exact) and invalid conformations get pi=0 -> rejected [P1 + OWN].
Energy cost: trivially cheap (contact count).
Spins: 20-100 qubits for 10-30 residues [LIT R20 scaling + OWN]; benchmark sequences are 48-103 residues [LIT R13].
Mixing the bottleneck? Yes for ground states of long sequences, but pull moves + bond rebridging + WL find all known putative ground states
  and improved 3D103 [LIT R13].
Best classical: WL with pull moves and bond-rebridging, replica-exchange WL [LIT R13].
Implementation: symmetric quantum proposal plugged into WL acceptance min(1, g(E)/g(E')) — WL requires a symmetric proposal, which QeMCMC
  provides [OWN]; WL itself is not stationary during the flat-histogram phase (standard caveat) [OWN].
Break-even: cheap energy, S* ~ 1e6-1e9.
Kill risk: most proposals violate self-avoidance (low acceptance); qubit count per residue; classical toolbox is excellent.
Verdict: KILL. <1%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3-15  Grand-canonical adsorbate lattice gas under reaction conditions (O, CO on Pt/Ru) with DFT-fitted lateral interactions
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: surface scientist gets coverage-dependent phase diagrams (p(2x2), (2x1) adlayers) at catalyst operating temperatures.
Regime: MEDIUM (adlayer order-disorder at a few hundred K [SPEC]) and HIGH (disordered adlayer at operating T).
Mapping: occupancy per site (1-3 species), multi-body lateral CE; grand canonical -> X mixer OK; canonical coverage -> XY mixer.
Energy cost: CE cheap; DFT-in-loop is the F2 case.
Spins: 100-1000 sites.
Mixing the bottleneck? Rarely: simple ordered adlayers, 2D, short-range; histogram reweighting and WL handle transitions.
Best classical: GCMC + WL; kinetic MC when rates matter (not equilibrium sampling) [SPEC: standard].
Implementation: trivial mapping; no reason to build.
Break-even: S* ~ 1e6-1e9.
Kill risk: no mixing problem.
Verdict: KILL. <1%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3-16  Potts model of grain growth
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: none for QeMCMC. Potts grain-growth (Anderson, Srolovitz, Grest 1984) [LIT R26] is used as a KINETIC model: the Monte Carlo
  step is a proxy for time and the observable is the coarsening path (grain size vs MCS), usually at low or zero T; n-fold way speeds it
  up [LIT R26].
Regime: LOW, non-equilibrium.
Mapping: Q-state Potts (Q ~ 50-100 orientations) -> ceil(log2 Q) qubits/site.
Energy cost: cheap.
Mixing the bottleneck? No: equilibrium is a single grain; nobody wants to sample it.
Best classical: n-fold way / rejection-free kinetic MC [LIT R26].
Implementation: none: a non-local quantum proposal destroys the physical kinetics that is the whole point [OWN].
Verdict: KILL (category error: kinetics, not equilibrium sampling). 0%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3-17  Unfrustrated magnets near criticality (Ising/clock models of ferromagnets, Curie point)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: critical exponents / Curie temperatures.
Regime: MEDIUM (critical).
Mapping: native.
Energy cost: cheap.
Mixing the bottleneck? Critical slowing down is real for local moves, but cluster algorithms remove most of it.
Best classical: Swendsen-Wang (1987) and Wolff (1989) cluster algorithms, small dynamic exponents for Ising-type ferromagnets [SPEC:
  standard literature, not re-verified in this session].
Implementation: none worthwhile.
Break-even: S* ~ 1e6-1e9 vs an already near-ideal cluster method.
Verdict: KILL. 0-1%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3-18  Lattice polymers and lattice-gas condensation (self-avoiding walks, coil-globule, liquid-gas in lattice gas)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: polymer scaling exponents, theta point.
Regime: MEDIUM-HIGH (coil-globule at theta T; good-solvent high T).
Mapping: occupancy + chain connectivity constraints -> same encoding pain as HP (B3-14).
Energy cost: cheap.
Mixing the bottleneck? Classical pivot algorithm (with efficient implementations) makes SAW sampling extremely fast [SPEC: Clisby's
  pivot work, not re-verified]; lattice-gas condensation maps to Ising and gets cluster moves.
Best classical: pivot + PERM / WL; Swendsen-Wang for lattice gas.
Verdict: KILL. 0-1%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3-19  Temperature-independent proposal amortised over a whole temperature ladder (finite-T phase diagram in one compiled circuit family)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: a phase-diagram user (any lattice model) needs 20-100 temperatures; since H_prob is not divided by T [P1], one compiled
  parametric circuit family serves every replica; compile cost is amortised over the ladder.
Regime: designed for MEDIUM+HIGH (the many-temperature need), where per-replica quantum value is lowest (F1).
Mapping: any of the above.
Energy cost: any.
Mixing the bottleneck? At HIGH T no; the amortisation only helps a cost term (t_compile) that is already amortisable within one chain
  (P1 used cached shots offline [P1]).
Best classical: PT + multi-histogram reweighting / WL give the full T range from fewer runs [SPEC: standard].
Implementation: batch submission of all replicas' current states in one job (one circuit per replica, same parameters drawn per step)
  to amortise latency over the ladder; detailed balance per replica as in P1; replica swaps standard [OWN].
Break-even: batching R replicas per job divides t_latency by R [OWN]; with R=50 and 1 s latency, t_Q ~ 20 ms + shot time per replica
  step, still ~1e5-1e6 above a CPU spin flip [OWN].
Kill risk: amortisation helps latency, not the T-blindness; high-T replicas get almost nothing (PILOT 1.29x).
Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (the batching trick is worth using in B3-01 experiments). Useful engineering, ~0% standalone value.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3-20  Quantum annealer as proposal engine (QAEMCMC) on annealer-native lattices
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: thousands of physical qubits today (913-spin kagome ice on 2742 qubits [LIT R18]); QAEMCMC claims larger gaps and faster
  convergence on SK [LIT R4, abstract only].
Regime: LOW-MEDIUM.
Mapping: native 2-body on Pegasus/Zephyr; multi-body CE needs aux spins (F4).
Energy cost: cheap.
Mixing the bottleneck? Yes at LOW T.
Best classical: PT + ICM, which was explicitly shown on the chimera topology [LIT R10].
Implementation and DETAILED BALANCE FLAG: a forward anneal returns s' from a distribution Q(s') that does not depend on s and is
  unknown; annealer outputs are in general not Boltzmann and the effective temperature fluctuates between programming cycles
  [LIT R21]. Metropolis acceptance min(1, pi(s')/pi(s)) is then WRONG (independence sampler needs Q(s)/Q(s') which is unknown) [OWN].
  Reverse annealing from s gives an s-dependent proposal but no proof of symmetry Q(s'|s)=Q(s|s') on real hardware [OWN]. Unless R4
  provides a symmetry argument valid on hardware (not checked; abstract only), stationarity is not guaranteed.
Break-even: annealer latency per call is ms-scale plus job overhead [SPEC]; S* ~ 1e5-1e8.
Kill risk: unverifiable detailed balance; classical PT+ICM on the same topology.
Verdict: WAIT FOR HARDWARE (needs a symmetric, characterised annealer proposal). ~3%.

Energy cost: cheap.

KNOWN-K15  Alloy and Li/vacancy ordering from cluster-expansion ECIs

  composition-conserving XY/SWAP mixers for canonical runs (B3-07); charge-conserving mixers if Ewald (B3-09).

Mixing the bottleneck? Generally no for binary CE; yes for charge-balanced long-range (B3-09).

Implementation: split into B3-07 (algorithmic conserving-mixer study) and B3-09 (the one subcase with a weak classical baseline).
Break-even: S* ~ 1e6-1e9 (CE); ~1e5-1e6 with dense Ewald.
Kill risk: binary CE has no mixing problem.
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE only in the B3-09 (charge-balanced Ewald) form; KILL for plain binary alloys / Li-vacancy semigrand. ~15% / <1%.
