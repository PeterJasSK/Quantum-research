PART 0. CROSS-CUTTING FINDINGS (read before the idea list)

0.1 Where does T=1 sit for a trained RBM? It moves during training.
  Early training: weights small, model paramagnetic, T=1 is HIGH relative to the model. Block Gibbs mixes in a few sweeps.
  Mid training: the model crosses a cascade of mode-forming transitions, each one a second-order transition with critical slowing
  down [LIT arXiv:2405.14689 "Cascade of phase transitions in the training of energy-based models"; arXiv:2405.15376 states that
  distant modes are encoded by second-order transitions and mixing times diverge]. T=1 is MEDIUM at those moments.
  Late training on clustered/multimodal data: slow mixing is tied to phase coexistence, as near a first-order transition
  [LIT arXiv:2206.01310, Bereux, Decelle, Furtlehner, Seoane, SciPost Phys. 14, 032 (2023)]. Here T=1 behaves as LOW relative to
  the model, and standard parallel tempering in temperature is reported to fail at first-order transitions [LIT, stated in the
  2405.15376 line of work]. Alternating Gibbs sampling does no better than Metropolis on the effective visible-layer landscape
  [LIT arXiv:2107.06013, Roussel, Cocco, Monasson, PRE 104, 034109 (2021)].
  Consequence: the RBM negative phase is NOT a uniformly HIGH-T problem. The MEDIUM/LOW window is real, but it is exactly where the
  newest classical tools (tethered MC, parallel trajectory tempering) were designed.

0.2 Is mixing the binding constraint in RBM training?
  Decelle, Furtlehner, Seoane [LIT arXiv:2105.13889, NeurIPS 2021]: two regimes. If the number of MC steps k per gradient update is
  below the mixing time, training is out of equilibrium; the model still produces good-looking samples after k steps from the same
  init but does not learn the correct equilibrium distribution. So mixing binds only if the user wants an equilibrium model
  (likelihood, calibrated generation, interpretability, physics/biology uses). For "make samples that look good" it does not bind.
  Bereux et al. [LIT arXiv:2405.15376, ICLR 2025]: for highly structured data, training is slowed by MCMC mixing and their PTT
  (parallel trajectory tempering, swaps between models saved along the training trajectory) plus low-rank PCA pre-training fixes much
  of it. This is the strongest current classical competitor in the regime where a quantum move might matter.
  For deep EBMs with neural-network energies the binding constraints are energy-evaluation cost and gradient variance/instability,
  not discrete mixing per se [SPEC, consistent with 2102.04509 and 2206.09914 framing].

0.3 Structural kill-point for coarse-grained QeMCMC on RBMs [OWN].
  RBM energy E(v,h) = -a.v - b.h - v^T W h. There are NO visible-visible and NO hidden-hidden couplings. If a P2-style group contains
  only visible units (hidden frozen) the effective H_prob on the group is pure local fields, so exp(-iHt) factorises into single-qubit
  rotations and the "quantum" proposal is a product distribution, strictly worse than exact block Gibbs, which samples that
  conditional exactly in one shot. Same for hidden-only groups. Any useful group must mix visible and hidden units (a k_v x k_h
  bipartite sub-block of W). This also says: in the joint (v,h) space the classical baseline is not single-spin Metropolis but exact
  block Gibbs of half the system in one step, a much stronger "local" baseline than in P1/P2.

0.4 Embedding cost of a dense bipartite RBM on gate hardware [OWN, rough].
  One Trotter step of H_prob needs n_v x n_h RZZ terms. On heavy-hex with a bipartite SWAP network, roughly 2 to 3 two-qubit gates per
  coupling per Trotter step. With t ~ U[2,20] [P1] tens of Trotter steps are plausible [OWN, depends on step-size tolerance].
  Example 16v x 16h: 256 couplings x ~3 x ~20 steps = ~15,000 two-qubit gates. At two-qubit error ~1e-3 to 5e-3 [SPEC, to be replaced
  by team hardware scan] fidelity is effectively zero; the proposal degenerates toward a depolarised, near-uniform proposal. A near-
  uniform proposal is useless at MEDIUM/LOW T and offers nothing over classical uniform at HIGH T. Rough budget for a meaningful
  proposal (~1e2 to 1e3 two-qubit gates) allows only about 8v x 8h dense, or ~50 to 100 spins on a hardware-native sparse graph with
  few Trotter steps [OWN].

0.5 Throughput, batching and the latency picture [OWN unless marked].
  ML negative phases need many independent chains (minibatch B = 1e2 to 1e4 fantasy particles). Chains are independent, so one job can
  advance B chains by ONE step each (step i+1 of a chain needs the classical accept of step i). Per synchronous round:
    t_round = t_latency + B x t_shot   (each chain = a different circuit: different initial bitstring, gamma, t).
  With t_latency ~1 s and t_shot ~1e-4 to 1e-3 s [brief ranges], latency is amortised once B >~ 1e3 to 1e4. Best-case QPU throughput is
  then ~1e3 to 1e4 chain-steps per second, set by shot rate, not latency. Batching rescues latency; it does not rescue throughput.
  Classical comparison: GPU block Gibbs for a 784v x 500h RBM, B=1000: one sweep is two GEMMs of ~0.4 GFLOP each; at ~50 TFLOP/s that is
  ~20 to 40 microseconds per sweep of 1000 chains, i.e. ~3e7 chain-sweeps/s, ~4e10 spin updates/s [OWN]. Independent anchor: FPGA p-bit
  machine at 50 to 64 billion flips/s, about 10x GPU/TPU [LIT arXiv:2303.10728, Niazi et al.].
  For a small RBM (32 spins) CPU block Gibbs is ~1e7 to 1e8 chain-sweeps/s [OWN].
  Break-even per proposal: S* = (classical chain-sweeps/s)/(quantum chain-steps/s) ~ 1e7/1e3 to 1e8/1e4 ~ 1e3 to 1e5 sweeps; with
  conservative latency (no amortisation) 1e5 to 1e8 [OWN]. A quantum proposal must be worth 1e3 to 1e5 classical sweeps even in the
  best batched case. That is only plausible when classical tunnelling between modes takes >1e5 sweeps, i.e. LOW/coexistence regime at
  sizes that do not fit hardware (0.4). Batching therefore converts the problem from "impossible" (1e6 to 1e9 sequential) to "very hard"
  (1e3 to 1e5), and GPUs gain from batching too, so the ratio does not improve by more than the latency factor.
  Offline caching (as P1 did [P1]): for n <= ~16 one can precompute proposal shots for all 2^n start states and replay them. Exact if
  each cached shot is used at most once (fresh randomness); reusing cached shots correlates chains and introduces bias [OWN]. Useful
  for experiments, not a deployment path.

0.6 Detailed balance checklist used below [OWN, from brief/P1].
  Symmetric proposal holds for any real-symmetric H_prob (even a wrong surrogate) when gamma, t are drawn independently of the state
  [P1]. Mixture or composition of pi-invariant kernels (block Gibbs + QeMCMC step) keeps pi invariant (stationarity; composition is not
  reversible unless symmetrised) [OWN, standard MCMC]. Random choice of coarse-grained group keeps symmetry; fixed-order multi-group
  sweep does not in general [P2]. Hardware noise breaks symmetry, notably asymmetric readout (1 -> 0 relaxation); mitigation: Pauli
  twirling [P1 SI] plus random spin-reversal (gauge) transforms per shot, which symmetrise readout bias on average [OWN/SPEC]; residual
  bias must be measured, not assumed.

0.7 Quantum-annealer Boltzmann-sampling history (relevant priors).
  Adachi and Henderson used D-Wave samples for RBM model expectations on coarse-grained MNIST [LIT arXiv:1510.06356]. Benedetti et al.
  showed annealers sample at an instance-dependent effective temperature that must be estimated [LIT arXiv:1510.07611, PRA 94, 022308];
  performance near CD-100 in their case study. Dixit et al. trained RBMs on D-Wave 2000Q (bars and stripes) [LIT arXiv:2005.03247].
  Kalis et al. used annealer samples as initial states for classical Gibbs chains [LIT arXiv:2304.12418]. Job and Adachi reproduced the
  2015 result on newer hardware and found simulated annealing matches it: no quantum advantage for classical RBM training on stoquastic
  annealers [LIT arXiv:2009.00134]. Quantum Boltzmann machine (transverse-field Gibbs model) [LIT arXiv:1601.02036, PRX 8, 021050].
  QeMCMC-specific ML prior art: QeMCMC adapted to sample neural-network wavefunctions in VMC [LIT arXiv:2502.20799]; quantum-enabled
  VMC for NQS [LIT arXiv:2412.12398]. No paper found applying Layden-style QeMCMC to classical RBM/EBM training [search result, one
  query; absence not proven]. Theoretical caution: no speedup for unstructured worst case [LIT arXiv:2403.03087, Orfi and Sels];
  gap bounded by inverse participation ratio, no advantage when quenching to an ergodic system, needs fine-tuned quenches
  [LIT arXiv:2408.07881].


PART 1. IDEAS

B2-01  QeMCMC as the persistent negative-phase kernel for small RBMs (PCD with quantum moves)
  Payoff: ML researchers training small equilibrium RBMs (bars-and-stripes, 8x8 binary images, small biology datasets) get a better-
  mixed negative phase per gradient step, hence closer-to-equilibrium models (2105.13889 regime) with fewer MC steps.
  Regime: moves through HIGH (early) -> MEDIUM (mode-forming transitions) -> LOW-like (late, clustered data) (see 0.1). Average over
  training is mostly HIGH; the gain window is the late phase only.
  Mapping: joint (v,h) Ising, bipartite K_{nv,nh}; spins = nv+nh; dense; embedding 2 to 3 two-qubit gates per coupling per Trotter
  step (0.4). Detailed balance: symmetric proposal on joint state, Metropolis accept with exact joint energy; composed with block Gibbs.
  Energy cost per step: cheap (one bilinear form, O(nv nh)).
  Spins needed: full 24 to 64 for toy RBMs; coarse-grained mixed v/h groups of 8 to 16 (0.3).
  Mixing the bottleneck? Only if equilibrium is required and data are clustered; otherwise gradient noise and k choice dominate (0.2).
  Best classical competitor: PCD with GPU block Gibbs; PT for RBMs [LIT Desjardins et al. AISTATS 2010, PMLR v9; adaptive PT
  arXiv:1012.3476]; PTT [LIT arXiv:2405.15376].
  Implementation sketch: B=100 to 1000 persistent chains; each gradient step: k block-Gibbs sweeps on CPU plus one QeMCMC step per chain
  batched into one job of B parametrised circuits (random gamma, t, init bits as X gates), 1 shot each; accept classically; update W.
  Simulate first with statevector at n<=20 to measure integrated autocorrelation of the log-likelihood gradient estimate.
  Break-even: S* ~1e3 to 1e5 block-Gibbs sweeps per quantum step even when batched (0.5); toy RBMs mix in far fewer sweeps.
  Kill risk: classical block Gibbs already exact on half the system; toy RBMs are too easy; real RBMs too big for hardware.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE as a benchmark only; wall-clock advantage WAIT FOR HARDWARE. Likelihood of any wall-clock win: <5% [SPEC].

B2-02  Mode-hopping QeMCMC move for multimodal RBMs in phase coexistence
  Payoff: groups that need equilibrium RBMs on clustered data (Decelle/Seoane line, protein RBMs) get transitions between distant
  modes, correct relative mode weights, and unbiased log-likelihood gradients late in training.
  Regime: LOW relative to the model (first-order-like coexistence at T=1, 2206.01310), adjacent MEDIUM at mode-forming transitions.
  This is the one ML setting that matches the P1/P2 claimed-advantage regime and where temperature-PT is known to struggle.
  Mapping: joint (v,h) Ising; dense bipartite; best done on a coarse-grained mixed group spanning the hidden units that encode the mode
  switch plus the visible units they couple to most (choose by |W| magnitude). Detailed balance: group chosen by a rule independent of
  the current state (e.g. fixed per epoch from W, random within that family) keeps symmetry; state-dependent group choice breaks it.
  Energy cost: cheap.
  Spins needed: full system 50 to 1000+; group of ~sqrt(n) = 8 to 30 mixed units.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Yes, by construction of the regime, per 2206.01310 and 2405.15376.
  Best classical competitor: tethered MC [LIT arXiv:2206.01310]; PTT [LIT arXiv:2405.15376]; also Metropolis in latent space when hidden
  units are weakly dependent [LIT, Roussel et al. 2107.06013 follow-up remark]; Wolff-type cluster moves do not apply (no simple symmetry).
  Implementation sketch: exact simulation at n<=20 on synthetic 2- to 4-cluster datasets trained to coexistence; measure mode-switch rate
  per proposal for quantum vs block Gibbs vs PTT vs uniform-on-group vs a classical "flip correlated block" proposal; then P2 groups
  at n=36 to 100 with statevector groups of 10 to 20. Hardware stage only if group proposals beat PTT per step by >1e3.
  Break-even: need classical mode-switch time >~1e5 sweeps and quantum switch in O(1 to 10) proposals (0.5). Plausible only at larger
  n where groups of 10 to 20 are still classically simulable, which makes it QUANTUM-INSPIRED at those sizes [P2 note].
  Kill risk: PTT already resolves coexistence cheaply; P2 gap exponents at group size are much weaker (0.64 to 0.77) than full QeMCMC
  (0.335) [P2]; the mode switch may require flipping more units than a group holds.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (strongest scientific test in ML). Likelihood it beats PTT per step at n<=20: ~25%; wall-clock: <5% [SPEC].

B2-03  Mixed visible-hidden coarse-grained groups for RBMs (method note turned idea)
  Payoff: methodological; fixes the trap in 0.3 for anyone porting P2 to bipartite models.
  Regime: any; most relevant MEDIUM/LOW.
  Mapping: group = k_v visible + k_h hidden with largest |W| block; effective fields from frozen remainder per P2. Symmetric if group
  selection is state-independent [P2].
  Energy cost: cheap. Spins: groups 8 to 20.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Same as B2-02.
  Best classical competitor: exact enumeration of the group conditional (2^20 = 1e6 states is classically tractable!), i.e. a block
  heat-bath move on the same group, which is the Peskun-better classical analogue of any proposal on that group [OWN].
  Implementation: compare quantum group proposal vs exact block heat-bath on the same group. If exact heat-bath wins (expected for
  k<=20), the quantum version is dead at these sizes.
  Break-even: classical exact group heat-bath costs ~2^k energy terms, ~1e6 at k=20 (ms on CPU) vs one QPU shot ~1 ms + latency: rough
  parity at k~20 to 25, quantum only plausible at k >~ 30 [OWN].
  Kill risk: exact block heat-bath dominates for k <= 25.
  Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY at k<=25; ~70% that exact heat-bath wins [SPEC].

B2-04  Hardware-native sparse deep Boltzmann machine (topology = heavy-hex or the device coupling graph)
  Payoff: co-designing the model to the QPU removes SWAP overhead; Niazi et al. showed sparse DBMs with ~30k parameters can be useful
  generative/classifier models [LIT arXiv:2303.10728].
  Regime: same trajectory as B2-01; sparse graphs have lower effective coordination, so transitions occur at stronger weights; trained
  sparse DBMs at T=1 likely MEDIUM [SPEC].
  Mapping: Ising on the device graph, one Trotter step = 3 colour layers of RZZ (heavy-hex is 3-edge-colourable); ~1.2n two-qubit
  gates per Trotter step [OWN]. Symmetric; exact accept.
  Energy cost: cheap. Spins: 100 to 156 (current Heron-class sizes) full system, no grouping needed.
  Mixing the bottleneck? For sparse DBMs trained with CD-n, Niazi et al. used up to 1e7 sweeps per update on FPGA [LIT 2303.10728],
  which suggests mixing matters, but they solved it with raw throughput.
  Best classical competitor: FPGA p-bits at 5e10 flips/s on the same sparse graph [LIT 2303.10728]; GPU graph-coloured Gibbs.
  Implementation: train small sparse DBM on device graph subset (n~50 to 100), QeMCMC step batched over B chains; compare per-sweep.
  Break-even: vs p-bits at 5e10 flips/s, a 100-spin sweep costs ~2 ns; QPU step ~1e-4 to 1e-3 s: S* ~1e5 to 1e6 [OWN].
  Kill risk: p-bit hardware is the purpose-built competitor and is 5 to 6 orders faster per sweep.
  Verdict: WAIT FOR HARDWARE; ~3% [SPEC].

B2-05  Replace CD-k (data-initialised, non-persistent) with one quantum step from the data
  Payoff: claimed less biased gradients than CD-1.
  Regime: early training HIGH; CD bias comes from short chains started at data, not from hard barriers.
  Mapping: joint (v,h) Ising; symmetric. Energy cheap. Spins: full.
  Mixing the bottleneck? No. CD bias is fixed by persistence (PCD) or more steps; the 2105.13889 analysis says short-k non-equilibrium
  training is a regime choice, not a sampler failure.
  Best classical competitor: PCD, CD-k with larger k on GPU (cheap).
  Implementation: none recommended.
  Break-even: unreachable; HIGH regime where uniform/local gaps are already near max [P1 Fig 2a, team pilot 1.29x at beta 0.1].
  Kill risk: certain.
  Verdict: KILL; ~95% confident.

B2-06  Unbiased "quantum-assisted" Boltzmann sampler: QeMCMC replaces annealer sampling in the D-Wave QA-RBM pipeline
  Payoff: the annealer pipelines (1510.06356, 1510.07611, 2005.03247) suffer from unknown effective temperature and biased samples; a
  Metropolis-corrected quantum proposal gives exact Boltzmann stationarity at any T without T_eff estimation [P1 property]. Clean story
  for "quantum-assisted training without temperature calibration".
  Regime: T=1 relative to the model; same trajectory as 0.1.
  Mapping: as B2-01. Detailed balance exact in ideal case; on hardware needs twirling/gauge (0.6).
  Energy cheap. Spins: as B2-01.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Partly; but Job and Adachi found simulated annealing matched the quantum-assisted result [LIT 2009.00134],
  suggesting the original gains were from better-than-CD sampling, which classical samplers also deliver.
  Best classical competitor: PCD/PT/SA with GPU throughput.
  Implementation: reproduce 2005.03247 bars-and-stripes setup with statevector QeMCMC vs PCD vs PT, matched wall-clock.
  Break-even: as B2-01.
  Kill risk: correctness is not the bottleneck; classical PCD is also asymptotically exact.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE only as a positioning paper (correction of annealer-bias literature); wall-clock KILL. ~10% useful [SPEC].

B2-07  Training quantum Boltzmann machines (transverse-field Gibbs models) with QeMCMC
  Payoff: none from QeMCMC. QBM [LIT 1601.02036] needs samples of a QUANTUM Gibbs state; QeMCMC samples a CLASSICAL Boltzmann
  distribution. The transverse field in QeMCMC is a proposal device, not part of the target.
  Regime: n/a. Mapping: mismatch. Energy: n/a.
  Best competitor: quantum Monte Carlo (path-integral) for stoquastic QBMs, or quantum Gibbs-state preparation algorithms.
  Kill risk: category error.
  Verdict: KILL; ~98%. Listed so nobody re-proposes it.

B2-08  Many-temperature ensembles with one temperature-independent proposal: AIS / log-likelihood estimation for RBMs
  Payoff: RBM practitioners get log-likelihood estimates (AIS needs a ladder of beta from 0 to 1, 1e3 to 1e4 intermediate distributions).
  QeMCMC's proposal does not depend on T [brief, P1], so one compiled circuit family serves the whole ladder; only the accept step
  changes. This is one of the brief's HIGH-T routes ("many-temperature ensembles").
  Regime: mostly HIGH (small beta) with a MEDIUM/LOW tail near beta=1; the hard part of AIS is the tail.
  Mapping: joint (v,h) or visible-only with hidden summed out (then energy is a sum of softplus terms, not 2-body, so H_prob must be a
  quadratic surrogate; still exact by symmetry [P1]). Energy cheap.
  Spins: full; or groups.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Partly: AIS variance explodes when intermediate chains fail to equilibrate near beta=1.
  Best classical competitor: AIS with block Gibbs; Bereux et al. online log-likelihood via annealing trajectory and PTT
  [LIT 2405.15376]; reverse AIS.
  Implementation: statevector study at n<=20 of AIS variance (log Z error) with quantum vs Gibbs transitions at equal step count;
  hardware-wise, compile per-chain circuits once (parameter binding only for init bits, gamma, t).
  Break-even: same throughput barrier (0.5); the T-independence saves compile time only, which is already small with parametric
  circuits [OWN].
  Kill risk: low-beta part has no advantage (P1 Fig 2a: uniform gap -> 1 at high T); high-beta part is B2-02 again.
  Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY / SIMULATE MORE as subtest of B2-02. ~10% [SPEC].

B2-09  Posterior sampling for Bayesian binary-weight neural networks (binary perceptron / small BNN)
  Payoff: calibrated uncertainty for binary-weight models (edge deployment, interpretability); posterior over w in {-1,+1}^n.
  Regime: LOW relative to the posterior landscape for the binary perceptron near capacity: typical solutions are isolated, with rare
  dense clusters [LIT-UNVERIFIED Huang and Kabashima PRE 2014; Baldassi et al. arXiv:1509.05753]. At moderate data/temperature it is
  MEDIUM. Posterior temperature 1 with a likelihood term over N data points.
  Mapping: energy = training loss(w) + prior, NOT quadratic in w in general (sign activations). For a squared-loss linear-threshold
  surrogate, E(w) ~ w^T (X^T X) w - 2 y^T X w is an exact dense Ising H_prob. Use that surrogate as H_prob and the true loss in the
  accept step: symmetry holds for ANY H_prob, so stationarity is exact [P1 property, OWN application].
  Energy cost per step: MEDIUM to EXPENSIVE: one pass over the dataset, O(N n); for N=1e5 to 1e6 on GPU ~1e-4 to 1e-3 s [OWN], i.e.
  comparable to t_shot, which pushes the per-step ratio toward 1 (brief break-even model).
  Spins needed: n = number of binary weights: 20 to 100 (perceptron), 1e3+ for real BNNs (groups needed: layer-wise blocks).
  Mixing the bottleneck? Yes for exact posterior sampling of the binary perceptron (glassy); no for practical BNN training (STE / SGD
  with latent weights does not need the posterior).
  Best classical competitor: DMALA / discrete Langevin, tested on binary NNs [LIT arXiv:2206.09914]; Gibbs-with-gradients
  [LIT arXiv:2102.04509]; locally balanced proposals [LIT arXiv:1711.07424, Zanella JASA 2020]; PT.
  Implementation: perceptron with n=16 to 24 binary weights, statevector QeMCMC with surrogate H_prob; measure ESS per energy
  evaluation (the honest currency when energy dominates) vs DMALA/GwG/PT; then scale data N to push t_energy up.
  Break-even: if t_energy >> t_propose for both, wall-clock speedup ~ tau_C/tau_Q per energy evaluation [brief model]; a quantum
  proposal also costs one energy evaluation, so the comparison is ESS per dataset pass. Batching across chains (B posterior chains) is
  natural.
  Kill risk: gradient-informed classical proposals use the same expensive pass and get many-coordinate moves for free; surrogate H_prob
  may be poor (low acceptance); real BNN sizes far beyond hardware.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE. ~20% that per-energy-evaluation ESS beats DMALA/PT at n<=24 [SPEC]; hardware relevance WAIT FOR HARDWARE.

B2-10  Corrector / refinement steps for discrete diffusion and masked generative models
  Payoff: better sample quality at the end of discrete diffusion via MCMC correction.
  Regime: HIGH relative (corrector operates at the model's temperature on near-data configurations, local moves suffice) [SPEC].
  Mapping: tokens are Potts variables with vocab q = 1e3 to 1e5 for text, 256 for pixels; one-hot or log2(q) binary encodings; energy
  is a transformer forward pass, not quadratic. Embedding cost prohibitive.
  Energy cost: EXPENSIVE (forward pass), which helps the ratio, but no Ising H_prob exists except as a crude surrogate.
  Spins: 1e3 to 1e6 binary variables.
  Mixing the bottleneck? No; model quality and step count dominate.
  Best classical competitor: predictor-corrector schemes for discrete diffusion [LIT-UNVERIFIED Campbell et al. arXiv:2205.14987];
  masked-conditional MH [LIT arXiv:2106.02736].
  Implementation: none. Exception worth one note: binary-latent diffusion models with small binary latent codes could use B2-16 instead.
  Kill risk: encoding size and absence of a usable H_prob.
  Verdict: KILL; ~90%.

B2-11  Constrained LLM decoding by MCMC with quantum proposals
  Payoff: unbiased sampling of grammar-constrained outputs (program fuzzing, structured generation).
  Regime: HIGH-ish (LM at T=1 over valid strings), but constraints create disconnected valid regions.
  Mapping: no natural Ising; Gonzalez et al. build proposals over valid outputs with MH accept on LM likelihood [LIT arXiv:2506.05754,
  NeurIPS 2025]; Goyal et al. MH over masked-LM energies [LIT arXiv:2106.02736]. A QeMCMC proposal over token bits would almost
  always land outside the grammar (rejected).
  Energy cost: EXPENSIVE (LM forward pass ~1e-2 to 1e-1 s [SPEC]), so per-step ratio near 1 is achievable; this is the only reason it
  is listed.
  Spins: 1e3+ binary. Mixing the bottleneck? The paper's point is that proposal validity is the bottleneck, not tunnelling.
  Best classical competitor: grammar-aware proposals (2506.05754), SMC-based constrained decoding.
  Implementation: none recommended; at most a quantum-inspired surrogate-Ising proposal over a binary choice structure (e.g. which
  constituents to resample).
  Kill risk: constraint violation makes quantum proposals near-always rejected.
  Verdict: KILL (QUANTUM-INSPIRED at most); ~90%.

B2-12  Energy-based reranking / diverse subset selection as a QUBO
  Payoff: sample diverse top-k subsets of N candidates (retrieval, LLM candidate reranking) with pairwise redundancy penalties.
  Regime: HIGH (users want diversity, soft distributions) [SPEC].
  Mapping: x_i in {0,1} chosen; E = -sum s_i x_i + lambda sum sim_ij x_i x_j + cardinality penalty: exact dense QUBO, n = N = 20 to 200.
  Energy cost: cheap once similarities are precomputed.
  Mixing the bottleneck? No; N small, HIGH T, swap moves mix quickly; exact DPP samplers exist for related kernels.
  Best classical competitor: swap-move MH, exact k-DPP sampling, greedy MAP.
  Break-even: S* ~1e6 to 1e9 (cheap energy, HIGH T) [brief].
  Kill risk: certain at HIGH T.
  Verdict: KILL; ~95%.

B2-13  Inverse Ising / Boltzmann learning for neural population data near criticality
  Payoff: neuroscience groups fitting pairwise maximum-entropy models to spike rasters (n = 40 to 150 neurons) need model averages at
  T=1 of a fitted model reported to sit near a critical point.
  Regime: MEDIUM [LIT-UNVERIFIED: Mora and Bialek "Are biological systems poised at criticality?" J Stat Phys 2011]. Fitted models are
  dense with both signs of J (frustrated), possibly glassy pockets.
  Mapping: dense SK-like Ising, n = 40 to 150. Embedding: all-to-all, ~n^2/2 couplings per Trotter step: 800 to 11,000 RZZ (0.4).
  Energy cost: cheap.
  Spins: full 40 to 150; groups of 7 to 12.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Only for exact Boltzmann-learning; pseudolikelihood and mean-field methods avoid sampling entirely
  [LIT-UNVERIFIED Aurell and Ekeberg arXiv:1107.3536] and are the standard.
  Best classical competitor: pseudolikelihood; Boltzmann learning with PT/Swendsen-Wang-like moves; GPU MCMC.
  Implementation: statevector QeMCMC vs PT on fitted 20-neuron models from public datasets; this matches the team pilot regime (SK-like,
  MEDIUM, beta near specific-heat peak gave 2.8x step ratio vs weak baselines [team pilot]).
  Break-even: cheap energy, S* ~1e3 to 1e5 batched (0.5); 2.8x per step is far below.
  Kill risk: pseudolikelihood makes sampling optional; PT handles MEDIUM.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (cheap, uses team pilot code) but expected outcome KILL for wall-clock; ~10% [SPEC].

B2-14  Bayesian network structure learning via MCMC over graphs
  Payoff: posterior over DAG structures.
  Regime: posterior can be multimodal (Markov-equivalence classes), MEDIUM/LOW [SPEC].
  Mapping: O'Gorman et al. QUBO with O(n^2) qubits plus acyclicity penalties [LIT arXiv:1407.3897]. Acyclicity is a hard constraint:
  QeMCMC proposals over edge bits mostly create cycles and are rejected.
  Energy cost: medium (decomposable score with caching is cheap).
  Spins: n(n-1) edge bits plus ordering auxiliaries: 100 to 1000+ for n=10 to 30 variables.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Yes classically for structure MCMC, but order-MCMC and partition-MCMC fix much of it [LIT-UNVERIFIED Friedman
  and Koller 2003; Kuipers and Moffa 2017].
  Best classical competitor: order MCMC, partition MCMC, exact DP for n <= ~25.
  Kill risk: hard constraint + exact DP at small n.
  Verdict: KILL; ~90%.

B2-15  Acquisition sampling in combinatorial Bayesian optimisation (BOCS-style)
  Payoff: BOCS uses a sparse second-order (quadratic) surrogate over binary inputs, i.e. an exact Ising/QUBO; its SA variant optimises
  the Thompson-sampled acquisition by simulated annealing [LIT arXiv:1806.08838, ICML 2018].
  Regime: optimisation (LOW, T -> 0) of a small (n = 10 to 60) dense QUBO per BO iteration.
  Mapping: exact QUBO; n small enough for hardware at the low end.
  Energy cost: cheap (acquisition); the expensive black-box evaluation sits OUTSIDE the MCMC loop.
  Mixing the bottleneck? No. Per BO iteration the cost is dominated by the black-box experiment (minutes to days); acquisition
  optimisation is seconds of SA and does not need to be exact.
  Best classical competitor: SA, SDP relaxation (BOCS-SDP), exhaustive search at n<=25.
  Kill risk: improving the acquisition step does not change BO wall-clock.
  Verdict: KILL; ~90%.

B2-16  Latent Boltzmann prior in discrete VAEs (DVAE / DVAE++ / QVAE)
  Payoff: discrete-latent generative models with an RBM prior over binary latents [LIT arXiv:1609.02200 Rolfe; arXiv:1802.04920
  DVAE++]; the QVAE variant used a QBM prior [LIT, via citation in arXiv:1805.07349; Khoshaman et al. arXiv:1802.05779 not directly
  verified]. The prior RBM is small (tens to a few hundred latent units), a plausible hardware size at the low end.
  Regime: priors are typically fitted to aggregated posteriors that are clustered, so LOW-like late in training is possible [SPEC];
  most of training HIGH/MEDIUM.
  Mapping: bipartite RBM prior, 2 x 32 to 2 x 128 units; dense (0.4) -> hardware only at ~8x8.
  Energy cost: cheap (prior energy); encoder/decoder costs are outside the sampler.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Probably not: prior negative-phase quality is one of several gradient noise sources; encoder/decoder gradient
  variance and relaxation bias dominate [SPEC].
  Best classical competitor: PCD on GPU, PT; same as B2-01.
  Implementation: small DVAE on binarised MNIST with 16+16 prior; swap PCD for statevector QeMCMC; measure ELBO/test LL at equal steps.
  Break-even: as B2-01.
  Kill risk: prior sampling is not binding.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE only as a low-priority side experiment; ~5% [SPEC].

B2-17  Low-T design sampling from trained biological RBMs (protein sequence design)
  Payoff: generating high-fitness novel sequences by sampling a trained protein RBM at T < 1 [LIT arXiv:1803.08718, Tubiana, Cocco,
  Monasson, eLife 2019 (design use is described there)].
  Regime: LOW by intent (biasing toward high-probability sequences); genuinely glassy.
  Mapping: Potts visible units q=21 per residue position, L=50 to 600 positions; one-hot (21 qubits per site) or 5-bit encoding with
  penalty terms. Hidden units 100 to 1000. Embedding cost very large.
  Energy cost: cheap.
  Spins: 1e3 to 1e4 binary.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Yes at low T (that is why design is hard), but the design goal is good sequences, not equilibrium; SA/greedy
  suffice for that.
  Best classical competitor: SA, PT, Gibbs with hidden-unit conditioning (sample h first, then v).
  Kill risk: encoding size; design does not need exact sampling.
  Verdict: WAIT FOR HARDWARE (far); ~2%.

B2-18  Neural-network quantum states: sampling |psi(s)|^2 of RBM/NN wavefunctions in VMC
  Payoff: VMC practitioners get decorrelated configurations in ordered/frustrated phases where local Metropolis sticks.
  Regime: |psi|^2 for ordered phases is multimodal (symmetry-broken sectors), LOW-like; paramagnetic phases HIGH.
  Mapping: log|psi|^2 for an RBM ansatz = sum of log-cosh terms, not 2-body; use the physical Hamiltonian's classical part or a fitted
  quadratic surrogate as H_prob; exact by symmetry [P1 property]. Prior art does precisely this: QeMCMC adapted to NN wavefunction VMC
  [LIT arXiv:2502.20799]; quantum-enabled VMC for NQS reported shorter chain convergence [LIT arXiv:2412.12398].
  Energy cost per step: MEDIUM to EXPENSIVE: each local-energy estimate needs psi on all connected configurations (O(n) to O(n^2) NN
  evaluations), and amplitude ratios need a network forward pass [OWN]. This improves the per-step ratio.
  Spins: 16 to 100 sites, full or grouped.
  Mixing the bottleneck? In symmetry-broken phases yes, but symmetry-projected ansatze or global symmetry moves (flip all spins) solve
  the simplest case classically; autoregressive NQS give exact independent samples with no MCMC at all.
  Best classical competitor: autoregressive NQS (exact sampling), cluster/global moves, PT over wavefunction "temperature".
  Implementation: reproduce 2502.20799 at n<=16 then add the classical global-move and autoregressive baselines that a reviewer will ask
  for; measure ESS per local-energy evaluation.
  Break-even: per-step ratio closer to 1 than RBM training thanks to expensive local energies, but hardware n is small.
  Kill risk: prior art already exists (novelty), autoregressive models bypass MCMC.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (mostly as replication + stronger baselines). ~15% [SPEC].

B2-19  Quantum moves as an equilibration diagnostic for persistent chains
  Payoff: detect when PCD chains are out of equilibrium (2105.13889) by testing whether long-range proposals from fantasy particles find
  lower-free-energy unseen modes. Needs few calls (a diagnostic, not a sampler), so latency hardly matters.
  Regime: MEDIUM/LOW late training.
  Mapping: as B2-01/B2-02. Energy cheap. Spins: as B2-02.
  Mixing the bottleneck? This idea measures it rather than fixes it.
  Best classical competitor: data-initialised vs random-initialised chain comparison, PTT swap statistics, tethered free-energy
  profile [LIT 2206.01310]. These are cheap and effective.
  Kill risk: classical diagnostics already do this.
  Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY; ~5%.

PART 3. STRONGEST CANDIDATES AND WHY

1. B2-02 (mode-hopping in coexistence). The only ML setting where T=1 is genuinely LOW relative to the model, where the literature
   documents mixing as the binding constraint (2206.01310, 2405.15376) and where temperature-PT is reported to struggle. A clean,
   falsifiable exact-simulation test at n<=20 against PTT and tethered MC; a negative result is publishable as a kill.
2. B2-09 (binary-weight posterior). Combines three properties the brief says a quantum case needs: expensive energy (dataset pass ~
   t_shot, so per-step ratio approaches 1), a glassy LOW-T posterior, and P1's "any H_prob is exact" property, which lets a quadratic
   surrogate drive the proposal while the true loss gates acceptance. Honest currency: ESS per dataset pass vs DMALA/GwG/PT.
3. B2-18 (NQS VMC). Expensive local energies again shrink t_C/t_Q, and QeMCMC-for-VMC prior art (2502.20799, 2412.12398) shows the
   community is already testing it, so a hard-nosed replication with the missing baselines (global flips, autoregressive exact
   sampling) has clear value. Novelty risk is the main weakness.

Overall verdict for the ML domain: no BUILD NOW candidate. RBM energies are cheap, classical block Gibbs samples half the system
exactly per step, GPU/p-bit throughput is 1e10 to 1e11 spin updates/s, and dense bipartite embedding blows the gate budget beyond ~8x8.
Batching many independent chains amortises QPU latency (B >~ 1e3) but leaves a break-even of ~1e3 to 1e5 classical sweeps per quantum
proposal. Any quantum case in ML must come from expensive energies (B2-09, B2-18) or genuine coexistence (B2-02).
