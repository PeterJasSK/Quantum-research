

5. IMPLEMENTATION VARIANTS (B7 inventor, tested in D1-D9, attacked in E1-E3)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
General results used below [CALC + SIM, B7 G1-G3, D3, E2]:
G1 Any real diagonal H_prob (true energy, surrogate, native-coupling subset), any real symmetric mixer (sum X, XY hopping)
   and a palindromic schedule give U^T = U, so Q is symmetric and Metropolis with the TRUE energy is exact.
G2 Unital Pauli noise with a time-symmetric profile keeps Q exactly symmetric (D3: asymmetry ~5e-16 for depolarizing and
   dephasing in a palindromic circuit). T1, leakage, readout error and non-palindromic compilation break it.
G3 Q(s'|s) cannot be computed or estimated beyond toy n, so any correction needing Q values (Hastings for an asymmetric
   kernel) is infeasible at scale.
G4 Block bound [CALC D7, proof checked by red team E2, sharp]: with a state-independent random choice of group, ANY
   Metropolis group proposal (quantum or not) has spectral gap <= 2x that of exact block heat-bath on the same group.
   Coarse-grained QeMCMC can never beat classical block Gibbs by more than 2x per step; measured 0.11-0.35x at SK n=10.
   Caveat: fixed-order sweeps are not covered (a classical Metropolized-Gibbs sweep reached 2.24x, E2).

7. TEMPERATURE MAP
------------------
HIGH. Applications: matched-temperature Bayesian posteriors at typical loads (telecom detection, imaging, genomics:
Nishimori line), data-fitted health/ecology Ising models, robust-design sampling, early RBM training. Best classical:
single-spin Metropolis / Gibbs (mixes in a few sweeps). Quantum: no step advantage in any family (D1: 0.90 vs best
mixture on SK n=10, decohered control matches coherent on lattices; D2 1.1-3.5 flips per quantum step). S > S*: never.

MEDIUM. Applications: MRF segmentation, ordering transitions in alloys and battery cathodes, RBMs at mode-forming
transitions, design ensembles. Best classical: Swendsen-Wang/Wolff where unfrustrated, PT, canonical swaps.
Quantum: vs weak baselines 3-8x (SK), but at parity with exact cluster moves on SK n=8-11 (S 0.94-1.09; clusters win
below beta_c, QeMCMC 2.3-4x ahead at 1.47 beta_c; E1), 0.4-0.6 on 2D EA and ~0.03 on ferromagnets (D1); vs tuned PT one
quantum step is worth 3.7-5.9 flips (D2). S > S*: never (S* ~1e8).

LOW. Applications: spin-glass ground-state-near sampling, LABS, coexistence in RBMs, cold sampling of overloaded
inference. Best classical: PT + Houdayer/ICM, population annealing, structure-aware proposals. Quantum: the only regime
with a real step gain: 10-25x vs best mixture (D1, SK n=10), 8-18 flips per step vs tuned PT (D2, n=12-14), k 0.35 vs
0.82 (D1), but 0.62-1.06 vs exact 2-3-spin heat-bath at n=6-8 (E3), and gone on FakeMarrakesh. S > S*: never at
reachable n (S* ~1e8 vs S ~10).

Regime | Typical applications | Best classical | Quantum S > 1 vs best classical? | S > S*?
HIGH | matched posteriors, fitted data Ising, early RBMs | Metropolis/Gibbs | no (0.9-1.3) | no
MEDIUM | MRF, alloy/cathode ordering, design ensembles | SW/Wolff, PT, swaps | parity vs clusters on SK (0.94-1.09); 3.7-5.9 flip-equivalents vs PT | no (S* ~1e8)
LOW | spin glasses, LABS, RBM coexistence | PT+ICM, population annealing | yes per step (8-25 at n=10-14), small-n block heat-bath ties | no (S ~10 vs S* ~1e8)

10. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
---------------------
1. 144 ideas (24 KNOWN): 128 KILL, 7 SIMULATE MORE, 5 QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY, 3 BUILD NOW (calibration), 1 WAIT; best wall-clock 0.5%.
2. HIGH T: no quantum step advantage anywhere. MEDIUM T: parity with exact cluster moves on SK, losses on lattices.
3. LOW T keeps a real step advantage (8-25x vs PT or best mixtures at n=10-14, ideal), consistent with P1.
4. A quantum step is worth 1-20 classical flips (D2); break-even ~1e8 on IBM (2-3 s vs ~30 ns); optimal PT fraction is zero.
5. Surrogate in U with the true energy in the accept is exact but beaten at n<=12 by classical delayed acceptance.
6. Coarse-grained QeMCMC is capped at 2x classical block Gibbs per step (proved, sharp); measured 0.11-0.35x.
7. Matched-temperature Bayesian inference sits on the Nishimori line: no glass phase, no advantage.
8. Depolarizing slows without bias; T1/readout/non-palindromic compilation bias (twirl + palindrome fix); Heron noise kills n=6 gain.
9. Useful now: the calibration study (K0/B3-04) and the correctness fixes (V02, V12) for anyone running QeMCMC.
10. Biggest single risk: the LOW-T gain needs ~100x deeper circuits than today's fidelity allows; it may wait for fault tolerance.
