A4 HARDWARE SCOUT REPORT -- QeMCMC on real quantum hardware
Date: 2026-11-20. Author: sub-agent A4 (hardware scout).
Scope: per-device numbers for running a SEQUENTIAL quantum-enhanced MCMC chain
(prepare |s> -> exp(-iHt) -> measure 1..few shots -> classical accept/reject -> next step).

CONVENTIONS
- Every number has a URL. "not found" = I searched and found no primary/secondary figure.
- "ESTIMATE:" marks my own inference. It is never a sourced number.
- "derived:" = arithmetic on sourced numbers. The inputs are cited.
- The main bottleneck for sequential QeMCMC is the per-JOB (per-task, per-QMI) round trip.
  Shot time is almost never the bottleneck. A step needs a new input state |s>, and on almost
  every cloud stack that means a new job, unless the accept/reject runs inside the device's
  real-time controller (see Helios and the IBM dynamic-circuits notes).

======================================================================================
0. KEY CROSS-CUTTING FACTS (read first)
======================================================================================
F1. IBM bills a fixed overhead of about 2 s per sub-job ("includes operations such as loading
    the payload into control electronics"). The quick estimate is 2 + 0.00035*<num executions> s.
    So one IBM job per MCMC step costs at least about 2 s of QPU-locked time, before any
    network or client latency.
    https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/estimate-job-run-time
F2. Sessions are billed by wall clock, including the idle interactive-TTL gap between jobs
    while your CPU does the accept/reject.
    https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/estimate-job-run-time
    https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/execution-modes-faq
F3. Braket charges per task ($0.30) plus per shot, on every gate/analog QPU. A 1-shot step
    therefore costs about $0.30, and the per-task fee dominates.
    https://aws.amazon.com/braket/pricing/
F4. A D-Wave QMI with a tiny problem in the official example reports qpu_access_time =
    16,016 us. Of that, qpu_programming_time = 15,935 us (including about 1 ms
    post-programming thermalization), anneal = 20 us/sample, readout = 39.76 us/sample, and
    delay = 21 us/sample. There is also a separate unbilled qpu_access_overhead_time of
    10,427 us. Internet latency and SAPI service time come on top and are not published.
    https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/quantum_research/operation_timing.html
F5. Quantinuum Helios offers "real-time integer and floating point arithmetic, real-time
    boolean logic, real-time arbitrary control flow", mid-circuit measurement, qubit reuse,
    and an RNG capability. In principle a whole QeMCMC chain (measure -> compute dE ->
    draw a uniform -> conditionally re-prepare |s'>) can run inside ONE program, with no
    network round trip per step. It is the only platform found where that is documented as
    supported.
    https://docs.quantinuum.com/systems/_static/assets/data_sheets/Quantinuum%20Helios%20Product%20Data%20Sheet.pdf
    https://docs.quantinuum.com/systems/user_guide/hardware_user_guide/helios.html
    Limit: 10,000 shots and 500,000 HQC per job (same helios.html page).

1.1 IBM Heron r2 (e.g. ibm_fez, ibm_marrakesh, ibm_kingston*)  [superconducting]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Qubits: 156, heavy-hex lattice.
  https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/processor-types
- Median 2q (CZ) error: published values vary by device and date.
  * 0.175% median CZ, plus SX 2.22e-4, on ibm_basquecountry (Heron r2). Source: arXiv paper
    surfaced by search; exact paper not pinned. The family figure is "median CZ error
    reduced from ~5e-3 to ~3e-3 by 2024 calibration updates" (same search).
  * A third-party reprint of IBM's dashboard gives Heron r2: 2Q median 2.03e-3,
    readout median 1.666e-2, CLOPS 340K. Undated.
    https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/computers (the live page is the primary source; values change daily)
- EPLG (100-qubit chain): ibm_marrakesh 3.98e-3 and ibm_kingston 9.93e-4 "at execution time"
  (per the paper cited in search results, arXiv 2510.16915 context).
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.16915
  [*ibm_kingston is labelled r2 in one paper and r3 in a Zenodo record. Treat it as uncertain.]
- Readout error: about 1-2% median (1.666e-2 per the reprint above). Not pinned to a primary source.
- T1: not found as a fleet median for r2. Nighthawk r1 is "highest coherence of our fleet,
  median T1 ~350 us", which implies Heron is lower.
  https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/01/13/ibm-announces-nighthawk-and-latest-heron-are-now-available/
- Usable depth: Marshall et al. 2026 ran QeMCMC proposals on ibm_boston (Heron r3) with up to
  5,532 2q gates / 2q-depth 228 (52-node MIS). The noise needed an estimated 4.28M shots/iter
  and they used 100,000.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06171v3 (Table 4)
  Layden 2023 used up to 48 layers of 2q gates on 10 spins (ibmq_mumbai, 27q, older generation).
  https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12497
- Shot rate: default rep_delay 250 us on most backends.
  https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/estimate-job-run-time
  Heron runs about 4,000 circuits/s. https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/nighthawk-r2
- Job latency: about 2 s per sub-job overhead (F1). Queue: not published; varies from seconds
  (Premium/Flex/dedicated, inside a session) to hours (Open plan).
  ESTIMATE: in-session 1-shot round trip = 2-4 s.
- Dynamic circuits: mid-circuit measurement plus feedforward, with feedforward latency about
  600 ns per IBM. https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/utility-scale-dynamic-circuits
  Classical logic is limited to bit conditionals and simple expressions. Computing an Ising dE
  and comparing it to a random threshold in real time is NOT documented as supported.
  (not found)
- Price: Pay-As-You-Go $96/min, Flex $72/min (min 400 min), Premium $48/min (min 5,200 min),
  Open plan free with 10 min/month.
  https://www.ibm.com/quantum/pricing
  derived: at 2-3 s/step on PAYG, one sequential step costs about $3.2-$4.8.

1.3 IBM Nighthawk r1 / r2 (ibm_miami = r1, ibm_phoenix = r2)  [superconducting]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Qubits: 120 on a square lattice with 218 tunable couplers. r2 adds 120 reset elements
  (458 physical elements in total).
  https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/01/13/ibm-announces-nighthawk-and-latest-heron-are-now-available/
  https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ibm-announces-availability-of-its-nighthawk-r2-quantum-processor/
- Median T1 (r1): about 350 us (TQI link above). Effective T1 during dissipative reset on r2:
  about 25 ns, versus about 200 us normally. https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/nighthawk-r2
- 2q error: "Heron-class gate fidelity" (r2 blog). No separate median found.
- Depth: up to 5,000 2q gates (r1 claim). r2 "demonstrated accurate observable estimation on
  circuits containing more than 7,500 gates" (with PEA).
  https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ibm-announces-availability-of-its-nighthawk-r2-quantum-processor/
- Shot rate: r2 runs more than 100,000 circuits/s, 25x Heron's ~4,000/s. Idle between shots
  can be as low as about 1 us. https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/nighthawk-r2
  r1 (exploratory): rep time raised to 4 ms. Dynamic circuits were NOT supported at launch.
  https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/01/13/ibm-announces-nighthawk-and-latest-heron-are-now-available/
- Relevance to QeMCMC: the faster shot rate does not help a 1-shot sequential step, because
  the about 2 s per-job overhead (F1) dominates. It helps a lot for batched independent
  chains (many PUBs/circuits per job).
- Price and latency: same as Heron (IBM plans).

1.6 Quantinuum Helios  [trapped ion, QCCD, 137Ba+]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Qubits: 98, all-to-all. Eight gate zones (four for 2q gates). T2 is about seconds.
- 2q infidelity: 8e-4 typical (2e-3 max). 1q: 0.3e-4. SPAM: 5e-4. Memory per depth-1: 6e-4.
  https://docs.quantinuum.com/systems/_static/assets/data_sheets/Quantinuum%20Helios%20Product%20Data%20Sheet.pdf
  Paper: 99.921% average 2q fidelity. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05465
- Speed: depth-1 time (random pairing plus 1q/2q gates on all qubits) averages 55 ms per layer.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05465
  derived: an all-to-all SK Trotter step on n spins needs about n-1 depth-1 layers. For n=20
  that is about 1 s per Trotter step, so a shot with r Trotter steps takes about r seconds.
  Sparse H is much faster.
- Real-time control: arbitrary control flow, float/int arithmetic, RNG, MCMR, and qubit reuse
  (F5). A whole QeMCMC chain can be a single Guppy program, so the per-step cost is just the
  circuit time. The job cap is 10,000 shots and 500,000 HQC.
  https://docs.quantinuum.com/systems/user_guide/hardware_user_guide/helios.html
- Logical: 48 error-corrected logical qubits at 2:1 encoding (vendor claim).
  https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/11/06/illuminating-helios-quantinuums-shiny-new-quantum-computer-gets-sunny-reception/
- Price: not found (contract/HQC). Queue: not found.
- Successor Sol (2027) is planned to run about 2x faster than Helios.
  https://www.hpcwire.com/2025/11/05/quantinuum-introduces-helios-quantum-system-as-roadmap-advances-toward-apollo/

1.8 QuEra Aquila  [neutral atom, ANALOG Rydberg, 87Rb]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Qubits: up to 256 atoms in user-defined 1D/2D geometry. Rows must be at least 4 um apart.
  https://aws.amazon.com/braket/quantum-computers/quera
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.11727 (Aquila white paper)
- Hamiltonian: Rydberg H = global Rabi drive (X) + global detuning (Z) + 1/r^6 Rydberg-Rydberg
  ZZ. That is an Ising + transverse + longitudinal field form, but the couplings are fixed by
  geometry (positive, unit-disk-like). Arbitrary J (SK spin glass) cannot be encoded natively.
- Limits: max evolution time 4 us, max Rabi amplitude 2.51 MHz, max detuning 19.89 MHz
  (at time of that post). https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/quantum-computing/analog-hamiltonian-simulation-with-pennylane
- Shot rate: "typically < 10 samples per second". The full array is reloaded every shot, and
  the cycle repeats "up to 10 times per second". https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.11727
- Preparing a computational basis state |s>: atoms always start in |g...g>. Local detuning
  (a static site pattern with time-dependent magnitude) can be used to "prepare a
  non-equilibrium starting state". It is available through Braket Direct experimental
  capabilities only, and it adds decoherence even at zero magnitude.
  https://docs.aws.amazon.com/braket/latest/developerguide/braket-access-local-detuning.html
  https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/quantum-computing/local-detuning-now-available-on-queras-aquila-device-with-braket-direct
  => every MCMC step with a new |s> is a NEW TASK (new local-detuning pattern).
- Availability window: 48 h/week (2023 figure, up from 10). Current window: not found.
  https://thequantuminsider.com/2023/08/29/quera-quadruples-availability-of-its-quantum-computer-on-amazon-braket/
- Price: $0.30/task + $0.01/shot. Reservation $2,500/h. https://aws.amazon.com/braket/pricing/
- Per-task overhead (compile, SLM phase-mask update, rearrangement, queue): not found.
  ESTIMATE: at least several seconds per task even when reserved.

1.13 D-Wave Advantage (Pegasus) / Advantage2 (Zephyr)  [quantum annealer]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Advantage2: 4,400+ qubits, 40,000+ couplers, Zephyr with 20-way connectivity (Advantage
  had 15). 40% higher energy scale, 75% lower noise, 2x coherence. GA 20 May 2025.
  https://support.dwavesys.com/hc/en-us/articles/32105885880087
  https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/d-wave-announces-general-availability-advantage2-quantum-computer-its-most-advanced
  Example solver sizes: Advantage_system4.1 5,627q, Advantage2_system1.9 4,590q.
  Max anneal 2,000 us. https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19685v1 (Table 1)
- Hamiltonian: H = -A(s)/2 sum X + B(s)/2 (g(t) sum h Z + sum J ZZ). This is a native
  Ising + transverse field, which is exactly the QeMCMC proposal Hamiltonian family.
  https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/quantum_research/solver_parameters.html
- Analog controls: anneal_schedule (piecewise linear, 0.01 us granularity) with pause and
  quench; reverse anneal from a given initial_state; reinitialize_state True/False;
  h_gain_schedule; fast_anneal (down to about ns-scale, but it cannot be combined with
  reverse anneal or h).
  https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/quantum_research/solver_parameters.html
  * reinitialize_state=False: each read starts from the previous read's final state, which
    gives an on-QPU chain with about 10 us extra per read. But there is no classical MH
    accept/reject between reads, so it does not sample the Boltzmann target exactly.
  * A QeMCMC step with a classical accept/reject needs a NEW QMI (new initial_state), which
    costs about 16 ms of QPU access plus SAPI, network and queue.
- Per-QMI timing (example on the docs page): programming 15.9 ms, anneal 20 us/sample,
  readout 39.8 us/sample, delay 21 us/sample. Unbilled overhead 10.4 ms, "roughly 10-20 ms".
  https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/quantum_research/operation_timing.html
- End-to-end: D-Wave advertises "QPUs with subsecond response times" on Leap.
  https://www.dwavequantum.com/solutions-and-products/cloud-platform/
  Measured round-trip numbers: not found.
- Batching: parallel disjoint embeddings (146-178 copies of a small native Ising) in one QMI.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19685v1
  => 100+ independent chains can advance per QMI for small problems.
- Price: no public per-minute list price found. Historical $2,000/h (2018).
  https://spectrum.ieee.org/dwave-launches-free-quantum-cloud-service
  AWS Marketplace 12-month Leap contract: $70,000.
  https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-swm25ss53l2ss
  Trial: 1 min QPU. https://dwavesys.com/take-leap

3. PUBLISHED WALL-CLOCK MEASUREMENTS OF QeMCMC / ITERATIVE LOOPS ON HARDWARE
======================================================================================
3.1 Layden et al., Nature 619, 282 (2023), arXiv:2203.12497. Hardware: ibmq_mumbai (27q),
    n = 10 spins, up to 48 layers of pulse-efficient 2q gates.
    They did NOT run a sequential chain. They "recorded quantum transitions between
    computational states with s uniformly distributed" to estimate the transition matrix and
    spectral gap offline.
    https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12497
    Wall-clock per step: not reported / not applicable.
3.2 Marshall, Egger, Garn, Schiavello, Brandhofer, Zoufal, Woerner, arXiv:2602.06171 (v3,
    17 Nov 2026). QeMCMC with parallel tempering (5 replicas) on ibm_aachen and ibm_boston
    (Heron r2/r3), 17-117 node MIS, using Qiskit Runtime Session mode with parameter
    streaming. Wall-clock "includes quantum execution and classical processing between
    iterations, but excludes queue".
    https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06171v3 (Table 4, Table 6, Appendix G)
    - 17-node: median 32 iterations at 5 shots/iter. t_solution,best = 1.63 min.
      derived: about 3 s per iteration (one iteration = one job advancing 5 replica chains).
    - 117-node: 10,000 shots/iter, 151 iterations to optimum on ibm_boston.
      t_solution,best = 76.88 min. derived: about 30 s per iteration.
    - t_trial (estimated full trial to the 200,000-iteration cap): 5.35 min (17-node) to
      170 min (117-node). TTS95 = 680 min for 117 nodes, versus CPLEX 1.06 s.
    Caveat: the derived per-iteration times divide best-run minutes by the median iteration
    count, so they are approximate.
3.3 D-Wave reverse-anneal chains: Le et al., arXiv:2512.19685, "Monte Carlo chains of reverse
    quantum anneals" on Advantage_system4.1/6.4 and Advantage2_system1.9 for
    partition-function estimation.
    https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19685v1
    These chains run on-QPU (reinitialize_state=False), so there is no per-step classical
    round trip. Per-step wall-clock: not reported in what I read.
3.4 Arai, Kadowaki et al., "Quantum Annealing Enhanced MCMC", arXiv:2502.08060: theory and
    simulation (spectral gap on SK). No hardware wall-clock found.
    https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08060v1
3.5 IBM feedforward latency (in-circuit, not job-level): about 600 ns.
    https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/utility-scale-dynamic-circuits
3.6 No published QeMCMC on neutral-atom (Aquila/Pasqal) or trapped-ion hardware was found.

======================================================================================
5. PER-STEP LATENCY FOR A SEQUENTIAL QeMCMC LOOP (one chain, one step = one new |s>)
   optimistic / realistic / pessimistic, by device class
======================================================================================
(a) IBM gate-based (Heron r2/r3, Nighthawk), one Runtime job per step inside a Session
    - Optimistic: about 2 s per step.
      Floor = the documented about 2 s per sub-job overhead; shot time (<1 ms) is negligible.
      https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/estimate-job-run-time
    - Realistic: about 3-5 s per step.
      Marshall et al. derived about 3 s per iteration (17-node, 5 shots, session plus
      parameter streaming, queue excluded); about 30 s per iteration at 10,000 shots.
      https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06171v3
    - Pessimistic: minutes to hours per step (no session or dedicated plan, so each job
      re-queues). ESTIMATE; queue times are not published.
    - Cost: $96/min PAYG, so about $3-5 per step. 10^4 steps cost about $30k-50k.
      https://www.ibm.com/quantum/pricing
    - Escape hatches:
      (i) Batch K independent chains per job. The cost is still about 2 s per job, so the
          effective time is about 2/K s per chain-step. Nighthawk r2 at >100k circuits/s
          makes K ~ 10^3-10^4 cheap.
      (ii) In-circuit MCMC via dynamic circuits (feedforward ~600 ns) would cut a step to
          about 10-100 us. But real-time Ising dE computation plus random comparison is NOT
          documented on IBM: not found.
(b) Trapped ion
    - Helios, whole chain in one real-time program (F5):
      * Optimistic: about 0.1-0.5 s per step for sparse or small H. derived from 55 ms per
        depth-1 layer x a few layers. https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05465
      * Realistic: about 1-5 s per step for all-to-all n~20-50 with 1-3 Trotter steps.
        derived: (n-1) layers x 55 ms per Trotter step.
      * Capped at 10,000 shots and 500,000 HQC per job.
        https://docs.quantinuum.com/systems/user_guide/hardware_user_guide/helios.html
    - One job per step (H2/Helios/IonQ via cloud):
      * Realistic: tens of seconds to minutes per step. ESTIMATE; per-job latency not found.
      * Pessimistic: hours (shared queue).
      * IonQ Forte shot time is about 0.1 s per 100 2q gates (970 us per 2q gate,
        https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03801). Braket costs $0.38 per 1-shot step.
        https://aws.amazon.com/braket/pricing/
(c) Neutral-atom analog (QuEra Aquila, Pasqal Orion)
    - Every new |s> needs a new task with a new local-detuning pattern (Aquila, Braket Direct
      only) or a new register/pulse (Pasqal).
    - Optimistic: about 1-10 s per step on a reserved Aquila. ESTIMATE; per-task overhead
      not found. The physics floor is 0.1 s per shot (<10 Hz).
      https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.11727
    - Realistic: about 1 min per step. ESTIMATE (Braket task submission + compile + queue
      priority under reservation).
    - Pessimistic: hours to days. Outside the availability window (48 h/week in 2023) or on
      the shared queue.
      https://thequantuminsider.com/2023/08/29/quera-quadruples-availability-of-its-quantum-computer-on-amazon-braket/
    - Pasqal floor: at least 4 s per shot (0.25 Hz, billing 1 shot = 4 s).
      https://docs.pasqal.com/cloud/billing/
    - Caveat: the native couplings are geometric 1/r^6, so arbitrary-J Ising is not native.
(d) D-Wave annealer (Advantage / Advantage2), one QMI per step (reverse anneal from s, 1 read)
    - Optimistic: about 30-50 ms per step. derived: about 16 ms QPU access (programming
      dominated) + 10-20 ms unbilled overhead, from a client co-located near the SAPI
      endpoint. https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/quantum_research/operation_timing.html
    - Realistic: about 0.2-1 s per step. Leap advertises "subsecond response times"
      (https://www.dwavequantum.com/solutions-and-products/cloud-platform/); internet + SAPI
      service time is not published, and the range is an ESTIMATE.
    - Pessimistic: a few seconds or more, under contention on the shared QPU. ESTIMATE.
    - Batching: 100+ parallel embeddings per QMI (https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19685v1), so
      the effective time drops to about 1-10 ms per chain-step for small native instances.
    - An on-QPU chain with reinitialize_state=False costs about 0.1 ms per step but has no
      MH correction (biased).
      https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/quantum_research/solver_parameters.html

BOTTOM LINE
- For a single sequential chain on today's clouds, D-Wave (about 0.03-1 s per step) is
  1-2 orders of magnitude faster per step than IBM (about 2-5 s per step), which in turn
  beats analog neutral atoms (seconds to minutes per step).
- The only gate-model route to sub-second steps is running the whole MH loop inside the
  device's real-time controller. That is documented on Quantinuum Helios (and H2: control
  flow plus RNG); on IBM it is not shown for arithmetic.
- For any device, batching many independent chains per job is the practical way to amortize
  the per-job floor.
