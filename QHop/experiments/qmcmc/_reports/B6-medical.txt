PROJECT CONTEXT THAT SHAPES THIS REPORT
- qh-17 "quantum-proposed tumour" (QHop/plans/study-idea-qh-17-quantum-proposed-tumour-CLOSED.md) is CLOSED.
  Its own bear case was "fitted genotype Ising is weakly frustrated, local Metropolis mixes fine at n <= 40".
  On 2026-11-19 quantum was dropped from the lung synthetic-tumour work because no genuine quantum point
  was found. The KNOWN items K8-K14 below reproduce that diagnosis item by item: most data-fitted
  health Ising models are HIGH-T or tiny (enumerable). Ideas that survive need either a physics-defined
  frustrated energy (not a weakly-coupled data fit) or an expensive energy.
- QeMCMC detailed balance, used throughout: ideal proposal Q(s'|s) is symmetric for ANY real
  symmetric H_prob [P1], so Metropolis with the true energy gives exact Boltzmann stationarity even if
  H_prob is a crude surrogate. Single random coarse-grained group: symmetric [P2; A2 report]. Fixed-order
  multi-group sweep: not symmetric in general (A2 report) -> use random order or per-group accept.
  Hardware noise breaks exact symmetry -> small bias unless twirled [P1 SI]. Binary encoding of
  q-state Potts labels: assign infinite energy to unused codes; proposal stays symmetric over the
  full 2^m register, so balance holds, at an acceptance cost [own].

GENERAL BREAK-EVEN ARITHMETIC USED BELOW [own, using A4 numbers]
  S* = t_Q / t_C. Cheap energies (incremental delta-E in ns-us): t_C ~ 1e-8 to 1e-6 s, t_Q ~ 2-5 s
  (IBM, sequential) -> S* ~ 1e6 to 1e9. No idea with a cheap energy can break even this decade on
  sequential cloud steps; batching K=1e3-1e4 chains per job [A4] lowers S* by K but classical chains
  parallelise just as well on GPU.
  Expensive energy t_E: S* = (t_lat + t_E)/(t_propC + t_E).
    t_E = 10 s, t_lat = 3 s -> S* = 1.3. t_E = 1 s -> S* = 4. t_E = 0.1 s -> S* = 31. t_E = 1 ms -> 3000.
  BUT for expensive energies the right classical competitor is NOT local/uniform: it is a surrogate-driven
  proposal (delayed acceptance, Christen & Fox 2005, J. Comput. Graph. Stat. 14(4) [lit-m]) that runs a
  long cheap classical chain on the same surrogate H_prob and proposes its end state. That costs
  ~ms classically and gives an almost independent draw from the surrogate Boltzmann law. So in the
  expensive-energy regime quantum only helps if the SURROGATE itself is hard to sample classically
  (glassy at the needed temperature). This kills most "expensive energy" stories unless the surrogate is
  LOW-T frustrated.
  Asymmetry warning [own]: with a residual-based energy (CT, PET), a classical 1-pixel flip costs one
  projector column update; a quantum proposal flipping k pixels costs k column updates. Per-step energy
  cost is not equal in the two chains; the brief's "t_E cancels" assumption only holds for full re-evaluation.

TEMPERATURE EVIDENCE FOR FITTED HEALTH ISING MODELS (decides the KNOWN items)
- Neural populations (retina, N up to 160): fitted max-ent model at its native T=1 sits near a
  specific-heat peak that sharpens with N; "poised near an unusual kind of critical point".
  Tkacik et al., PNAS 112, 11508 (2015), arXiv:1407.5946 [lit]; Mora & Bialek, J Stat Phys 144, 268
  (2011), arXiv:1012.2242 [lit]. => MEDIUM by the brief's definition.
  Caveat: Nonnenmacher et al., PLoS Comput Biol 13, e1005718 (2017), arXiv:1603.00097 [lit]: the
  specific-heat signature arises from random subsampling of any correlated population, so "near
  critical" may be generic, not special. Simoes et al., arXiv:2511.14872 (2025) [lit]: max-ent
  signatures appear for critical AND supercritical cultures.
- Whole-brain fMRI (binarised ROIs): fitted models are "in the paramagnetic phase but close to the
  boundary" with the spin-glass phase; individuals are "substantially off" criticality.
  Ezaki et al., Commun Biol 3, 52 (2020), bioRxiv 10.1101/688655, N=264 ROIs, 138 adults [lit].
  Typical energy-landscape analysis uses N ~ 10 (N=7 whole-brain example); exact gradient fit "slow
  even with N=15 or 20". Masuda et al. tutorial, arXiv:2411.16979 (PLoS Complex Syst 2025) [lit].
  Temperature fixed at 1 by construction (Watanabe et al., PMC4174295 [lit]). => HIGH-to-MEDIUM,
  paramagnetic side; small N enumerable.
- Symptom networks: IsingFit / eLasso (van Borkulo et al., Sci Rep 4, 5918 (2014)) [lit]; typical
  N = 9-20 symptoms, largest 120 symptoms in 34,653 subjects (Boschloo et al.) [lit, via search
  snippet]. "Network temperature" (Grimes, Epskamp et al., Nature Mental Health 2025,
  doi 10.1038/s44220-025-00415-5) [lit]: low-temperature networks are bimodal (healthy vs depressed),
  temperature decreases across adolescence. Couplings mostly positive (ferro). => LOW-T but
  ferromagnetic two-well; unfrustrated, so cluster moves / exact enumeration work.
- TCGA driver mutations: no published pairwise max-ent Ising fit of driver co-occurrence was found
  [lit search negative this session]. Mutual exclusivity is often a subtype / mutation-load confound
  (PMC6816465 review; DIALECT bioRxiv 2024) [lit]. Driver frequencies are low (most below ~10%, a few
  like TP53/KRAS higher) [lit-m] -> strong negative fields, weak sparse couplings => HIGH-T, near
  product distribution [own].
- Protein Potts (DCA): Russ et al., Science 369, 440 (2020) designed functional chorismate mutases by
  sampling the bmDCA Potts model at formal temperature T <= 1 to reach low energies [lit, from the
  paper text]. Lower-T sampling improves design quality (arXiv:2204.07110) [lit]. => design regime is
  BELOW the training temperature: LOW/MEDIUM.

B6-01  Few-view binary / label CT: Bayesian posterior sampling of discrete tomography
  Payoff: uncertainty maps and multiple plausible reconstructions for limited-angle / few-view
    imaging with discrete content (contrast-filled vessels in rotational angiography, bone vs soft
    tissue, metal implants, dental), and for industrial NDT. Clinical CT is rarely truly binary; the
    honest medical niche is small [own].
  Regime: LOW at high SNR and few views. Why [own + lit-m]: with x in {0,1}^N and Gaussian noise,
    E(x) = ||Ax - b||^2 / (2 sigma^2) + prior is EXACTLY quadratic -> Ising with J = A^T A / sigma^2.
    Pixels on a common ray compete to explain the same ray sum -> antiferro-like, frustrated couplings.
    Reconstruction of lattice sets from >=3 projection directions is NP-hard (Gardner, Gritzmann,
    Prangenberg, Discrete Math 202 (1999) [lit-m]). Small sigma = low T; the many near-degenerate
    "switching components" (ghost configurations invisible to the projections) are exactly the
    separated basins local moves cannot cross [own]. This is the rare health case where the frustrated
    energy is physics-defined, not a weak data fit.
  Mapping: exact Ising (no surrogate). Coupling graph: long-range along rays; within a P2 patch of m
    pixels nearly all-to-all. Embedding on heavy-hex: swap network, ~m(m-1)/2 ZZ per Trotter step,
    m=20 -> 190 ZZ, roughly 400-600 CZ per Trotter step [own estimate].
  Energy cost per step: incremental residual update O(k x V) per k flipped pixels and V views:
    ~us. Cheap.
  Spins: full 64x64 = 4096 to 512x512; coarse-grained group = 9-25 pixel patch (P2 sqrt-n style),
    effective field from the frozen rest exactly as P2 Eq 14.
  Mixing the bottleneck? Plausibly yes at few views and low noise (ghost-component degeneracy);
    unknown whether parallel tempering already handles it at N ~ 1e3-1e4 [spec].
  Best classical: DART (Batenburg & Sijbers, IEEE TIP 20, 2542 (2011)) [lit-m] for MAP; parallel
    tempering / SA for posterior; QUBO-on-annealer CT already explored (Nau et al. J Imaging 2023,
    arXiv:2212.01312; Jun, Sci Rep 2023; Dremel et al. IEEE TQE 2025; arXiv:2607.10566 finds classical
    SA ~ D-Wave hybrid) [lit]. Note: these are optimisation, not posterior sampling -> sampling angle
    is open.
  Sketch: 16x16 binary phantoms, 2-4 views, sigma sweep; exact enumeration impossible (2^256) so
    reference = long PT. Compare tau_int of patch-QeMCMC (statevector, m<=16) vs local, uniform-in-patch,
    PT, and "switching-component" moves (classical swap of a known ghost pattern - the domain-specific
    cluster move that may make quantum moot). Detailed balance: single random patch per step,
    symmetric ideal proposal -> exact [P1, P2].
  Break-even: cheap energy -> S* ~ 1e6+ on sequential cloud steps [own]; no wall-clock case today.
    Value would be a step-count/scaling result on a physics-defined frustrated medical energy.
  Kill risk: classical "ghost-swap" cluster moves or PT already mix; the patch group is small enough
    to be classically simulable (P2's own "quantum inspired" footnote).
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE. Likelihood of a defensible step-scaling result vs PT: ~20-25% [spec].

B6-02  Surrogate-driven QeMCMC for expensive nonlinear forward models (polychromatic CT, MR
       fingerprinting dictionary-free fits, Monte Carlo scatter/dose), discrete parameter maps
  Payoff: posterior over label/tissue-class maps when each true-likelihood call is a costly
    simulation (Monte Carlo photon transport, Bloch simulation).
  Regime: depends on surrogate; LOW only if label prior + few data. Why: the brief's only HIGH/any-T
    route to wall-clock is expensive energy [own].
  Mapping: H_prob = linearised quadratic surrogate (Gauss-Newton A^T A around current estimate) +
    Potts prior; true energy used only in accept. Proposal stays symmetric for any H_prob [P1] ->
    detailed balance exact with the true energy.
  Energy cost per step: 0.1-10 s per true evaluation [spec; depends on simulator].
  Spins: 10-30 qubits per group (label patch), rest frozen.
  Mixing the bottleneck? The bottleneck is the NUMBER of true evaluations; the question is
    acceptance per true call.
  Best classical: delayed-acceptance / two-stage MCMC with a classical chain on the same surrogate
    (Christen & Fox 2005 [lit-m]); multilevel MCMC. These propose a near-independent draw from the
    surrogate Boltzmann law for ~ms classical cost.
  Sketch: compare acceptance-per-true-call of QeMCMC vs delayed acceptance on identical surrogates.
  Break-even [own]: t_E = 1-10 s, IBM t_lat 2-5 s -> S* ~ 1.3-6 vs local. But vs delayed acceptance
    the step advantage is ~1 unless the surrogate is LOW-T glassy (then classical inner chain also
    fails to give independent draws).
  Kill risk: surrogate is easy (smooth, near-convex) -> delayed acceptance wins outright.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (only if B6-01 shows surrogate-level glassiness). ~10-15%.

B6-03  Posterior label-map sampling for segmentation uncertainty (Potts MRF, brain MRI tissue / tumour)
  Payoff: calibrated per-voxel uncertainty, volume error bars.
  Regime: HIGH to MEDIUM. Strong data term acts as large external field; Potts beta usually estimated
    or set below the lattice critical value beta_c = ln(1+sqrt q) [lit-m].
  Mapping: Potts, q = 3-5 labels, 2-3 qubits per voxel binary encoding, nearest-neighbour grid ->
    heavy-hex friendly for small 2D patches.
  Energy cost: local, ~ns per site. Spins: 1e5-1e7 voxels; group = 3x3 to 5x5 patch.
  Mixing bottleneck? Mostly no: Gibbs mixes except along ambiguous boundaries; single-site Gibbs is
    known to mix poorly in hidden Potts models (arXiv:1602.03048) [lit], but cluster and exact
    samplers below already fix that.
  Best classical: Swendsen-Wang cuts (Barbu & Zhu, IEEE TPAMI 27, 1239 (2005)) [lit]; perfect MCMC
    (coupling-from-the-past) for MRF segmentation UQ, Med Image Anal 2019, PubMed 31085445 [lit]; deep
    probabilistic segmentation (ensembles, probabilistic U-Net) [lit-m].
  Break-even: S* ~ 1e7+ [own]. Kill risk: exact perfect sampling already exists. Detailed balance: fine
    but irrelevant.
  Verdict: KILL. <3%.

B6-04  Synthetic texture phantoms from ferromagnetic Ising/Potts fields (lung parenchyma, GGO texture)
  Payoff: training/validation data for detection AI (the lung project's current classical path).
  Regime: chosen by designer; natural-looking textures sit near criticality (MEDIUM) [spec].
  Mapping: nearest-neighbour Potts, unfrustrated.
  Energy cost: ns. Spins: 1e4-1e6.
  Mixing bottleneck? Critical slowing down yes for local moves, but Swendsen-Wang/Wolff remove it for

B6-05  Frustrated-texture phantoms (competing-interaction / ANNNI-like priors giving labyrinthine,
       fibrotic or honeycomb patterns)
  Payoff: same as B6-04 with patterns ferro fields cannot produce.
  Regime: MEDIUM/LOW; frustrated, so cluster moves lose their guarantee [lit-m].
  Mapping: 2D grid with nn ferro + nnn antiferro; fits heavy-hex poorly (diagonals) -> extra SWAPs.
  Energy cost: ns. Spins: 1e4+; group 9-25.
  Mixing bottleneck? Yes in principle, but the user does not need equilibrium: any visually plausible
    texture suffices, so a quenched or approximate sampler is fine [own].
  Best classical: PT, Kawasaki/cluster variants, or simply reaction-diffusion / GAN texture generators.
  Kill risk: no one needs exact Boltzmann textures. Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (demo value). ~5%.

B6-06  Low-count PET/SPECT discrete reconstruction (lesion-present labels, activity levels as Potts)
  Payoff: lesion-detection posterior probability in very low-dose scans.
  Regime: HIGH. Low counts -> broad Poisson posterior -> weak effective couplings [own].
  Mapping: Poisson log-likelihood is not quadratic -> needs quadratic surrogate H_prob (fine for
    detailed balance [P1]). Energy: projector column updates, us-ms.
  Spins: 1e5+ voxels; group 10-30.
  Best classical: MLEM/OSEM + MAP priors, Gibbs/Langevin, learned reconstructions [lit-m].

B6-07  Protein-family sequence design from bmDCA Potts models (Russ 2020 style), sampled at T < 1
  Payoff: functional enzyme/protein variants; experimentally validated pipeline exists [lit].
  Regime: LOW/MEDIUM. Designs are drawn at T <= 1 below the training temperature (Russ et al.,
    Science 369, 440 (2020)) [lit]; multimodal subfamily structure makes low-T Gibbs slow [spec].
  Mapping: Potts q=21, L=60-300 positions -> 5 qubits per site binary (11 unused codes = infinite
    energy), 300-1500 qubits full; couplings dense (all position pairs nonzero in bmDCA). Group = 3-4
    contact positions = 15-20 qubits, effective field from the rest.
  Energy cost: O(L) per site change, ~us. Cheap.
  Mixing bottleneck? Not for practice: autoregressive arDCA (Trinquier et al., Nat Commun 2021
    [lit-m]) and protein language models give i.i.d. samples with no Markov chain at all; adabmDCA
    already tracks MCMC mixing time via autocorrelation (arXiv:2109.04105, 2501.18456) [lit].
  Best classical: arDCA (exact ancestral sampling), Gibbs + PT, PLMs.
  Break-even: S* ~ 1e6+ [own].
  Kill risk: ancestral samplers make mixing irrelevant. Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. ~5-8%.

B6-08  Antibody CDR-H3 design with an expensive affinity oracle (surrogate Potts proposal, oracle accept)
  Payoff: Boltzmann-weighted diverse binders under a physics/ML affinity score (developability).
  Regime: LOW on the surrogate (design wants high-affinity tail) [spec].
  Mapping: L = 10-20 CDR positions, q = 20 -> 50-100 qubits binary, or restricted alphabets
    (q = 4-8 per position -> 2-3 qubits) to fit 20-40 qubits. H_prob = Potts fitted to oracle scores
    (pairwise). Proposal symmetric -> detailed balance with the oracle energy exact [P1].
  Energy cost per step: 0.1 s (ML scorer) to minutes (docking / FEP) [spec].
  Mixing bottleneck? The bottleneck is oracle calls per accepted, decorrelated sample.
  Best classical: delayed acceptance on the same Potts surrogate; Bayesian optimisation; generative
    language models; D-Wave peptide design already shown as optimisation (Meuser et al.
    arXiv:2503.05458; Mulligan et al. bioRxiv 752485) [lit].
  Break-even [own]: t_E >= 10 s -> S* <= 1.3 vs local. Against delayed acceptance, quantum needs the
    q=20, L=15 Potts surrogate to be glassy; it may be, as protein Potts at T<1 is multimodal [spec].
  Kill risk: oracle noise dominates, surrogate easy, or design is optimisation not sampling (then
    QeMCMC is the wrong algorithm).
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (couple to B6-02). ~10%.

B6-11  Boltzmann-machine learning loop for large neural / fMRI max-ent models (QeMCMC as the moment
       estimator inside fitting, N = 100-1000+)
  Payoff: faster, less biased fitting of large pairwise/K-pairwise models (modern recordings reach
    1e3 neurons; Meshulam et al. 2019 [lit-m]).
  Regime: MEDIUM: fitted models sit near the specific-heat peak at T=1 (Tkacik 2015 [lit]) with
    mixed-sign couplings, so Wolff/SW lose their guarantee [own]. Closest health analogue to the
    PILOT's beta 0.3 SK point (2.8x steps vs weak baselines) [PILOT].
  Mapping: all-to-all Ising, N spins; group 10-30 neurons.
  Energy cost: O(N) per flip, ns-us. Cheap.
  Mixing bottleneck? Partly: each gradient step needs equilibrated moments; persistent chains and
    pseudo-likelihood / minimum-probability-flow avoid most sampling [lit-m].
  Best classical: persistent contrastive divergence, PT, pseudo-likelihood, GPU Gibbs.
  Break-even: S* ~ 1e6+ [own]; and fitting tolerates biased short chains, so exactness is not the
    payoff.
  Kill risk: pseudo-likelihood removes the sampler from the loop; MEDIUM gives only ~3x steps vs weak
    baselines [PILOT], likely ~1x vs PT [spec].
  Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. ~8%.

B6-12  Clinical-trial rerandomization: sampling balanced treatment allocations
  Payoff: exact design-based inference with very tight covariate balance; small trials (n = 20-200)
    where imbalance hurts most.
  Regime: LOW. Imbalance = ||X^T w||^2 with w in {+1,-1}^n is a dense Mattis-sum Ising, i.e. a
    multi-dimensional number-partitioning energy (known golf-course landscape at tight thresholds)
    [own]. Morgan & Rubin rerandomization (Ann Stat 2012, arXiv:1207.5625) accepts assignments below a
    Mahalanobis threshold; optimal acceptance probabilities can be "astronomically small" with many
    covariates (arXiv:2602.07613, MH rerandomization) [lit]; FastRerandomize arXiv:2501.07642 [lit].
  Mapping: QUBO with J = X X^T (rank p, dense), plus equal-arm constraint (penalty or fixed-weight
    subspace). Ties to the repo's number-partitioning epic. All-to-all -> swap network.
  Energy cost: O(p) incremental per flip, ns. Spins: n = 20-200; group = whole trial for n <= 30.
  Mixing bottleneck? Yes at extreme thresholds (rejection sampling dies; local MH swaps slow) [lit,
    arXiv:2602.07613 motivates MH for exactly this reason].
  Best classical: MH rerandomization (arXiv:2602.07613), GPU rejection (2501.07642), Kallus optimal
    a priori balance via MIP (arXiv:1312.0531) [lit]; pair matching.
  Detailed balance: target is uniform on {w : imbalance < a}; with symmetric QeMCMC proposal and
    accept = indicator, stationary law is exactly uniform on the set [own, from P1 symmetry].
  Break-even: one trial needs ~1e3-1e4 allocations once, offline; seconds of classical time suffice
    [own]. Wall-clock payoff ~ nil; value is a clean LOW-T, dense, small-n, real-world energy for a
    step-scaling study.
  Kill risk: nobody needs it faster; regulators prefer simple minimisation/stratification.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (as a benchmark instance family, not a product). ~10%.

B6-13  Radiotherapy beam-angle set sampling (diverse near-optimal beam sets)
  Payoff: give planners several Boltzmann-weighted near-optimal angle sets.
  Regime: LOW (near-optimal tail) [own].
  Mapping: one-hot / k-of-M selection over M = 36-72 candidate angles, k = 5-9; pairwise QUBO
    surrogate from beam-pair scores; true energy = inner fluence-map optimisation.
  Energy cost per step: seconds to minutes per inner FMO solve [spec] -> expensive energy.
  Mixing bottleneck? This is optimisation; samples are a nice-to-have.
  Best classical: column generation, iCycle, GA/SA, quantum-inspired MIP (Shinde et al., Med Phys

  Break-even [own]: t_E ~ 10-60 s -> S* ~ 1.05-1.3 vs local; vs surrogate-driven classical proposal ~1
    unless the beam-pair surrogate is glassy (k-of-M with smooth pair scores is probably not) [spec].
  Kill risk: optimisation problem dressed as sampling. Verdict: KILL for QeMCMC (fine for QAOA/annealing
    folks). ~3%.

B6-18  GWAS fine-mapping: posterior over causal-SNP configurations in high-LD loci
  Payoff: credible sets that drive which variant gets functional follow-up; mis-assigned causality in
    high-LD regions is a known practical problem [lit-m].
  Regime: LOW/MEDIUM effective. Strong LD makes SNPs near-interchangeable -> near-degenerate,
    well-separated modes (swap causal SNP A for its LD partner B requires two coordinated flips through
    a low-probability state) [own].
  Mapping: one spin per SNP indicator (gamma_j); energy = -log marginal likelihood (needs k x k solves
    for k causal; NOT pairwise) -> quadratic surrogate H_prob from the LD matrix (z^T R^-1 z style
    approximation) [own]. Detailed balance with the exact energy holds for any symmetric surrogate [P1].
  Energy cost: O(k^3) with k <= 5-10 -> us-ms. Spins: locus of 100-5000 SNPs; group = one LD block of
    10-30 SNPs.
  Mixing bottleneck? Yes for naive MCMC; but best tools sidestep it: FINEMAP shotgun stochastic search,
    SuSiE variational, CAVIAR/DAP-G exhaustive up to small k, and LD-aware swap moves [lit-m].
  Best classical: FINEMAP (Benner et al., Bioinformatics 2016), SuSiE (Wang et al., JRSS-B 2020)
    [lit-m]; PT with swap moves.
  Break-even: S* ~ 1e3-1e6 [own].
  Kill risk: the dedicated "swap" move (replace causal SNP with LD partner) is a classical cluster move
    that solves exactly the hard transition; sparsity (k small) makes the space searchable.
  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (cheap to test on simulated LD blocks; good honest negative if swap moves
    win). ~10%.

B6-20  Spatial-transcriptomics cell-type maps with a Potts prior (BayesSpace-style MCMC)
  Payoff: tumour-microenvironment clone/cell-type maps with uncertainty (closest live analogue to the
    qh-17 clone-map layer).
  Regime: HIGH-to-MEDIUM. BayesSpace (Zhao et al., Nat Biotech 2021 [lit-m]) uses a Potts smoothing
    prior with a Gibbs sampler; strong expression data term dominates [lit-m].
  Mapping: Potts on hexagonal spot lattice (Visium ~4,000 spots), q = 5-15 -> 3-4 qubits per spot;
    hex lattice is not heavy-hex native but low degree [own].
  Energy: local, ns-us. Mixing bottleneck? No in practice; frustration only if competing-clone
    antiferro couplings are imposed (qh-17's condition), which data do not support [own].
  Verdict: KILL. <3%.

KNOWN-K12  Neuron-population max-ent Ising (Schneidman 2006; Tkacik)
  Payoff: generative model of population codes; sampling for entropy/thermodynamics estimates.
  Regime: MEDIUM. Fitted retina models (N up to 160) sit near the specific-heat peak at T = 1, peak

  Mapping: all-to-all Ising with mixed-sign couplings; N = 40-160 (retina) to 1e3 (Neuropixels).
    Group 10-30.
  Energy: O(N) ns-us. Spins: 40-1000.
  Mixing bottleneck? Moderate: near the specific-heat peak local moves slow down; mixed signs block

  Best classical: PT, GPU Gibbs, pseudo-likelihood / MPF for fitting (sampling avoided).
  Break-even: S* ~ 1e6+ [own]. PILOT at the critical (MEDIUM) point gives ~2.8x steps vs local/uniform

KNOWN-K13  Symptom / comorbidity networks (IsingFit)

  Mixing bottleneck? Two-well ferro: tunnelling between wells is slow for local moves, but a global

  Verdict: KILL. <2%.

=====================================================================================================
SYNTHESIS
=====================================================================================================
1. The qh-17 closure generalises [own]: data-fitted health Ising/Potts models (genotypes, symptoms,
   comorbidity, fMRI, segmentation, spatial omics) are HIGH-T, ferromagnetic, or small enough to
   enumerate. Fitting at T = 1 to finite, sparse clinical data with regularisation pushes models toward
   the paramagnetic side. The one partial exception is neural populations (MEDIUM, near a specific-heat
   peak), and even there the signature may be a subsampling artefact.
2. Where frustration is real in health, it comes from PHYSICS or DESIGN, not from data fits:
   few-view discrete tomography (J = A^T A, NP-hard), tight-balance rerandomization (number-partitioning
   energy), LD-degenerate fine-mapping, low-T protein/antibody Potts design.
3. Every candidate has cheap energy except B6-02/08/13. Expensive energies make S* ~ 1-30 against local
   moves, but the honest competitor is classical delayed acceptance on the same surrogate, which
   reduces the quantum case to "is the surrogate glassy?".
4. Nothing here is BUILD NOW. Nothing reaches wall-clock break-even on sequential cloud hardware
   (A4: ~2-5 s per IBM step).

TOP 3 (for the team lead)
  1. B6-01 few-view discrete tomography posterior: physics-defined frustrated LOW-T energy, exact
     quadratic (no surrogate), natural P2 patch groups, open sampling angle (prior quantum CT work only
     optimises). Test vs PT and ghost-swap cluster moves.
  2. B6-02 + B6-08 expensive-energy family (nonlinear imaging forward models, antibody oracle): the only
     route where S* drops to ~1-6 at today's latency; succeeds only if the surrogate is glassy, so run
     it after B6-01 measures glassiness.
  3. B6-18 GWAS fine-mapping in high-LD blocks (or B6-12 rerandomization as a clean LOW-T dense
     benchmark): real decision value, cheap to simulate, and a well-defined classical cluster move
     (causal-SNP swap) to beat, so a negative result is still clean.
  Best KNOWN item: K12 neural max-ent (MEDIUM), QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY.
