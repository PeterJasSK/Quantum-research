1. B6-01 IN DEPTH: FEW-VIEW BINARY CT POSTERIOR
=====================================================================================================
1a. Mapping and temperature [own]. x in {0,1}^N, b = A x0 + w, w ~ N(0, sigma^2 I), A = ray-pixel incidence
  (unit weights, digital lines, V directions). -log posterior = ||Ax - b||^2/(2 sigma^2) - log prior(x).
  Quadratic part: (1/(2 sigma^2)) x^T A^T A x - (1/sigma^2) b^T A x. (A^T A)_ij = number of rays shared by
  pixels i,j (0..V off-diagonal; V on the diagonal). In spin variables s = 2x-1 the coupling is
  J_ij = (A^T A)_ij/(4 sigma^2) > 0 with a penalty sign, i.e. antiferromagnetic between every pair of pixels on a
  common ray: each pixel couples to ~V sqrt(N) others. Three pixels pairwise sharing a row, a column and a
  diagonal form an antiferro triangle, so the data term IS geometrically frustrated. Temperature: the posterior
  is the Boltzmann law of E = ||Ax-b||^2/2 at beta = 1/sigma^2, so the noise variance sets T. Low noise = low T.
1b. Nishimori check (the point raised by B4). Verified:
  Nishimori, Prog. Theor. Phys. 66, 1169 (1981), doi:10.1143/PTP.66.1169 [lit]
    https://www.scilit.com/publications/3d6d1ba1d09a549727e9758270544eb5 (bibliographic record; full text not read).
  Zdeborova & Krzakala, "Statistical physics of inference: thresholds and algorithms", Adv. Phys. 65, 453 (2016),
    arXiv:1511.02476, https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02476 ; section II.7 "No replica symmetry breaking in
    Bayes-optimal inference" read via https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1511.02476 [lit]. What it says: on the Nishimori
    line (prior, likelihood and temperature all matched = Bayes-optimal posterior of a planted instance) the overlap
    with the planted signal is self-averaging and there is no static RSB, i.e. "absence of glass phase at
    equilibrium"; a rigorous version (Montanari) exists for sparse systems; magnetisation self-averaging is not
    fully rigorous. IMPORTANT caveat in the same text: this excludes only STATIC RSB. A dynamical (d1RSB) glass and
    metastability can still appear when the inference transition is first order (the "hard phase"), and then
    local dynamics can be stuck although Bayes-optimal marginals are well defined.
  Consequence for B6-01 [own]: when the tomography posterior is the calibrated one (the only one whose
  uncertainty maps a clinician should trust), the instance is planted and matched -> Nishimori line -> no
  SK-like static glass. The P1/P2/PILOT evidence (unplanted SK at T<=1) does not transfer by default. The LOW-T
  claim in B6-01 as a "glassy" claim is therefore KILLED for matched posteriors. What survives:
   (i) Activated barriers that are O(1) per elementary switching component, not extensive: for 2 views a
       4-pixel interchange; a single flip raises the energy by ~V/(2 sigma^2), so local Metropolis crosses at rate
       ~exp(-c V/sigma^2). This is low-T slowness, but N-independent per component and removable by a classical
       switching move (below).
   (ii) First-order / hard-phase metastability at intermediate SNR and few views (binary signal, linear
       measurements, structured A). For random A this is the CDMA / compressed-sensing family: Krzakala et al.,
       Phys. Rev. X 2, 021005 (2012), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.2.021005 [lit, existence of paper and
       BP-based reconstruction verified; its first-order phase diagram is lit-m]; Tanaka, IEEE Trans. IT 48, 2888
       (2002), doi:10.1109/TIT.2002.804053 [lit, paper verified; the first-order coexistence region of the
       individually-optimal binary detector is lit-m]. In a genuine hard phase, all known polynomial algorithms
       fail; a quench proposal on a small patch is not expected to change that, and the Orfi-Sels results [A3] warn
       against unstructured-landscape gains.
   (iii) Mismatched posteriors (prior or temperature chosen by hand, e.g. annealing toward MAP or a too-cold
       smoothness prior) are off-Nishimori and can be glassy. But then the samples are from a model nobody
       calibrated, so the medical UQ value disappears; this is an SK study in tomography clothes.
1c. Classical discrete-tomography competitors (verified):
  NP-hardness for >=3 directions: Gardner, Gritzmann, Prangenberg, Discrete Math. 202, 45-71 (1999),
    doi:10.1016/S0012-365X(98)00347-1, https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/on-the-computational-complexity-of-reconstructing-lattice-sets-fr/ [lit]
    (worst-case consistency/reconstruction; says nothing about typical planted posteriors).
  DART: Batenburg & Sijbers, IEEE TIP 20(9), 2542-2553 (2011), doi:10.1109/TIP.2011.2131661,
    https://homepages.cwi.nl/~kbatenbu/papers/basij_tip_2011.pdf [lit] (MAP-type, not sampling).
  Bayesian marginals for binary tomography by belief propagation with an Ising smoothness prior: Gouillart,
    Krzakala, Mezard, Zdeborova, Inverse Problems 29, 035003 (2013), arXiv:1211.2379,
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.2379 [lit]: fast BP reconstruction, beats convex methods in recovery bounds and
    under moderate Gaussian noise; maps the recovery threshold vs number of projections. This is the strongest
    competitor for "posterior marginals / uncertainty maps" and B6 did not list it.
  Gibbs priors for binary tomography: Matej, Vardi, Herman, Vardi, in Discrete Tomography (Birkhauser 1999),
    doi:10.1007/978-1-4612-1568-4_8 [lit, chapter exists]; marginal-posterior-mode approach with Gibbs priors for
    very few views (MaRDI Q3439202) [lit, record only].
  Switching-component moves: for 2 directions every pair of binary matrices with equal row/column sums is
    connected by 4-pixel interchanges (Ryser 1957) [lit-m]; for V>=3 elementary switches are not always a
    connected move set (that is where NP-hardness lives) [lit-m].
  PT over sigma (temperature ladder = noise ladder) flattens the ~V/sigma^2 barriers [own].
  Quantum-annealer CT papers are all optimisation (QUBO MAP), none samples the posterior; a search this session
    found no posterior-sampling quantum tomography paper (arXiv:2212.01312, arXiv:2607.10566, arXiv:2606.24561 are
    MAP) [lit]. The "sampling angle is open" statement in B6 stands.

  Energy asymmetry: a k-pixel quantum move costs k incremental updates vs 1 for a classical flip, irrelevant at
