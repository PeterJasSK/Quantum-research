=====================================================================================================
0. CROSS-CUTTING FINDINGS (these drive most verdicts below)
=====================================================================================================

C1. Effective temperature of a Bayesian posterior. A posterior is sampled at T=1 by definition, but the
log-likelihood is a sum over n_obs observations, so n_obs acts like beta [OWN; this is the standard
power-posterior picture]. Regime per problem therefore depends on how rugged the posterior is:
  effectively HIGH: weak data, near-independent coordinates (posterior close to prior, product-like).
  effectively MEDIUM: moderate data, competing explanations of similar weight (several modes at small
    Hamming distance, critical-like slow down of single-flip chains).
  effectively LOW: strong data plus non-identifiability (collinear predictors, linkage disequilibrium,
    label symmetry, equivalent DAGs): deep modes separated by Hamming distance >= 3 through
    low-probability states. Single-flip and add-delete-swap chains get trapped here.
Yang, Wainwright and Jordan show rapid mixing of add-delete-swap Metropolis for variable selection only
under restricted-eigenvalue type conditions, i.e. NOT under strong collinearity (Ann. Statist. 2016,
arXiv:1505.07925) [LIT, recalled].

C2. Surrogate-driven QeMCMC is exact in the ideal limit. The proposal U=exp(-iHt) is symmetric for ANY
real H_prob [P1], so one can put a cheap Ising surrogate (quadratic approximation of a non-Ising
log-posterior or of a simulator output) into U and evaluate the TRUE energy only in the Metropolis
accept. Detailed balance with respect to the true target holds because Q(s'|s)=Q(s|s') and the accept
uses the true ratio [P1 argument, OWN application]. Hardware noise breaks exact symmetry; P1 SI needed
twirling and a small bias remains possible [P1]. The quality of the surrogate only changes the
acceptance rate, not correctness.

C3. The classical surrogate competitor (the strongest kill risk for every "expensive energy" idea).
When the true energy is expensive and a cheap Ising surrogate exists, the classical side can also use
the surrogate: run parallel tempering (PT) on the surrogate at ~ns per flip, take a near-independent
surrogate-Boltzmann sample s' ~ pi_sur, and use it as an independence proposal with accept
min(1, pi(s') pi_sur(s) / (pi(s) pi_sur(s'))); pi_sur is known up to a constant, so this is exact
Metropolis-Hastings [OWN; same family as delayed-acceptance MCMC, Christen and Fox, J. Comput. Graph.
Stat. 14, 795 (2005) [LIT, recalled]]. It costs ONE true-energy evaluation per step, exactly like
QeMCMC. Millions of surrogate flips fit into the time of one 1 s energy evaluation. Consequence [OWN]:
in the expensive-energy regime the quantum proposal only wins if (a) the surrogate itself is
classically hard to sample (glassy, LOW T, n beyond what PT handles in ~1 s), or (b) the quantum
proposal has a structurally better acceptance against the true target than surrogate-Boltzmann
proposals (no argument for this exists). Any expensive-energy study in this domain MUST include this
competitor or it is not a fair comparison.

C4. Hard constraints. Many problems here live on constrained sets: cardinality (portfolio), one-to-one
matching (record linkage), acyclicity (Bayesian networks), radiality/spanning trees (distribution
grids), permutations (routing), fixed margins (disclosure tables). The transverse-field mixer proposes
states outside the set, which get pi=0 and are rejected. A Hamming-weight-preserving XY mixer keeps H
real symmetric inside the fixed-weight subspace, so symmetry and detailed balance survive [OWN]; this
route is studied for fixed-weight Boltzmann sampling with QAOA XY mixers plus neural surrogates by
Kawamata, Nakano and Fujii (arXiv:2604.20701, simulation, 14x and 7.7x autocorrelation-rate gains vs
local and global Kawasaki dynamics) [LIT]. No native mixer exists for acyclicity, one-to-one matching
beyond small blocks, radiality or permutations at reasonable qubit cost [OWN].

C5. Optimisation is not sampling. Unit commitment, portfolio selection, routing and facility location are
optimisation problems; the deliverable is one good solution, not a Boltzmann sample. MILP/MIQP solvers
are the competitor and QeMCMC is the wrong tool [OWN]. Only their genuinely Bayesian or rare-event
versions qualify.

C6. Learning loops. Fitting an Ising model to data (species, votes, rain) is where sampling is needed
(Boltzmann learning), but pseudo-likelihood avoids sampling entirely and is the default in these fields
(Harris, Ecology 2016, doi 10.1002/ecy.1605; exact normalisation feasible up to ~30 species) [LIT].

C7. Break-even arithmetic for this domain [OWN, using A4-hardware.txt numbers].
  IBM gate model, one job per step: t_Q ~ 2-5 s. D-Wave reverse-anneal step: ~0.03-1 s. Batched
  K chains per job: ~2/K s per chain-step (A4).
  Cheap statistical energy (marginal likelihood with k<=10 via cached Gram matrix, Ising delta-E):
  t_E ~ 1-10 us -> S* = t_Q/t_C ~ 2e5 to 5e6 (IBM sequential), ~200-5000 even at K=1000 batching.
  Medium energy (AC power flow ~10-100 ms, Laplace-approximated GLMM marginal likelihood ~1-100 ms):
  S* ~ 20-500 (IBM), ~1.3-100 (D-Wave).
  Expensive energy (cascade simulation, hydrological/fire simulator, ~1 s or more): S* ~ 1.1-6.
  The team pilot step ratios are 1.3x (HIGH), 2.8x (MEDIUM, critical), 25x (LOW) vs WEAK baselines
  (local, uniform) [BRIEF]. Against PT or cluster moves these shrink further (A3: Mazumder and Towe
  2607.09893, PT recovers most of the ESS gap). So at HIGH and MEDIUM T only the expensive-energy
  corner can reach break-even, and there C3 applies.

B5-01  Bayesian variable selection with collinear predictor blocks (spike-and-slab / g-prior, 2^p models)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: posterior inclusion probabilities and model averaging when predictors come in correlated groups
  (economics, environmental drivers, omics). Wrong mixing = wrong inclusion probabilities, silently.
Temperature regime: T=1 posterior. Effectively LOW to MEDIUM when blocks of 3+ predictors are nearly
  collinear and jointly predictive: the modes are "one representative from each block", separated by
  Hamming >= 2 through bad-fit states (drop all) or penalised states (keep two) [OWN, C1]. Effectively
  HIGH for near-orthogonal designs, where TGS/informed proposals already mix fast.
Mapping: gamma_j in {0,1} -> spins. Ising surrogate = second-order expansion of the log marginal
  likelihood in gamma: h_j from marginal correlation with y and the prior inclusion odds, J_ij negative
  (repulsive) for collinear pairs, positive for suppressor pairs [OWN]. Dense J inside blocks, near zero
  across blocks: block-sparse. Embedding: all-to-all inside each block (block size 5-30); natural fit
  for P2 coarse-graining with groups = correlation blocks instead of index blocks [OWN].
Energy per step: log marginal likelihood under g-prior with k active variables: O(k^3) Cholesky on the
  cached Gram submatrix, ~1-10 us for k<=10; O(n k^2) if not cached [OWN]. Cheap.
Spins needed: full p = 50-20000; per group 5-30.
Is mixing the bottleneck? Yes for collinear designs (C1, Yang-Wainwright-Jordan). No for orthogonal.
Best classical competitor: tempered Gibbs sampler / importance tempering, Zanella and Roberts, JRSS-B 81,
  489 (2019), arXiv:1805.00541 (orders of magnitude over alternatives, tens of thousands of regressors)
  [LIT]; locally informed proposals, Zanella, JASA 2020, arXiv:1711.07424 [LIT, recalled]; adaptive
  add-delete-swap, Griffin, Latuszynski and Steel, arXiv:1708.05678 [LIT, recalled]; block-swap moves
  that exchange a variable for a correlated partner [OWN].
Implementation sketch: surrogate-driven single-group QeMCMC: pick one correlation block at random
  (symmetric choice), freeze the rest as effective field (P2), propose inside the block with U built
  from the quadratic surrogate, accept with the TRUE marginal likelihood. Detailed balance: holds in the
  ideal case (symmetric proposal per group, random-group choice independent of state) [P2 single-group
  argument + C2]. Device: exact statevector for blocks <=20 first; trapped ion for all-to-all blocks.
Break-even: cheap energy -> S* ~ 1e5+ sequential; hardware wall-clock win impossible today [OWN, C7].
  The realistic product is quantum-inspired (MPS/statevector proposal on a 10-20 spin block is cheap
  classically, A3: Christmann et al. arXiv:2411.17821) [LIT].
Main kill risk: TGS plus a classical block-swap move already crosses Hamming-2/3 barriers; exhaustive
  enumeration inside a 15-spin block (32768 states x 10 us = 0.3 s) is an exact block Gibbs update, a
  perfect classical competitor for blocks up to ~20 [OWN].
Verdict guess: SIMULATE MORE (as the cleanest stats testbed), then likely QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY.
  Likelihood of defensible advantage: ~10%.

B5-02  Bayesian fine-mapping of causal variants inside linkage-disequilibrium (LD) blocks
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: which SNPs in a GWAS locus are causal; posterior inclusion probabilities feed credible sets and
  drug-target prioritisation. Direct link to the genome/healthcare angle wanted by the Siemens
  direction (memory note), though the client there now prefers classical lung work.
Temperature regime: effectively LOW. High LD (r^2 > 0.9) makes SNPs interchangeable; large GWAS sample
  sizes (n_obs ~ 1e5) make the posterior very peaked; with 2-5 causal signals the modes are sets of
  representatives separated by Hamming 2k [OWN, C1].
Mapping: same as B5-01 using summary statistics: h from z-scores, J from the LD matrix. Natural blocks.
Energy per step: summary-statistic likelihood with k<=10 causal: O(k^3) on the LD submatrix, ~us [OWN].
Spins needed: 100-5000 SNPs per locus; groups 10-30 (sub-blocks of high LD).
Is mixing the bottleneck? Partly. Existing tools avoid MCMC: FINEMAP uses shotgun stochastic search
  over k<=5 configurations, SuSiE uses variational sum-of-single-effects (Benner et al. Bioinformatics
  2016; Wang et al. JRSS-B 2020) [LIT, recalled]. Their known failure is under-coverage when LD is
  extreme and signals multiple, which is exactly the multimodal case [SPEC on size of the effect].
Best classical competitor: FINEMAP, SuSiE, plus exact enumeration over k<=3 within a block [LIT/OWN].
Implementation sketch: as B5-01, groups = LD sub-blocks. Benchmark against exact enumeration on small
  loci (p<=25) so ground truth is known. Detailed balance as B5-01.
Break-even: cheap energy, hardware hopeless now; quantum-inspired only [OWN].
Main kill risk: k is small, so the space of relevant models is C(p,k) and enumerable for k<=3; shotgun
  search covers it; MCMC is not the field's bottleneck.
Verdict guess: SIMULATE MORE (shared code with B5-01, real public summary stats), expect
  QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. Likelihood ~5-10%.

B5-03  Variable selection with EXPENSIVE likelihoods (GLMM, survival, Gaussian-process or simulator models)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: input selection for models where each candidate model must be fitted (Laplace/INLA marginal
  likelihood for GLMMs, Cox models, GP emulators for environmental simulators).
Temperature regime: as B5-01; MEDIUM to LOW when inputs are correlated (meteorological drivers).
Mapping: inclusion spins, Ising surrogate from a cheap linear-Gaussian proxy (B5-01 surrogate on the
  same design), true energy = expensive marginal likelihood (C2).
Energy per step: medium to expensive: Laplace/INLA fit ~10 ms to seconds; GP marginal likelihood
  O(n_obs^3) ~0.1-10 s for n_obs ~ 1e3-1e4 [OWN estimate].
Spins needed: 20-200 inputs.
Is mixing the bottleneck? Here the bottleneck is the number of true evaluations; mixing per evaluation is
  what matters.
Best classical competitor: C3 surrogate independence sampler / delayed acceptance (Christen-Fox), plus
  TGS on the proxy; also model-space Bayesian optimisation.
Implementation sketch: QeMCMC proposal from the linear proxy, accept with expensive likelihood; compare
  number of TRUE evaluations to ESS for (a) QeMCMC, (b) surrogate-PT independence sampler, (c) TGS.
  Detailed balance: yes, C2.
Break-even: S* ~ 1.1-6 at 1 s energy (C7); the step ratio must be measured vs (b), not vs local.
Main kill risk: C3. The proxy posterior on <=200 spins is classically easy to sample exactly-ish, so
  (b) is near-ideal and the quantum proposal has nothing structural to add [OWN].
Verdict guess: SIMULATE MORE (cheap to test with B5-01 code), likely KILL by C3. Likelihood ~5%.

B5-04  Bayesian network structure learning (structure MCMC over edges; order and partition MCMC)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: posterior over causal/graph structures (systems biology, risk models, environmental drivers).
Temperature regime: effectively LOW: Markov equivalence classes and strong data give many equal-score
  DAGs and deep modes; structure MCMC is known to mix poorly [LIT: Kuipers and Moffa, JASA 2017,
  arXiv:1504.05006].
Mapping: edge indicators -> spins (p(p-1) of them); acyclicity is a global hard constraint not
  expressible as Ising; order MCMC works on permutations (not binary) [OWN, C4].
Energy per step: BDe/BGe scores decompose per node and are cached: cheap (~us) [OWN].
Spins needed: 20-100 nodes -> 380-9900 edge spins.
Is mixing the bottleneck? Yes, but solved better classically by changing the state space.
Best classical competitor: order MCMC (Friedman and Koller 2003) [LIT, recalled], partition MCMC
  (arXiv:1504.05006) and the hybrid scheme of Kuipers, Suter and Moffa (arXiv:1803.07859, BiDAG)
  [LIT].
Implementation sketch: only plausible as QeMCMC on parent-set indicators of ONE node with all else fixed
  (acyclicity then reduces to excluding descendants), which is tiny and enumerable.
Break-even: cheap energy, constraint rejection -> no.
Main kill risk: acyclicity rejections plus partition MCMC.
Verdict guess: KILL. Likelihood <2%.

B5-05  Mixture-model allocations / Bayesian clustering (cluster labels = Potts)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: clustering with uncertainty (customer segments, weather regimes, phenotypes).
Temperature regime: LOW for well-separated clusters (label switching and split/merge barriers), HIGH
  for overlapping clusters. Label symmetry gives K! exactly equivalent modes that do not need to be
  mixed over (post-hoc relabelling) [OWN].
Mapping: Potts labels need one-hot (n x K qubits) with one-hot constraint -> transverse field leaves the
  feasible set; or log2 K binary encoding with dense multi-body terms [OWN, C4].
Energy per step: collapsed conjugate likelihood update ~us [OWN].
Spins needed: n data points x K, i.e. thousands.
Is mixing the bottleneck? Sometimes (split/merge).
Best classical competitor: split-merge MCMC, Jain and Neal, J. Comput. Graph. Stat. 2004 [LIT, recalled];
  variational and EM for point estimates.
Implementation sketch: none worth building; qubit count and constraint dominate.
Break-even: no. Main kill risk: encoding overhead plus split-merge.
Verdict guess: KILL. Likelihood <2%.

B5-06  Bayesian record linkage / entity resolution (bipartite matching posterior)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: census and health-record linkage with uncertainty propagated to downstream estimates.
Temperature regime: MEDIUM to LOW in ambiguous blocks (common names, typos): several near-equal matchings
  differing by swaps of 2-4 records [OWN].
Mapping: binary match matrix with one-to-one constraint (permutation-like) -> C4: X mixer violates it;
  only small blocks (<= 4x4 records) are representable with penalties [OWN].
Energy per step: Fellegi-Sunter comparison-vector likelihood, cheap (~us) [OWN].
Spins needed: block size squared; blocks are small after blocking.
Is mixing the bottleneck? In small ambiguous blocks yes, but these are enumerable.
Best classical competitor: Sadinle, JASA 2017 (bipartite matchings) [LIT, recalled]; locally informed
  proposals applied exactly to record linkage in Zanella arXiv:1711.07424 [LIT, recalled]; d-blink
  distributed partially collapsed Gibbs, Marchant et al., JCGS 2021, arXiv:1909.06039 (>300x speedups)
  [LIT].
Implementation sketch: not recommended.
Main kill risk: blocks small enough for quantum are enumerable; large blocks violate constraints.
Verdict guess: KILL. Likelihood <2%.

B5-08  Grid N-k contingency rare-event sampling on LARGE grids with an LODF Ising surrogate (expensive energy)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: blackout risk estimation and the list of dangerous multi-outage sets for planners (NERC-style
  extreme-event studies).
Temperature regime: target pi(s) proportional to p_outage(s) x exp(lambda x blackout_size(s)), a tilted
  (low effective T) importance distribution; blackout size is a threshold-like, rugged function of the
  outage set, so effectively LOW to MEDIUM [OWN].
Mapping: line/transformer outage indicators -> spins; Ising surrogate from line outage distribution
  factors: J_ij = overload induced on j by losing i (and vice versa), h_i from base loading and failure
  rate [OWN]. Sparse-ish but long-range (LODF is non-local). Embedding: dense on hardware; P2 groups =
  electrical neighbourhoods.
Energy per step: one cascading-failure simulation. Random-chemistry studies report ~2.35 s per RC trial
  on the 2383-bus Polish case and ~9.5 s on a Western US model (each trial = several cascade sims)
  [LIT: Rezaei, Hines, Eppstein arXiv:1405.4213, timing from search snippet]; DC cascade sim on a
  2000-bus case ~10-100 ms, AC with protection ~0.1-few s [OWN estimate]. Medium to expensive.
Spins needed: 186 lines (IEEE 118) to ~2900 branches (Polish 2383); groups 10-30.
Is mixing the bottleneck? The number of simulator calls is the bottleneck; mixing per call matters.
Best classical competitor: Random Chemistry, Eppstein and Hines 2012, O(log n) simulations per minimal
  contingency found (https://phines.w3.uvm.edu/publications/2012/eppstein_2012.pdf) [LIT]; its risk
  estimator arXiv:1405.4213 [LIT]; SIGHT group testing arXiv:1909.04513 [LIT]; correlated-outage
  extension arXiv:1901.03304 [LIT]; splitting / cross-entropy importance sampling [LIT, recalled];
  and C3 surrogate-PT independence sampler [OWN].
Implementation sketch: surrogate-driven coarse-grained QeMCMC: choose an electrical neighbourhood group
  at random, propose with LODF-surrogate U, accept with the true cascade simulator (MATPOWER-based DC
  then AC). Detailed balance: holds ideally (C2 + P2 single-group). Device: exact sim of 10-20 spin
  groups first, then IBM session batching (one job per step is acceptable when t_E ~ 1 s).
Break-even: per-step ratio ~1.1-6 (C7). Required step advantage vs the BEST classical (Random Chemistry
  and C3) of ~2-6 is within the range the pilot shows at MEDIUM-LOW T vs weak baselines, but unknown vs
  the real competitors [OWN].
Main kill risk: C3: the LODF surrogate is classically trivial to sample with PT at these sizes, and
  Random Chemistry exploits the monotone structure (supersets of a failing set fail) that no Boltzmann
  sampler uses. Also: risk estimation needs unbiased weights, which IS-style methods give directly.
Verdict guess: SIMULATE MORE (the one power-system idea with expensive energy). Likelihood ~5-10%.

Payoff: day-ahead scheduling cost; huge money, but an optimisation problem (C5).

Main kill risk: wrong tool (C5).
Verdict guess: KILL. Likelihood ~0-1%.

B5-10  Cardinality-constrained portfolio sampling (Bayesian / near-optimal portfolio ensembles)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: an ensemble of diverse near-optimal K-asset portfolios (robustness to estimation error), or a
  Bayesian posterior over which K assets to hold.
Temperature regime: target exp(-beta x (risk - mu x return)) on fixed-weight states; asset correlation
  blocks (sectors) give MEDIUM multimodality [OWN].
Mapping: x_i in {0,1}, quadratic covariance term = dense Ising, cardinality = fixed Hamming weight ->
  XY (Hamming-preserving) mixer (C4); weights continuous -> inner QP per subset [OWN].
Energy per step: Markowitz QP over K assets ~10-100 us (K<=50) [OWN estimate]; pure binary version ~us.
Spins needed: 30-2000 assets.
Is mixing the bottleneck? For optimisation no (MIQP/branch-and-bound solves OR-Library instances
  31-225 assets of Chang et al. 2000 [LIT, recalled]). For sampling of near-optimal ensembles, Kawasaki
  swap dynamics is the baseline.
Best classical competitor: MIQP solvers; Kawasaki/swap MCMC with PT; Kawamata et al. arXiv:2604.20701
  is the closest quantum-proposal study (fixed weight, XY mixer + neural surrogate, simulation) [LIT].
Implementation sketch: XY-mixer QeMCMC on a sector block, accept with true QP objective; detailed
  balance holds if the XY Hamiltonian is real symmetric and the block choice is state-independent [OWN].
Break-even: cheap energy -> no hardware case.
Main kill risk: C5 (users want optimisation) and Kawasaki+PT on dense but small instances.
Verdict guess: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. Likelihood ~3%.

B5-11  Interbank default cascade (Eisenberg-Noe clearing) tail sampling
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: systemic-risk stress testing: probability that >X banks default given correlated shocks.
Temperature regime: tilted rare-event target, effectively LOW at the tail; cascade threshold gives
  bimodal outcomes (contained vs systemic) [OWN].
Mapping: initial-shock indicators (which banks are hit) -> spins; Ising surrogate from first-order
  exposure matrix (J_ij = loss transmitted i->j relative to capital) [OWN].
Energy per step: Eisenberg-Noe clearing vector by fixed-point iteration, O(n^2) per iteration, ms for
  n~100-1000 banks [LIT for model: Eisenberg and Noe, Management Science 2001, recalled; OWN timing].
Spins needed: 50-1000 banks.
Is mixing the bottleneck? Rare-event efficiency is; cascades are monotone in the shock set, so
  importance splitting and Random-Chemistry-like shrinkage apply.
Best classical competitor: importance sampling for correlated defaults (Glasserman and Li, Management
  Science 2005) [LIT, recalled]; splitting; C3.
Break-even: ms energy -> S* ~ 20-500 on IBM; not reachable at MEDIUM T [OWN, C7].
Main kill risk: monotonicity exploited classically; data confidentiality means few real networks.
Verdict guess: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. Likelihood ~2%.

B5-12  Flat-histogram (Wang-Landau / multicanonical) tail estimation with quantum proposals
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: a generic rare-event engine: density of states g(E) of an Ising risk model -> tail probabilities
  P(E > e*) at ALL tilts/temperatures from one run (credit loss, compound extremes, outage counts).
Temperature regime: covers all temperatures at once; the hard part is the low-energy end (LOW T) and
  first-order-like barriers [OWN]. This is the brief's "many-temperature ensemble" lever.
Mapping: any Ising risk model; WL accept min(1, g(E)/g(E')) needs only a symmetric proposal, which the
  QeMCMC proposal is [P1 symmetry + OWN]. Note the proposal is temperature-independent [BRIEF], so the
  same proposal kernel serves every energy window.
Energy per step: Ising delta-E, cheap.
Spins needed: 10-20 for exact study; groups for larger.
Is mixing the bottleneck? Yes: WL/multicanonical round-trip time in energy grows fast at first-order
  transitions and in glassy low-energy regions [LIT, recalled: Berg and Neuhaus 1992; Wang and Landau,
  PRL 2001].
Best classical competitor: WL with replica-exchange windows; population annealing; multicanonical with
  cluster moves where couplings are ferromagnetic [LIT, recalled].
Implementation sketch: in the existing pilot code, replace the Boltzmann accept with the WL/multicanonical
  accept (fixed converged weights for the measurement phase so the chain is a proper MCMC with a fixed
  target); measure energy round-trip time quantum vs local vs uniform vs replica-exchange WL on SK and on
  K20/K2-style instances. Detailed balance: holds for the fixed-weight multicanonical phase (symmetric
  proposal, MH accept); the WL learning phase is non-Markov by design and is excluded from claims [OWN].
Caveat: P1 proposals stay close in energy (that is why they are accepted at low T); a flat-histogram walk
  also needs energy-changing moves, so the quantum proposal may help tunnelling within an energy window
  but not diffusion across windows [SPEC]. This is exactly what the experiment would show.
Break-even: cheap energy -> hardware no; value is a regime/benchmark result and a quantum-inspired tool.
Main kill risk: replica-exchange WL already removes most barriers at n<=50; advantage limited to LOW-T
  windows where P1 already claims it (nothing new).
Verdict guess: SIMULATE MORE (1-2 days on existing pilot). Likelihood ~5%; high information value.

Verdict guess: KILL (interesting theory, no practical path). Likelihood ~1%.

B5-15  Grid topology / breaker-status identification posterior (hidden outages from SCADA/PMU)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: detect wrong topology in state estimation after storms or cyber events.
Temperature regime: LOW when measurement redundancy is low (several topologies explain the data equally);
  otherwise the posterior is a single spike [OWN].
Mapping: breaker status spins; surrogate from measurement-sensitivity of each switch [OWN].
Energy per step: weighted-least-squares state estimation per topology ~1-10 ms [OWN estimate].
Spins needed: tens of suspect switches.
Is mixing the bottleneck? No: candidate topologies are few (k<=3 changes from nominal) and are
  enumerated or solved by MILP/sparse methods [OWN].
Verdict guess: KILL. Likelihood ~1%.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B5-16  Distribution-network reconfiguration / restoration sampling
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: diverse restoration plans after outages.
Temperature regime: LOW (sparse feasible set).
Mapping: switch spins with radiality (spanning-tree) global constraint; no Ising encoding without many
  ancilla variables; most proposals infeasible (C4) [OWN].
Energy per step: radial power flow (backward-forward sweep) ~ms.
Best classical competitor: MILP with spanning-tree constraints, branch exchange heuristics.
Verdict guess: KILL. Likelihood ~0%.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B5-17  Logistics: sampling diverse near-optimal routes / schedules (VRP, crew scheduling)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: route diversity for robustness.
Temperature regime: LOW (rugged); but C5 applies (optimisation).
Mapping: permutation one-hot encoding n^2 qubits with two-way one-hot constraints; X mixer infeasible;
  no compact constraint-preserving mixer at scale (C4) [OWN].
Energy per step: route cost cheap; with stochastic simulation medium.
Best classical competitor: LKH/OR-Tools, large neighbourhood search, column generation.
Verdict guess: KILL. Likelihood ~0%.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B5-18  Facility / sensor-network siting with simulation-based cost (Bayesian optimisation over binaries)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: where to place depots, rain gauges, air-quality sensors, substations when each candidate design
  needs a slow simulation (discrete-event logistics sim, hydrological model) of seconds-minutes.
Temperature regime: inside a BOCS-style loop one samples/optimises an acquisition over a sparse Bayesian
  quadratic (Ising) surrogate; acquisition landscapes are MEDIUM [OWN].
Mapping: site spins; surrogate is natively Ising (BOCS: Baptista and Poloczek, ICML 2018,
  arXiv:1806.08838 [LIT, recalled]).
Energy per step: the simulator is called once per BO iteration, not per MCMC step; the MCMC/annealing
  inside the acquisition step runs on the cheap surrogate [OWN].
Spins needed: 20-100 sites.
Is mixing the bottleneck? No: the bottleneck is simulator calls; the inner surrogate sampling is cheap
  classically (simulated annealing / SDP in BOCS).
Main kill risk: the quantum part would only accelerate the cheap inner step.
Verdict guess: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY at best; effectively KILL. Likelihood ~1%.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B5-19  Epidemic contact-network inference (latent edges given infection/recovery times)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: who-could-have-infected-whom networks for outbreak response (households, wards, farms).
Temperature regime: effectively MEDIUM to LOW: sparse epidemic data leave many networks consistent with
  the timeline; consistency (every infected node needs an infectious neighbour) is a hard constraint, so
  the feasible edge set is fragmented [OWN].
Mapping: edge indicators -> spins (n(n-1)/2); prior = Erdos-Renyi/ERGM (Ising-like); likelihood couples
  edges to the transmission tree (multi-body). Groendyke, Welch and Hunter fit a stochastic SEIR on an
  Erdos-Renyi network to recovery times with MCMC (Scand. J. Stat. 2011) [LIT]; dynamic-network data
  augmentation arXiv:1910.04221 [LIT].
Energy per step: epidemic likelihood given network and augmented times, O(edges + events), ~us-ms [OWN].
Spins needed: n=30-200 hosts -> 435-20000 edge spins; only edges among infectious-period overlaps
  matter (much smaller).
Is mixing the bottleneck? Partly; the joint update of transmission tree and edges is the slow part, and
  that is a tree (not Ising) variable.
Best classical competitor: data-augmentation MCMC with tree moves (Groendyke et al.; Fintzi et al.
  arXiv:1606.07995) [LIT].
Main kill risk: hard consistency constraint (C4) and the tree variable, not edges, dominates mixing.
Verdict guess: KILL (QUANTUM-INSPIRED at most). Likelihood ~1%.

KNOWN-K1  Multi-site rain occurrence weather generator (autologistic/Ising, real station data, summer vs winter)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: stochastic weather generator that reproduces joint wet/dry patterns across stations; summer vs
  winter is a natural regime contrast.
Temperature regime: fitted model, so T=1 by construction. Effective regime from the fitted couplings:
  summer convective = short-range, weak couplings, strong local fields -> HIGH; winter frontal = strong,
  long-range positive couplings, near-bimodal "all wet / all dry" days -> MEDIUM (near ordering) [OWN,
  to be measured as the specific-heat-like peak position of the fitted model under a beta sweep].
Mapping: station wet/dry = spin; autologistic = Ising with h from covariates (circulation type) and
  J >= 0 between stations. Literature: autologistic within non-homogeneous HMM, Hughes and Guttorp 1994
  [LIT: https://sites.stat.washington.edu/NRCSE/pdf/trs04_hgc.pdf]; latent-Gaussian alternatives
  (Wilks 1998; arXiv:2003.07998) [LIT].
Energy per step: Ising delta-E ~ns-us. Cheap.
Spins needed: 10-50 stations (exact sim feasible up to ~20).
Is mixing the bottleneck? No. Couplings are ferromagnetic (unfrustrated), so Swendsen-Wang with a ghost
  spin for fields mixes fast even near ordering [LIT, recalled, standard]; fitting by pseudo-likelihood
  needs no sampling (C6). For n<=20 the distribution is exactly enumerable.
Best classical competitor: Swendsen-Wang / Wolff; exact enumeration for n<=20; Gibbs at HIGH T.
Implementation sketch: real station data (e.g. national met service daily records), fit autologistic
  per season by pseudo-likelihood, then run the pilot quantum-vs-local-vs-uniform-vs-SW gap analysis on
  the two fitted instances. Detailed balance: ideal QeMCMC yes (P1); SW yes (standard).
Break-even: cheap energy -> S* ~ 1e5+; no wall-clock route.
Main kill risk: SW mixes ferromagnets near-optimally; no application advantage possible.
Verdict guess: SIMULATE MORE only as an honest real-data regime benchmark (HIGH summer vs MEDIUM winter,
  ferromagnetic non-SK instance, 2-3 days); as an application: KILL. Likelihood of advantage ~1%.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KNOWN-K2  Compound extremes (drought/flood coincidence) at low T or conditioned on an extreme
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: probability of multi-station, multi-day joint dry spells or wet spells (crop failure, flood
  coincidence), and samples of how such events look.
Temperature regime: either tilted (LOW effective T) or conditioned on "fraction wet >= q over D days",
  which adds a hard constraint; the conditioned space-time field is effectively MEDIUM to LOW [OWN].
Mapping: space-time autologistic (stations x days) Ising; conditioning = constraint on magnetisation.
Energy per step: cheap.
Spins needed: 20 stations x 10-30 days = 200-600.
Is mixing the bottleneck? Partly: conditioning on a magnetisation is handled classically by
  multicanonical sampling in the magnetisation (1D order parameter) or by fixed-magnetisation Kawasaki
  plus SW-type clusters [OWN].
Best classical competitor: multicanonical / Wang-Landau in magnetisation; importance splitting; PT.
Implementation sketch: use B5-12 machinery on the fitted K1 model; fixed-magnetisation version needs an
  XY mixer (C4).
Break-even: cheap -> no hardware route.
Main kill risk: ferromagnetic model with a 1D order parameter is the easiest possible case for
  multicanonical methods.
Verdict guess: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (fold into B5-12 as an instance). Likelihood ~2%.

KNOWN-K3  3x4 convective-cell / frost lens
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: a 12-spin lattice picture (convective cells, frost pockets) that fits current hardware.
Temperature regime: whatever is fitted; at 12 spins the notion of regime is weak (no phase transition at
  this size, only a smooth crossover) [OWN].
Mapping: 12-spin nearest-neighbour Ising, 17 bonds; heavy-hex embedding cheap.
Energy per step: trivial.
Spins needed: 12.
Is mixing the bottleneck? No: 4096 states, exact enumeration in microseconds; exact sampling is free.
Best classical competitor: exact enumeration (perfect iid samples).
Implementation sketch: hardware validation of the proposal kernel only: compare measured Q(s'|s) and the
  resulting chain against the exact enumerated target; quantify noise-induced asymmetry (P1 SI issue).
  Detailed balance: ideal yes; on hardware only approximately (test it, that is the point).
Break-even: impossible by construction.
Main kill risk: none as a calibration demo; fatal as an application claim.
Verdict guess: BUILD NOW as a hardware symmetry/noise calibration demo only; KILL as an application.

KNOWN-K19  Grid cascading outages, IEEE 14-bus
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: didactic N-k risk picture.
Temperature regime: tilted rare-event target as B5-08; effectively LOW at high tilt.
Mapping: 20 branches -> 20 spins; LODF surrogate (B5-08).
Energy per step: DC cascade on 14 buses ~0.1-1 ms; AC ~1-10 ms [OWN estimate].
Spins needed: 20.
Is mixing the bottleneck? No: all 2^20 ~ 1e6 outage sets x ~1 ms = ~17 min of DC simulation enumerates
  the whole space exactly; for k<=3 only C(20,1)+C(20,2)+C(20,3) = 1350 cases [OWN].
Best classical competitor: exhaustive enumeration; Random Chemistry at larger scale (B5-08).
Implementation sketch: use only as the validation instance for B5-08 (exact ground truth available).
Break-even: impossible at this size.
Main kill risk: enumerable.
Verdict guess: KILL as an application; keep as B5-08 ground-truth test case (BUILD NOW as a test only).

KNOWN-K20  Credit default contagion (Ising default model, tail loss)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: portfolio loss tail (VaR/ES) with contagion, beyond conditional-independence copula models.
Temperature regime: the long-range Ising credit model shows a first-order-like transition with a jump in
  risk as correlation rises (Molins and Vives, arXiv:cond-mat/0401378) and doubly-peaked loss
  distributions in contagion variants (Molins and Vives, "Model risk on credit risk", arXiv:1502.06984)
  [LIT]. So the tail is a second mode: effectively LOW for the tail event, MEDIUM in the bulk [OWN].
Mapping: obligor default = spin; J = contagion/exposure links; h = individual default propensities.
  Mean-field (all-to-all equal J) in the homogeneous case.
Energy per step: cheap (O(n) delta-E).
Spins needed: 100-10000 obligors.
Is mixing the bottleneck? For mean-field or near-mean-field models no: the state is summarised by one
  order parameter (number of defaults), so exact combinatorics (homogeneous case, solved by Molins and
  Vives) or multicanonical sampling in that 1D variable work; the two modes sit at Hamming distance
  O(n), which a quantum proposal also does not bridge efficiently (transverse-field tunnelling in
  mean-field models is exponentially suppressed in n) [OWN, SPEC on the last point]. Only a sparse,
  heterogeneous exposure network makes it genuinely glassy.
Best classical competitor: exact/semianalytic for homogeneous; Glasserman-Li importance sampling
  (2005) [LIT, recalled]; multicanonical/Wang-Landau; population annealing.
Implementation sketch: B5-12 instance with a sparse heterogeneous exposure network.
Break-even: cheap energy -> no hardware route.
Main kill risk: 1D order parameter makes classical rare-event methods near-optimal.
Verdict guess: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (or KILL). Likelihood ~2%.

KNOWN-K21  Species co-occurrence (Markov network / Ising of presence-absence)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: infer direct species interactions from co-occurrence (Harris, Ecology 2016, doi
  10.1002/ecy.1605, R package rosalia) [LIT].
Temperature regime: fitted ecological couplings are weak relative to site/environment fields -> HIGH
  effective T [OWN; consistent with the field fitting by likelihood/pseudo-likelihood without trouble].
Mapping: species presence = spin, J = interaction, h = environment; exact normalisation up to ~30 species
  [LIT: Harris].
Energy per step: cheap.
Spins needed: 20-300 species.
Is mixing the bottleneck? No (HIGH T; pseudo-likelihood fitting avoids sampling, C6).
Best classical competitor: pseudo-likelihood; Gibbs; exact enumeration below ~30 species.
Verdict guess: KILL. Likelihood ~0-1%.

KNOWN-K22  Roll-call voting blocs (maximum-entropy Ising of votes)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: bloc structure, influence of members; nice public data.
Temperature regime: fitted pairwise maximum-entropy model; Lee, Broedersz and Bialek found a strong pull
  to unanimity, a simple energy landscape with a few local maxima (ideological split, conservative core),
  and couplings of both signs (J. Stat. Phys. 2015, arXiv:1306.5004) [LIT]. For legislatures with party
  discipline: two dominant blocs -> MEDIUM, few modes, mostly ferromagnetic within party [OWN].
Mapping: legislator vote = spin; J from inverse Ising fit.
Energy per step: cheap.
Spins needed: 9 (Supreme Court; enumerable, 512 states) to 100 (Senate) to 435 (House).
Is mixing the bottleneck? No: 9 justices are enumerable; for the Senate two blocs are bridged by PT or
  cluster moves; standard political science uses ideal-point models (Clinton, Jackman and Rivers, APSR
  2004; NOMINATE) where sampling is easy [LIT, recalled].
Best classical competitor: exact enumeration (n=9); PT + Houdayer/Wolff-type clusters for n=100.
Verdict guess: KILL as an application; possible outreach demo (n=9 fits hardware, ground truth exact).
  Likelihood ~1%.

KNOWN-K23  Wildfire lens
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Payoff: burn-probability maps, or inverse problem: infer a binary fuel-dryness map from an observed burn
  scar using a fire-spread simulator.
Temperature regime: forward burn sampling is a percolation-like process best simulated directly (no MCMC
  needed). The inverse posterior over fuel cells given a scar is MEDIUM (only cells near the front are
  constrained, the rest is prior) [OWN].
Mapping: cell fuel state = spin; Ising surrogate = spatial autologistic prior + linearised front
  sensitivity; true energy = simulator mismatch (C2). Burned-area connectivity is a global constraint
  if the burned field itself is the variable (C4).
Energy per step: fire-spread simulator (cellular automaton seconds; physics-based FARSITE-class
  seconds to minutes) [OWN estimate, not verified].
Spins needed: 100-1e4 cells; groups of 10-30 cells near the front.
Is mixing the bottleneck? The number of simulator calls is.
Best classical competitor: C3 surrogate independence sampler / delayed acceptance; ensemble Kalman and
  ABC methods used in fire data assimilation [LIT, recalled].
Break-even: expensive energy -> S* ~ 1.1-6 (C7), but C3 applies and the surrogate is a ferromagnetic
  autologistic field (classically easy to sample).
Main kill risk: C3; also fire inverse problems are usually posed with continuous fuel moisture, not
  binary fields.
Verdict guess: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (formally sound, no structural edge). Likelihood ~2%.

=====================================================================================================
4. THREE STRONGEST CANDIDATES AND WHY
=====================================================================================================
1. B5-01 (+B5-02) Bayesian variable selection / fine-mapping with collinear (LD) blocks. The only problem
   class here where a T=1 posterior is genuinely effectively LOW for a structural, well-understood reason
   (non-identifiable collinear blocks), where the Ising surrogate falls out of the model (h from
   marginal association, J from the correlation/LD matrix), where P2 groups map onto natural blocks,
   where the true energy can be kept in the accept (exact detailed balance in the ideal limit), and
   where the named competitor (Zanella-Roberts TGS, informed proposals, block enumeration) is concrete
   and runnable. Honest expected outcome: quantum-inspired at best; the hard test is TGS + block swap +
   exact block Gibbs.
2. B5-08 Large-grid N-k rare-event sampling with an LODF surrogate. The one idea in this domain with a
   truly expensive energy (cascade simulation, ~seconds per Random-Chemistry trial on the Polish case),
   so S* drops to ~1.1-6 and a step advantage could become wall-clock. Must beat Random Chemistry and
   the C3 surrogate-PT independence sampler; K19 (IEEE 14-bus, enumerable) gives exact ground truth.
3. B5-12 Flat-histogram (multicanonical) tail estimation with quantum proposals. Cheapest to test (swap
   the accept rule in the existing pilot), answers a question no QeMCMC paper has asked (does the
   temperature-independent proposal help an all-T rare-event walk?), and absorbs K2 and K20 as
   instances. High information value even if negative.

Not recommended as applications: unit commitment, routing, portfolio optimisation (optimisation, C5);
BN structure, record linkage, mixtures, reconfiguration (hard constraints, C4); change-point (exact
methods); species, rain, voting (HIGH T or ferromagnetic, solved by SW/pseudo-likelihood/enumeration).
