All numbers marked SIM are own exact calculations with common/qmc.py (exact transition matrices), single SK instances

STATIONARITY METRIC. err = max_s |(pi P)(s) - pi(s)| for the exact chain matrix P and Boltzmann pi. err ~1e-16 =
exact. When err > 1e-10 I also give TV = total variation between the chain's actual stationary distribution and pi
(the size of the bias). For variants that never move some spins, I mixed 50/50 with single-spin local moves so the
stationary distribution is unique (stated where used).

TWO GENERAL RESULTS USED THROUGHOUT (own derivation, checked numerically)
G1. "Surrogate theorem" (proved). Take any real diagonal H_prob, any real symmetric mixer (sum X, XY hopping, a
   shift on qudits), and any time-dependent schedule that is PALINDROMIC (H(t) = H(T-t)). Then U^T = U (the product of
   symmetric matrices in mirrored order), so |<b|U|a>|^2 = |<a|U|b>|^2. The proposal is symmetric, and Metropolis with
   the TRUE energy gives exact Boltzmann stationarity. This holds even when H_prob is not the target energy. A
   constant laser phase (Y terms) is also harmless: it is a diagonal-unitary conjugation and leaves |amplitudes|
   unchanged. Consequence: hardware-native coupling subsets, Rydberg geometry, surrogates, decoupled groups and
   shallow Trotter circuits are all EXACT as long as they are ideal and palindromic. Mixtures of symmetric kernels are
   symmetric. A non-palindromic schedule is NOT symmetric (counterexamples V07, V10, V15).
G2. Noise theorem (proved). Take a Lindbladian with real H and Hermitian Pauli jump operators (X, Y, Z dephasing or
   flips, i.e. unital Pauli noise) and a time-symmetric rate profile. Let T be the transpose map. Then
   T L T = -L_H + D = L^dagger, so Q(a|b) = Q(b|a) exactly. Non-unital noise (amplitude damping T1, leakage, atom
   loss, thermalisation) breaks the symmetry. So does any noise placed non-palindromically, e.g. readout error only at
   the end. SIM n=4: Pauli Lindblad err 1.4e-17 (V12).
G3. Quantum output probabilities Q(s'|s) are NOT computable, either classically (that is the point) or from few shots
   (a specific outcome has probability ~2^-n). So every correction that needs Q values (Hastings for an asymmetric
   kernel, generalized MH with arbitrary kernels, Rao-Blackwell weights) is infeasible beyond toy n. Only schemes where
   Q cancels by symmetry, or where the asymmetry comes from a CLASSICAL computable factor (group-selection
   probabilities), are usable.

COST NUMBERS USED (from BRIEF / A4 / A5): t_Q per sequential step ~2-3 s on IBM (2 s sub-job floor), 30-50 ms best on
D-Wave, 0.1-5 s on Helios (ESTIMATE), 1-10 s+ on neutral atoms; t_C ~30 ns per dense local proposal (n<=100). Break-even
S* ~7e7 (IBM), ~1e6 (D-Wave). IBM batched throughput ~4,000 circuits/s (2 s + 0.35 ms per execution).
Own calc: with N=4,000 chains per job, the cost per chain-step is (2 + 1.4)/4000 ~ 0.85 ms, so S* per chain-step
~0.85 ms / 30 ns ~ 3e4. Batching cuts S* by ~2,000x, but only for many independent chains.
RE-B circuit count: 5-spin SK -> 823 CZ (BRIEF), i.e. ~80 CZ per coupled pair at that Trotter depth.

=====================================================================================================================
V01  Hardware-tuned coarse-grained groups (size, selection rule)
=====================================================================================================================
Change vs P1: quantum proposal on a group g of k spins only. The rest is frozen into an effective field (P2). New
here: k is chosen from the device, and g is chosen (a) uniformly, (b) with state-independent weights (coupling
strength, community/cluster structure, random bond percolation on |J_ij|), or (c) depending on the state (frustration
of the current configuration, Wolff/FK clusters).
Why/regimes: fewer qubits and shallower circuits -> lower noise. Value is LOW T (P2: group exponent 0.64-0.77 vs
local 0.92, T=1). MEDIUM/HIGH: no case (local mixes fast enough).
Detailed balance:
 (a),(b) PROVED. The group's H is real symmetric and depends only on the frozen spins, which are the same in s and s',
   and p(g) does not depend on the state. SIM n=6, beta=1: uniform groups err 5.6e-17; coupling-weighted err 2.2e-16.
 (c) BROKEN if plain Metropolis is used. SIM: frustration-weighted selection p(g|s) ~ exp(lam*F_g(s)): lam=1 err 6.1e-3,
   TV 0.035; lam=3 err 1.6e-2, TV 0.064.
   CORRECTED: treat g as a labelled component and accept with
       A = min(1, [pi(s') p(g|s')] / [pi(s) p(g|s)]).
   The group kernel is symmetric, so the quantum factor cancels. p(g|s') is a cheap classical function, so the
   correction is COMPUTABLE. SIM: err 5e-18 (lam=1), 5.6e-17 (lam=3). But the gap got WORSE: 0.021 and 0.011 vs
   0.026 for uniform groups. Targeting frustrated spins did not help on this instance.
 Wolff/FK-cluster groups: the probability of growing a given cluster C, P(C|s), is a sum over spanning subgraphs
   (reliability-polynomial type, #P-hard in general), so it is not computable. A computable variant uses the bond set
   B as the auxiliary variable: P(B|s) = product over bonds. But P(B|s') = 0 as soon as the quantum move unsatisfies a
   bond inside B, and an internal quantum move does that almost always. Then everything is rejected except global
   cluster flips, which is just Wolff. Verdict: FK-state-dependent groups are effectively useless. Use
   state-independent percolation on |J| instead: exact, and it captures the "strong-coupling cluster" intuition.
Group size from hardware (own calc, rough): V12 gives a fidelity budget of ~1,100 CZ for a 2x step gain at n=8
   (eps2q = 2e-3, global depolarising model). At ~41 k^2 CZ for all-to-all k-spin SK (823/20 per k^2 at k=5), that
   allows k ~ 5 on heavy-hex before SWAP routing, maybe 5-7 on Nighthawk square. V13 then shows k<=16 is classically
   emulated in <0.3 s, which is cheaper than ONE IBM step. So hardware-sized groups are quantum-inspired by
   construction (P2 already says this for small k).
Resources: k qubits, depth ~ r*k^2 two-qubit gates (all-to-all), 1 circuit per step.
Hardware fit: IBM heavy-hex (k<=6), Nighthawk square (k<=8), Helios all-to-all (k up to ~20 by fidelity).
Evidence: PAPER (P2) + SIMULATED (own).  Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (for hardware-feasible k).
Main risk: at feasible k the move is classically simulable faster than the QPU latency.

V02  Valid multi-group sweep (fix of P2's fixed-order sequential sweep)
=====================================================================================================================
Change: P2 Alg. 4 updates groups g1..gm in a FIXED order, with the field updated between groups, and does ONE
Metropolis test at the end. Fixes: (i) pick the forward or reverse order with probability 1/2 each (more generally,
any order distribution closed under reversal, e.g. a uniformly random permutation), then one Metropolis test; (ii) a
separate Metropolis test after each group (Metropolis-within-Gibbs).
Regimes: LOW T (P2 multi-sample exponent 0.50).
Detailed balance: P2 fixed order is BROKEN. Q_fwd = Q1 Q2 has transpose Q2 Q1, which is not equal to Q1 Q2.
   SIM n=6, beta=1: max|Q-Q^T| = 0.107, err 2.0e-2, TV 0.061 (a 6% bias in the distribution).
   Fix (i) PROVED: (Q1 Q2 ... Qm)^T = Qm ... Q1, so the fwd/rev mixture is symmetric. SIM asym 8e-17, err 1.1e-16.
   Uniform random order over 3 overlapping groups: err 1.1e-16.
   Fix (ii) PROVED (each factor is reversible and preserves pi; the composition is not reversible). SIM err 1.1e-16.
   Gaps (n=6): fwd/rev with one test 0.059; systematic per-group test P1P2 0.074 (non-reversible |lambda2| gap);
   random-scan per-group 0.032. Per-group tests give the best gap AND need no change in circuits. Recommended fix:
   per-group Metropolis.
Resources: m sequential circuits per sweep (m ~ sqrt(n)), so the latency cost is m x t_Q per sweep.
Evidence: SIMULATED + REASONED.  Verdict: BUILD NOW (as the correct baseline for any CG study; it is a one-line change).
Main risk: none for validity. The m-fold latency per sweep worsens S*.

V03  Several QPUs proposing on disjoint groups in parallel
=====================================================================================================================
Change: groups g1, g2 (disjoint) get proposals at the same time on different QPUs (or different qubit regions of one
chip), with fields from the current state.
Regimes: LOW T. Parallelism hides latency only if the groups are fully independent.
Detailed balance: naive (Jacobi fields from the old state, one joint test) is BROKEN. The field on g1 contains s_g2,
   which changes in s'. SIM n=6 (+50% local moves for irreducibility): err 4.3e-4, TV 3.1e-3.
   CORRECTED by G1, the "decoupled surrogate": drop the couplings BETWEEN the active groups from the H_prob inside each
   circuit (keep the frozen-spin fields) and accept with the TRUE energy. Each group kernel then depends only on
   frozen spins, so the product kernel is symmetric. SIM err 5.6e-17, gap 0.030. Bonus: with the decoupled surrogate
   you can also run SEPARATE sequential Metropolis tests per group on proposals made in parallel. g2's proposal does
   not depend on g1's spins, so it is not stale when g1 has already moved. Exact.
   Alternative without surrogate error: choose groups with no couplings between them (graph colouring). That only
   works on sparse graphs.
Resources: m QPUs x k qubits, 1 parallel round per sweep.
Hardware fit: one large chip partitioned into regions (IBM 156 q, Nighthawk 120 q) is the cheapest "several QPUs".
Evidence: SIMULATED.  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (acceptance loss from the dropped couplings vs the parallel gain).
Main risk: on dense SK the dropped inter-group couplings are most of the energy, so acceptance collapses. Useful only on
sparse/lattice problems, where classical cluster/PT methods are strongest.

V04  Quantum move as one move type in a classical sampler (mixture, composition, PT cold replicas)
=====================================================================================================================
Change: P = w P_quantum + (1-w) P_local (mixture), or K local sweeps then 1 quantum move (composition), or quantum
moves only in the coldest PT replicas, where local moves and swaps fail.
Detailed balance: PROVED. A mixture or composition of pi-invariant kernels is pi-invariant, and PT stays exact if every
   replica kernel preserves its pi_beta. SIM n=8: mixture w=0.1 err 1.1e-16.
Superadditivity (SIM n=8, beta=1): gap(w)/(w g_q + (1-w) g_l) peaks at 2.07-2.10 for w = 0.1-0.3 (1.56 at beta=0.3).
   Local moves (inside a valley) and quantum moves (between valleys) complement each other.
Best ratio, cost-weighted (own calc): maximise gap(w)/(w R + 1 - w), with R = t_Q/t_C. SIM n=8: for R = 1e2, 1e4, 1e8
   the optimum is w = 0 at both beta=1 and beta=0.3. At small n the local chain is fast enough that ANY quantum
   fraction costs more than it gains.
   General rule: when local moves cannot cross the barrier, gap(w) ~ w g_eff for small w, so the gap per unit time
   ~ g_eff/(R + 1/w). Quantum moves pay off only once g_eff > R g_local, i.e. the same break-even S*. Choose
   w ~ 1/R (spend comparable wall time on both). How to find it in practice: measure the IAT of the slowest
   observable per unit wall time on a w grid in a pilot run.
   Crossover with P1 exponents (k_q 0.264, k_local 0.94, prefactors ignored): n* = log2(S*)/0.676 = 39 (IBM,
   S*=7e7), 30 (D-Wave, 1e6), 22 (batched IBM, 3e4). n=39 full-register QeMCMC needs ~41 n^2 ~ 6e4 CZ, far beyond
   the ~1e3 CZ budget (V12).
   Latency trick (exact): speculative execution. While the QPU processes the proposal from s0, precompute the
   local-move continuation of the reject branch from s0, using independent random numbers. If the quantum move is
   rejected (most of the time at low T: acceptance ~2% in SIM), keep that continuation.
PT placement: the quantum move goes only to the replicas whose local+swap IAT is the bottleneck (lowest T). Cost is
   then t_Q per cold-replica step only.
Regimes: LOW T only. At MEDIUM T PT+cluster already mixes. At HIGH T w*=0.
Evidence: SIMULATED + REASONED.  Verdict: BUILD NOW (simulation harness: this is the honest way to deploy any quantum
   move). On hardware: WAIT FOR HARDWARE.
Main risk: the cost-weighted optimum is w=0 at every size where hardware is usable.

V05  Surrogate-driven proposals (cheap H_prob inside U, true energy in the accept step)
=====================================================================================================================
Change: H_prob = a cheap Ising/QUBO surrogate (fit, truncated couplings, hardware-native subset). The true, expensive
energy is evaluated only in the accept step.
Detailed balance: PROVED by G1 (any real diagonal H_prob gives symmetric U). SIM n=8, beta=1, err <=2.2e-16 for every
   surrogate. Quality model (SIM, one instance; acceptance and gap vs correlation rho(surrogate, E)):
     exact E          rho 1.00  acc .022  gap 3.8e-2
     +0.3 sd noise    rho 0.96  acc .018  gap 4.3e-2
     +1 sd noise      rho 0.73  acc .015  gap 2.4e-2
     +3 sd noise      rho 0.24  acc .004  gap 9.1e-3
     line-only J      rho 0.62  acc .015  gap 1.3e-2 (hardware-native subset)
     fields only      rho 0.29  acc .014  gap 3.3e-3
     unrelated SK     rho 0.18  acc .004  gap 4.1e-3   (local 1.2e-3, uniform 4.3e-3)
   The gap degrades smoothly with rho. An uncorrelated surrogate falls back to uniform-proposal quality.
Classical analogue that may dominate: the "surrogate transition method" (Liu 2001, Monte Carlo Strategies, ch. 9;
   Christen & Fox 2005, J. Comput. Graph. Stat. 14:795). Propose by running ANY reversible classical chain (even PT)
   on pi_surrogate. Its kernel is reversible w.r.t. pi~, so the exact acceptance is
   min(1, pi(s') pi~(s) / (pi(s) pi~(s'))), which is computable. In one QPU latency (~2 s) a CPU runs ~7e7 surrogate
   flips, so the classical surrogate chain can fully equilibrate on the surrogate. The quantum version wins only
   where the surrogate itself is classically hard to sample AND correlates with E. That is a narrow window.
Regimes: the only HIGH-T rationale (expensive energy, t_energy dominates both chains). LOW T for gap.
Resources: as P1, with fewer 2q gates when the surrogate is sparse.
Evidence: SIMULATED + LITERATURE.  Verdict: BUILD NOW (simulation, cheap, and the needed head-to-head vs the
   surrogate-transition method).
Main risk: the classical surrogate-transition MCMC dominates at equal wall time.

V06  Temperature-aware tuning of gamma, t, alpha
=====================================================================================================================
Change: P1 draws gamma ~ U[0.25,0.6], t ~ U[2,20] regardless of T. Use a per-beta setting instead. Valid adaptation:
tune during burn-in and freeze, or diminishing adaptation (Roberts & Rosenthal 2007), or a FIXED random mixture over a
grid (always valid by G1).
Detailed balance: PROVED for any fixed choice and any fixed mixture. Frozen-after-burn-in is exact. Online adaptation
   needs diminishing adaptation + containment. A beta-scaled alpha keeps H real symmetric, so it is exact.
SIM (n=8, 2 SK instances, grid 5 gamma x 3 t-windows x {normalised alpha, beta-scaled alpha}): the best fixed setting
   per beta beats the P1 default by 1.27x/1.30x (beta 0.1), 1.18x/1.12x (0.3), 1.39x/1.16x (1.0), 1.65x/1.32x (3.0).
   The best gamma is mostly 0.45 (consistent with Christmann et al. 2411.17821, gamma ~0.45). The beta-scaled alpha
   was NEVER best. The fixed grid mixture reached 0.53-0.98x of P1 (valid but worse).
Regimes: all, but the gains are <2x, which does not move a break-even of 1e4-1e8.
Evidence: SIMULATED.  Verdict: BUILD NOW as standard practice (tune per beta in burn-in, freeze). Not a research line.
Main risk: none for validity. The gain is too small to matter for wall clock.

V07  Analog neutral-atom (Rydberg) quench as proposal
=====================================================================================================================
Change: U from H = Omega/2 sum X - Delta sum n + sum V_ij n_i n_j (geometry-fixed couplings). The device Hamiltonian
is not the target energy.
Detailed balance: by G1 the Rydberg H is just a surrogate, so the ideal dynamics are EXACT for ANY target energy if
   the pulse is palindromic. SIM n=5 (SK target, beta=1): constant pulse err 1.1e-16; constant laser phase 0.7 err
   5.6e-17; one-way detuning sweep (standard adiabatic-prep pulse) BROKEN err 3.7e-3, TV 0.16; palindromic sweep
   err 6.9e-17; 50/50 mixture of a sweep and its time reverse err 3.5e-17.
   Real device: Rydberg decay and atom loss are non-unital (G2) and the detection errors are asymmetric, so the kernel
   is asymmetric with no computable correction (G3). Partial fix: random gauge (V12) does not apply, because blockade
   physics is not spin-flip symmetric. Bias is unquantified.
Resources: n atoms, 1 task per start state (new programmed initial pattern; A4: <10 shots/s, 1-10 s best per step).
Hardware fit: QuEra Aquila, Pasqal. The geometry restricts the surrogate to unit-disk-like couplings (low rho for spin
   glasses: V05 says rho ~0.3-0.6 gives 3-10x lower gap).
Evidence: SIMULATED (ideal) + LITERATURE (Wilson et al. 2407.13830, simulation only; no hardware QeMCMC found).
Verdict: WAIT FOR HARDWARE (needs per-shot programmable initial states and loss-free detection). KILL for
   cheap-energy problems (S* >1e8).
Main risk: an uncorrectable bias from non-unital noise, plus a 1-10 s latency.

V08  D-Wave reverse annealing proposal (start in s, anneal back to s_p, pause, return)
=====================================================================================================================
Change: the proposal is a reverse anneal with the initial state programmed to s.
Detailed balance: closed-system palindromic reverse anneal is EXACT (G1; SIM n=5 err 5e-15). Open system: model
   Q = (1-lam) Q_closed + lam G^n, with G = Glauber heat bath at beta_phys = 2 on the programmed H (thermalisation
   toward low energy).
     lam   plain Metropolis       "Gibbs-corrected" A=min(1,pi' pi_phys/(pi pi_phys'))   exact Hastings (needs Q)
     0.1   err 1.1e-2 TV 0.066    err 0.135 TV 0.99 (WORSE)                              err 3e-17
     0.5   err 5.4e-2 TV 0.25     err 0.075 TV 0.98 (WORSE)                              err 3e-17
     1.0   err 0.11   TV 0.42     err 3e-17 (exact only if the kernel is purely thermal)  err 3e-17
   With purely thermal sampling but a 3% (of sd) programming error (ICE), the Gibbs correction with the nominal H gives
   TV 0.053.
   So the correction needed is Q(s|s')/Q(s'|s). It is exact only if the device kernel is reversible w.r.t. a KNOWN
   Gibbs state, which is never exactly true (mixed coherent + thermal, freeze-out at s* where H(s*) has a transverse
   field, ICE errors). A partial correction can be much worse than none.
   Estimating the ratio from shots: a given s' has probability ~2^-n, so ~2^n shots per pair are needed
   (exponential). A pseudo-marginal rescue would need unbiased estimators placed inside an extended target. I know no
   worked construction for estimated PROPOSAL densities (speculation: possible in principle via a random-weight
   extended space, but at the same exponential shot cost).
Resources: n qubits (embedding chains for dense problems), ~30-50 ms per step best, ~80 us per extra sample.
Evidence: SIMULATED (toy open-system model) + LITERATURE (Arai & Kadowaki 2502.08060: simulation only; no D-Wave
   hardware QeMCMC found by A3).
Verdict: KILL for exact Boltzmann sampling. A heuristic optimiser use is outside the scope of this review.
Main risk: unknown, uncorrectable bias, the same as using the annealer as a Gibbs sampler.

V09  Shot-level batching: multiple-try Metropolis and multi-proposal (hub) schemes
=====================================================================================================================
Change: extra shots from the SAME circuit are almost free (0.35 ms each on IBM vs the 2 s floor). Use K shots per step.
 (a) MTM (Liu, Liang & Wong 2000, JASA 95:121). Draw y1..yK ~ Q(.|x) (one circuit, K shots). Select yj with
     probability ~ pi(yj): for symmetric Q take lambda = 1/Q, so the weights are w = pi and no Q values are needed.
     Draw K-1 reference points ~ Q(.|yj) (a SECOND circuit from yj), add x, and accept with
     min(1, sum pi(y) / sum pi(x*)).
 (b) Hub multi-proposal (Tjelmeland 2004; Calderhead 2014, PNAS 111:17408). Draw z ~ Q(.|x) (circuit 1), then
     y1..yN ~ Q(.|z) (circuit 2, N shots). The joint Q(x|z) prod Q(yi|z) is exchangeable in {x, y} because Q is
     symmetric. Choose the next state among {x, y1..yN} with probability ~ pi. No Q values are needed.
 (c) Naive best-of-K (pick the lowest energy among K shots, then plain Metropolis): BROKEN.
Detailed balance (SIM n=4, beta=1, exact enumeration K=N=2): MTM err 5.6e-17; hub err 6.9e-18; naive best-of-2 err
   2.6e-2, TV 0.21. Calderhead-type GMH with an arbitrary kernel needs Q values, so it is infeasible (G3).
Efficiency (SIM): gap per step: plain 0.106; MTM 0.173; hub 0.195. Both use 2 sequential circuits, and 2 plain steps
   give 0.201. At equal circuit count there is no gain at K=2. The open question is larger K, where shots are cheap,
   plus the hub's free Rao-Blackwellised estimates over all N+1 points.
Resources: 2 sequential circuit executions per step (2 x latency) + K shots.
Evidence: SIMULATED + LITERATURE.  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (K = 8-1000; cost = 2 latencies + K shot times).
Main risk: the second sequential circuit doubles the latency, which dominates the cost.

V10  Shallow hardware-native circuits (Trotter depth, native coupling subsets, QAOA-like layers)
=====================================================================================================================
Change: replace exp(-iHt) with r first-order Trotter steps, p-layer QAOA-type circuits, or only native couplings.
Detailed balance: PROVED for first-order Trotter with equal steps. U = (D B)^r with D diagonal. The leftmost D only
   multiplies output amplitudes by phases, and the rest, B D B ... D B, is palindromic with symmetric factors, so the
   proposal is symmetric. Second-order splitting is symmetric too. A QAOA circuit with arbitrary angles is BROKEN
   unless the angle sequence is palindromic (Nakano et al. 2305.08789 impose this). Native coupling subsets are
   exact by G1. Gate compilation changes nothing (same unitary).
   SIM n=6, beta=1: Trotter r=1,2,4,8,32: asym <=1.1e-16, err <=1.1e-16. Random-angle p=3 QAOA: err 2.6e-3, TV 0.045.
   Palindromic B A B' A B: err 1.7e-18.
Proposal quality vs depth (SIM n=6): gap r=1 0.021, r=2 0.038, r=4 0.037, r=8 0.030, r=32 0.046, exact 0.047, local
   0.0096. Even r=1 (a single "QAOA layer") is 2.2x local here. Line-only couplings retain ~1/3 of the gap (V05).
Resources: r x |E| ZZ gates (|E| = number of native couplings), with no SWAPs if native couplings only.
Hardware fit: IBM heavy-hex (native subset = heavy-hex subgraph), Nighthawk square lattice, Helios (all-to-all).
Evidence: SIMULATED.  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (gap vs r and vs native fraction, scaling in n; noisy at FakeNighthawk).
Main risk: the shallow circuit's gain may come from short-range moves that classical cluster moves also provide.

V11  Potts / multi-valued variables (one-hot, binary, qudit; mixer choice)
=====================================================================================================================
Change: q-state variables encoded as one-hot (q qubits/site), binary (ceil log2 q), or qudits.
Detailed balance (SIM q=3 Potts on a triangle, 27 feasible states, beta=1):
   one-hot + X mixer, infeasible outcome -> REJECT (stay): exact, err 5.6e-17. But 73% of proposals leak out and are
     wasted (gap 0.032).
   one-hot + X mixer, RESAMPLE until feasible (post-select): BROKEN, err 3.2e-3, TV 0.031 (the normalisation Z(s) is
     state-dependent and not computable).
   one-hot + XY mixer per site (real symmetric hopping, conserves one-hot exactly): exact, err 5.6e-17, zero leakage,
     gap 0.111 (3.5x the X mixer).
   binary encoding, invalid code -> reject: exact, 12% leakage, gap 0.089.
   qudit with a real symmetric shift mixer (X_q + X_q^T): exact by G1 (reasoned).
   For reference, a classical single-site recolouring move has gap 0.122. It beats all of them at this toy size.
Resources: one-hot n q qubits + XY gates (3 CNOT each); binary n log2 q qubits with multi-qubit diagonal terms.
Hardware fit: XY mixer on Helios/IBM; qudits on trapped-ion/qudit devices.
Evidence: SIMULATED.  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (XY one-hot only). Do not use the X mixer or post-selection.
Main risk: no application need demonstrated. The classical heat-bath Potts update is very strong.

V12  Error mitigation vs none: twirling, gauge averaging, symmetric sandwich
=====================================================================================================================
Detailed balance under noise (SIM n=4 SK, beta=1, exact Lindblad, rate in 1/(time unit), t ~ U[2,20]):
   unital Pauli X,Y,Z noise, constant rate:             err 1.4e-17 (EXACT, proved in G2)
   amplitude damping (T1) rate 0.01:                    asym 4.9e-2, err 5.6e-3, TV 0.052
   AD 0.01 + full spin-reversal gauge average (16):     asym 6.9e-4, err 3.5e-5, TV 3.1e-4  (~170x smaller, NOT zero)
   AD 0.05 no gauge / with gauge:                       TV 0.139 / TV 3.0e-3
   ideal U + symmetric readout flips eps=0.02 at the end only (Q B):     err 3.9e-4, TV 2.4e-3 (BROKEN)
   same + matched random INPUT flips with the same eps (B Q B):          err 2.8e-17 (EXACT)
Answer to "does gauge averaging restore exact symmetry?": NO. Counterexample: the gauge-averaged readout noise is a
   symmetric bit-flip channel B, but it acts only at the end, and (Q B)^T = B Q is not equal to Q B. SIM TV 2.4e-3 at
   eps=2% (Heron median readout error ~1.7%, A4). Gauge averaging of T1 also leaves an O(rate^>1) residual (the
   average of e^{t L_g} over gauges is not e^{t <L_g>}).
   Exact fixes: (1) symmetric sandwich: add classical random input flips at the measured readout rate, giving B Q B;
   (2) Pauli twirling makes noise Pauli/unital, but it is exactly symmetric only if the noise profile is palindromic
   (G2). Twirl the forward and mirror halves identically, or use a palindromic circuit layout. Residual non-unital T1
   stays at second order after gauge + twirl.
Where noise turns the move into a uniform jump (own calc, global-depolarising model Q_F = F Q + (1-F) uniform, SK n=8):
   gap(F) is ~linear in F. Keeping a 2x step gain over the best of local/uniform needs F >= 0.11-0.12 at beta = 1-3.
   That is <=1,100 CZ at eps2q = 2e-3. At beta=0.3 (MEDIUM) a 2x gain is never reached, even at F=1. Scaling: the
   advantage factor is ~F g_q/g_u, and g_q/g_u ~ 2^(0.68 n) (P1 exponents), so a 2x gain needs N_CZ < ~0.68 n ln2 /
   eps ~ 240 n. Full-register SK needs ~41 n^2 CZ, so this caps n at ~6 (all-to-all, before routing). P1 measured a 3x
   gap loss on ibmq_mumbai at n<=10 with 1D instances (fewer gates).
Evidence: SIMULATED + REASONED (proofs).  Verdict: BUILD NOW (B Q B sandwich + Pauli twirl + palindromic layout are
   cheap and should be default in any hardware run).
Main risk: T1/leakage residual bias; the fidelity budget caps n far below the break-even n*.

V14  Non-reversible / lifted QeMCMC   [NEW]
=====================================================================================================================
Change: augment the state with a direction sigma in {+,-} and an observable O (energy or magnetisation). From
(s, sigma), draw s' ~ Q. If O(s') - O(s) has sign sigma, do a Metropolis test; on any non-move (wrong direction or
rejection) flip sigma.
Detailed balance: the lifted chain satisfies SKEW detailed balance. PROVED: pi(s') Q(s|s') A(s',s) =
   pi(s) Q(s'|s) A(s,s') with Q symmetric, so the inflow to (s, sigma) equals pi(s)/2 without knowing Q. This
   construction needs NO Q values (unlike the minimal-flip-rate Turitsyn-Chertkov-Vucelja lift, which needs
   sum_s' T(s'|s) and is infeasible by G3). Ties in O are treated as non-moves.
   SIM n=6: err 2.6e-18 (energy), 1.7e-18 (magnetisation).
Efficiency (SIM): mixing time t_mix(TV<0.01): beta=1: lifted 204-210 vs reversible QeMCMC 106 (2x WORSE); beta=0.3: 89-92
   vs 46. The classical lifted local move is also worse (883 vs 492). With acceptance ~2-4%, sigma flips almost every
   step and half the proposals are discarded. Cui et al. 2606.23350 report polynomial gains with a different
   construction (lifted flows over quantum-generated candidate paths), which I did not replicate.
Regimes: LOW T claimed by Cui (sim T=0.1; hardware n=3 at T=10).
Evidence: SIMULATED (our generic lift) + LITERATURE (Cui).  Verdict: KILL our generic lift. SIMULATE MORE only as an
   exact replication of Cui's construction.
Main risk: lifting needs high acceptance, and QeMCMC acceptance is low.

=====================================================================================================================

V21  Quantum proposal over fixed block (super-spin) flips   [NEW]
=====================================================================================================================
Change: partition the spins into STATE-INDEPENDENT blocks b1..bm. Qubit j = "flip block j or not". H_prob(f) =
E(s XOR blocks(f)), and the circuit always starts in |0...0> on m qubits.
Detailed balance: PROVED. From s' = s XOR f, the flip-space Hamiltonian is X^f H_s X^f, so
   <f|U_s'|0> = <0|U_s|f> = <f|U_s|0>. SIM n=6, 3 pair-blocks (3 qubits for 6 spins): err 4.5e-17. The block moves
   alone are not ergodic (gap 0). Mixed 50/50 with local moves: gap 0.057 vs full 6-qubit quantum 0.047 and local
   0.0096 (one tiny instance).
   With state-dependent FK clusters (Swendsen-Wang conditioning on bonds), the cluster-flip conditional target is a
   product of independent cluster fields, which is exactly Gibbs-samplable classically. The quantum move adds nothing.
Resources: m qubits (m << n); dense effective couplings between blocks.
Hardware fit: any; the qubit reduction is n/m.
Evidence: SIMULATED.  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (blocks from known structure: strongly bound pairs, domains, community
   detection).
Main risk: good blocks require prior structure knowledge; the m-block problem is small, so again quantum-inspired.

V24  Latency-hiding mask donation (state-independent quantum flip masks)   [NEW]
=====================================================================================================================
Change: generate flip masks m = r XOR s'_r from quantum runs on REFERENCE states r that are independent of the chain
being updated: a separate one-way replica, a fixed library, or random states. Apply the move s -> s XOR m with
Metropolis. Masks can be produced in bulk (throughput mode, ~4,000 circuits/s) and reused, which removes the per-step
latency.
Detailed balance: PROVED. The mask distribution does not depend on s, so Q(s'|s) = M(s XOR s') = Q(s|s'). This
   includes a finite fixed library reused forever. With a donor chain that never uses the receiver's information,
   the receiver is, conditional on the donor's path, a time-inhomogeneous chain of pi-invariant kernels, so it is
   exact. SIM n=8: err <=4.4e-16.
Efficiency (SIM n=8): beta=1: reference r ~ pi keeps 76% of the full QeMCMC gap (2.9e-2 vs 3.8e-2; local 1.2e-3,
   uniform 4.3e-3); uniform references keep 32%. beta=0.3: 74% / 60%. Classical competitor, a differential-evolution
   mask (r1 XOR r2 with r1, r2 ~ pi from an independent population, ter Braak 2006): gap 6.7e-4 at beta=1 (much
   worse), 6.1e-2 at beta=0.3 (better than the quantum masks).
   Preliminary n-scaling (SIM, B7-checks/mask_scaling.txt; median of 5 SK instances, n=4..10, fitted k in
   gap ~ 2^-kn; small n, weak evidence):
     beta=1.0: P1 quantum 0.32 | mask r~pi 0.42 | mask r uniform 0.71 | local 1.34 | uniform 0.93
     beta=0.3: P1 quantum 0.29 | mask r~pi 0.48 | mask r uniform 0.69 | local 0.58 | uniform 0.86
   So the masks keep most of the scaling gain when references come from pi, and a partial gain with uniform
   references. Caveat: an r ~ pi donor must itself be equilibrated, which is circular on hard instances. Uniform or
   library references avoid that but lose more.
Why it matters: it is the only variant found that changes the COST MODEL. The QPU moves from latency mode (~2-3 s per
   step) to throughput mode (~0.25-0.85 ms per mask, amortised and reusable), so S* falls by ~3,000-10,000x.
Risk to the idea: the masks are state-independent, so at large n a mask learned around r may be useless at s. The
   scaling exponent decides it.
Evidence: SIMULATED.  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (top Wave-4 candidate).
Main risk: the mask-gap exponent converges to the uniform exponent at larger n.

V25  Quantum Houdayer move (quantum proposal on replica-difference clusters)   [NEW]
=====================================================================================================================
Change: two replicas A, B (same or adjacent PT temperatures). Take a connected component C of the difference set
D = {i : A_i != B_i}. Qubits encode x = A_C, with B_C = NOT x forced. H_prob(x) = E(A with x) + E(B with NOT x). The
quantum proposal runs on |C| qubits and accepts with the joint ratio piA piB.
Detailed balance: PROVED. The move keeps D (and therefore the component set) invariant, so the selection is
   state-symmetric. The joint H is real diagonal, so the kernel on C is symmetric (G1). SIM ring n=5 x 2 replicas
   (beta 1.0 and 0.5, 1,024 joint states): err 3.5e-18.
   The naive alternative (a quantum move on A only, inside D) is BROKEN: the reverse group differs and the reverse
   probability is 0.
Regimes: LOW/MEDIUM T on sparse 2D lattices, where classical Houdayer/ICM is the state of the art (A5: 2^16 vs 2^25
   sweeps at N=1024). The quantum version replaces the deterministic cluster exchange with a quantum-chosen partial
   exchange.
Resources: |C| qubits (clusters are small below percolation on 2D; on SK, D percolates, so |C| ~ n/2).
Evidence: SIMULATED (validity only).  Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (gap vs classical Houdayer on 2D EA L<=4).
Main risk: classical ICM already solves 2D EA well. On 3D/SK the clusters percolate and the move needs n/2 qubits.
