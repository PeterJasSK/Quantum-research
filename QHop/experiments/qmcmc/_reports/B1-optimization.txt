B1 REPORT: QeMCMC APPLICATION IDEAS IN OPTIMIZATION AND ENGINEERING DESIGN
Sub-agent B1 (ideation), QHop QeMCMC applied review, 2026-11-20
Scope: scheduling, routing, layout, VLSI, topology/structural design, simulation-in-the-loop design, robust design, design-space
exploration, UQ. Weighted to MEDIUM and HIGH temperature. Plus KNOWN project ideas K4, K5, K6, K7.

Label key used throughout:
  [P1] shown in Layden et al. 2023 (arXiv:2203.12497)
  [P2] shown in Ferguson and Wallden 2025 (arXiv:2405.04247)

  [LIT-V] literature, verified by web search/fetch this session (URL given)

======================================================================================================================
SECTION 0. BOTTOM LINE
======================================================================================================================

1. Almost every scheduling / routing / layout / VLSI idea uses cheap energies (microseconds or less per delta-E), which puts S* at
   1e5 to 1e9 [BRIEF]. On top of that, the variables are permutations or one-hot assignments, so QUBO embedding is heavy, and
   specialised classical solvers (CP-SAT, HGS, LKH, hMETIS/KaHyPar, DREAMPlace) are far ahead. Verdict: KILL or QUANTUM-INSPIRED
   ONLY. Cheap-energy HIGH-T ideas lose on wall-clock every time.

2. The only design family where the break-even maths can work is "simulation-in-the-loop on a natively binary design grid":
   pixelated antennas and metasurfaces, binary topology grids with FEM, pin-fin/heat-sink layouts with CFD, discrete
   circuit-sizing with SPICE. Here t_energy is 0.1 s to hours, so S* falls to about 1 to 2 [OWN, below].

3. The usual way to handle non-Ising true energies is surrogate-driven QeMCMC: a QUBO surrogate goes inside U and the true energy
   goes in the accept step. Detailed balance with respect to the true Boltzmann distribution holds, because the proposal stays
   symmetric for any H_prob [P1 argument]. The approach has two structural problems [OWN]:
   (a) Acceptance scissor. If the surrogate residual has standard deviation sigma and is uncorrelated between the current and
       the proposed state, the stationary acceptance is a = 2 Phi(-beta sigma / sqrt 2): beta sigma = 0.5 gives 0.72, 1 gives
       0.48, 2 gives 0.16, 3 gives 0.034, 5 gives 0.0004. QeMCMC proposals jump far in Hamming distance, so residuals ARE
       decorrelated, which is the worst case. Local single-flip moves see only local residual differences and lose much less.
       Acceptance therefore collapses at LOW T (large beta), which is exactly where the quantum step advantage lives [P1, PILOT].
       At HIGH T acceptance is fine, but the step advantage is about 1.3x and only against weak baselines [PILOT]. Only a
       MEDIUM-T window survives, where both factors are moderate.
   (b) Classical dominance by surrogate transition. When t_energy is seconds or more, a classical computer can afford roughly
       1e6 to 1e9 surrogate-energy steps per true-energy call at no extra wall-clock cost. It can therefore run an inner
       classical chain (or PT) on the surrogate nearly to equilibrium and propose from pi_surr. The MH correction is
       pi(s')pi_surr(s)/(pi(s)pi_surr(s')): surrogate-transition / two-stage MH [LIT-M Liu 2001, Monte Carlo Strategies in
       Scientific Computing] and delayed acceptance [LIT-V Christen and Fox 2005, described in arXiv:1503.00996,
       arXiv:1509.00172]. This is an independence proposal from the surrogate Boltzmann law. It has the same residual-acceptance
       penalty as (a) but gives fully decorrelated proposals, and no symmetric quantum proposal can beat that. So the quantum
       route only wins if pi_surr itself is classically hard to sample within about 1e9 steps of PT plus cluster moves. That
       needs a glassy surrogate at n of several hundred or more at LOW-to-MEDIUM T, which runs straight back into (a). The
       window exists but is narrow.

4. Goldilocks band for t_energy [OWN]. MCMC on the true expensive energy needs about 1e4 to 1e6 energy calls per useful ensemble.
   The band where (i) S* is close to 1 and (ii) MCMC on the true energy is affordable at all is about 0.1 s to 1 min per call:
   SPICE, 2D FEM, fast EM solvers, coarse CFD. Below 0.1 s, S* is too large. Above about 10 min, practitioners do not run MCMC
   on the true energy; they run Bayesian optimisation with about 1e2 calls in total. Koziel-type surrogate antenna optimisation
   uses about 90 to 120 full-wave analyses per design [LIT-V, Sci Rep 2024 and 2023, s41598-024-60749-5, s41598-023-44023-8].
   In that regime the quantum chain has no MCMC to accelerate.

5. Prior art on the "PT plus QeMCMC" combination already exists, so a generic method paper is not novel:
   IBM, Marshall et al., arXiv:2602.06171 [LIT-V]. QeMCMC, warm-start and parallel tempering on 117 qubits for MIS
     (QOBLIB). They "recover global optima" up to 117 variables, but these instances are "solved efficiently by CPLEX", and the
     abstract gives no wall-clock claim.
   Ferguson and Wallden, arXiv:2602.01353 [LIT-V]. QeSA and QePT on SK, with "superior scaling over classical benchmarks"
     (baselines not named in the abstract).
   Any B1 work must be application-specific: an expensive true energy plus a surrogate in U, which neither paper does as far as
   their abstracts show.

Break-even arithmetic used throughout [OWN]: S* = (t_Q + t_E) / (t_C + t_E). With t_C about 1 us and t_Q = 0.1 to 1 s per
QeMCMC step:
  t_E = 1 ms   -> S* = 100 to 1000
  t_E = 1 s    -> S* = 1.1 to 2.0
  t_E = 60 s   -> S* = 1.002 to 1.017
  t_E = 3600 s -> S* about 1.0003
Caveat: S here is the step ratio against the BEST classical method for the same expensive-call budget. For expensive energies
that method is delayed-acceptance or surrogate-transition MCMC, not plain local Metropolis, and the pilot does not measure S
against it.

B1-01  Job-shop scheduling, makespan minimisation (LOW-T reference)
Payoff: factories save makespan and tardiness cost. Industrial value is real but already served by CP solvers.
Regime: LOW. The goal is the single best schedule, so it is an optimisation problem, not sampling.
Mapping: time-indexed QUBO, x_{op,t} one-hot over time slots. Precedence and machine-capacity constraints become quadratic
  penalties. Qubits = ops x horizon: a 10x10 instance with horizon about 1000 needs about 1e5 binaries [OWN]. Penalties are
  dense within each operation and connectivity is poor.
Energy cost: cheap (makespan of a decoded schedule, microseconds).
Spins: full is impossible. A coarse group of one machine's ops times a local time window, about 30 to 100.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. The bottleneck is constraint propagation and bounding, which CP handles. Penalty landscapes
  make every QeMCMC jump infeasible.
Best classical: OR-Tools CP-SAT / IBM CP Optimizer (constraint programming with interval variables) [LIT-M, OR-Tools docs;
  CP-SAT dominates recent JSSP benchmarks].
Implementation sketch: coarse-grained QeMCMC on one machine-window block, rest frozen, Metropolis on penalised energy.
  Detailed balance holds for single-group random-block choice [P2]. Penalty weight tuning dominates.
Break-even: t_E about 1 us, so S* about 1e5 to 1e6. Impossible.
Kill risk: certain.
Verdict: KILL (likelihood of useful quantum advantage < 1%).

B1-02  Nurse / crew rostering: ensemble of near-optimal, diverse rosters (MEDIUM)
Payoff: planners want several roster options of similar cost so they can negotiate staff preferences. A sample of equally good
  but different rosters has soft value.
Regime: MEDIUM. T is chosen so that the mean cost is within a few percent of optimal and the ensemble keeps diversity (same
  logic as MGA in energy planning [LIT-V, arXiv:2206.08637, CMU Open Energy Outlook]).
Mapping: x_{nurse,day,shift} one-hot per (nurse, day) Potts variable. Coverage = quadratic penalties per (day, shift). Rest
  rules = pairwise penalties. 30 nurses x 28 days x 4 shifts gives about 3,400 binaries and many one-hot penalties [OWN].
Energy cost: cheap (microseconds).
Spins: full about 3e3. Coarse group = one nurse's month (about 112 binaries) or a 7-day slab.
Mixing really the bottleneck? Partly. One-hot and coverage constraints freeze single flips, but the classical answer is swap
  moves (exchange shifts between two nurses), which preserve coverage exactly.
Best classical: CP-SAT / MIP for optimum, plus MGA-style "solve again with a distance constraint" or tabu search with swap
  neighbourhoods [LIT-M, Burke et al. nurse rostering review 2004].
Implementation: coarse-grained QeMCMC on one nurse-week group, Potts one-hot embedded with penalty. Detailed balance holds if
  the group is chosen at random and accept is per group [P2].
Break-even: S* about 1e5 or more. Lose.
Kill risk: swap-move classical MCMC mixes well within the feasible manifold.
Verdict: KILL (as quantum), <1%.

B1-03  University course timetabling (ITC-2019 style), LOW reference
Payoff: universities save scheduling labour. Instances are large (thousands of classes).
Regime: LOW (optimisation).
Mapping: class x (room, time) one-hot. Thousands of classes x hundreds of options gives 1e5 to 1e6 binaries.
Energy cost: cheap.
Spins: infeasible full. Coarse group = one course's sections.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. Feasibility search dominates.
Best classical: MIP and MaxSAT were among the ITC-2019 finalists; a MaxSAT tool (UniCorT) made the top five and a MIP model
  placed second [LIT-V, Müller, Rudová and Müllerová, J. Scheduling, doi 10.1007/s10951-023-00801-w; UniCorT paper
  s10951-021-00715-5].
Implementation: none worth building.
Break-even: S* 1e5 or more.
Kill risk: certain.
Verdict: KILL, <1%.

B1-04  Vehicle routing under demand uncertainty: posterior ensemble of route plans (MEDIUM)
Payoff: fleet operators want robust route sets. Sampling plans by a Boltzmann weight of expected cost gives hedged options.
Regime: MEDIUM (ensemble). LOW if just optimising.
Mapping: permutation encoding x_{customer,position,vehicle} needs N^2 K binaries. 100 customers x 10 vehicles gives 1e5
  binaries, with dense row/column penalties [OWN].
Energy cost: cheap for a deterministic cost. Medium (ms) if expected cost is estimated by scenario sampling.
Spins: infeasible full. Coarse group = one route's positions (about 10^2).
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. Route-space local moves (2-opt, relocate, SWAP*) are excellent; the permutation encoding
  wastes the quantum proposal on infeasible states.
Best classical: HGS-CVRP (Vidal 2022), "largely outperforms all other existing CVRP algorithms" [LIT-V, arXiv:2012.10384].
  LKH-3 [LIT-M].
Implementation: not recommended.
Break-even: S* 1e3 (scenario cost) to 1e6.
Kill risk: encoding overhead plus best-in-class heuristics.
Verdict: KILL, <1%.

B1-05  Facility layout / quadratic assignment (QAP)
Payoff: shorter material flow in plants and hospitals.
Regime: LOW.
Mapping: QAP is natively a quadratic form over a permutation matrix, so the QUBO is natural, but it needs N^2 binaries plus 2N
  one-hot penalties. N = 30 gives 900 binaries, all-to-all.
Energy cost: cheap (delta-cost of a swap is O(N)).
Spins: full 900 for N = 30. Coarse group = a block of 4 to 6 facilities (16 to 36 binaries).
Mixing really the bottleneck? Partly at large N (rugged landscape). The classical swap neighbourhood is already constraint
  preserving.
Best classical: robust tabu search (Taillard 1991) and iterated tabu [LIT-M]. QAPLIB best-knowns [LIT-M].
Implementation: coarse-grained Potts-to-QUBO block moves. Detailed balance as in P2 for random block choice.
Break-even: S* about 1e5 or more.
Kill risk: certain.
Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY (block-permutation moves sampled classically), <1% quantum.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1-06  VLSI / hypergraph netlist bipartitioning (min-cut, balanced)
Payoff: EDA flow quality (wirelength, timing) via better cuts.
Regime: LOW (optimisation). Possibly MEDIUM for "ensemble of good partitions" used in placement robustness, but no industrial
  demand for that.
Mapping: the most natural Ising in this list. s_i = side, cut edges = ferro J terms, balance = (sum s_i)^2 penalty, dense
  all-to-all. Hyperedges need clique expansion or aux spins.
Energy cost: cheap (microseconds).
Spins: industrial netlists have 1e5 to 1e7 cells. Coarse group = 10 to 30 cells. Multilevel coarsening already does the coarse
  graining classically.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. Multilevel coarsening plus FM refinement solves it near-optimally in seconds.
Best classical: hMETIS (Karypis et al. 1999) and KaHyPar (Schlag et al.) [LIT-M; not verified this session].
Implementation: none.
Break-even: S* 1e6 or more.
Kill risk: certain.
Verdict: KILL, <1%.

B1-07  VLSI global placement
Payoff: chip area and timing.
Regime: LOW (continuous optimisation).
Mapping: placement is continuous (x, y per cell), solved analytically with density penalties. Discretising onto bins gives
  Potts with millions of variables.
Energy cost: cheap per cell move.
Spins: 1e6 cells. Not mappable.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. Gradient-based analytic placement wins.
Best classical: DREAMPlace (GPU, about 40x faster than RePlAce at equal quality; DAC 2019 best paper) [LIT-V, NVIDIA research
  page, doi 10.1145/3316781.3317803].
Verdict: KILL, <1%.

B1-08  Binary topology optimisation, compliance minimisation with FEM (LOW reference)
Payoff: lighter, stiffer parts (aerospace brackets, additive manufacturing).
Regime: LOW (single optimum).
Mapping: rho_e in {0,1} per element. Compliance c = f^T K(rho)^-1 f is NOT quadratic in rho. QUBO only via first- and
  second-order sensitivity expansion (local surrogate), plus a volume penalty (sum rho - V)^2.
Energy cost: medium to expensive. One FEM solve on a 2D 60x20 to 200x100 grid takes ms to about 1 s; large 3D grids take
  minutes to hours (giga-voxel runs used thousands of CPU cores [LIT-M, Aage et al., Nature 550, 84, 2017]).
Spins: 1e3 to 1e9 elements. Coarse groups about 30.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. Adjoint sensitivities make gradient methods (SIMP plus MMA, Bendsoe and Sigmund [LIT-M]) or
  sequential integer programming (TOBS) vastly more call-efficient than any MCMC [LIT-V, Sivapuram and Picelli 2018,
  doi 10.1016/j.finel.2017.10.006; TOBS GitHub renatopicelli/tobs].
Prior quantum work: quantum-annealing topology updates with classical FEM (Sukulthanasorn et al., arXiv:2406.18833; Ye, Qian and
  Pan, arXiv:2301.11531) [LIT-V titles via search snippet]. QeMCMC would add nothing new for pure optimisation.
Break-even: per-call S* about 1 to 2 for large FEM, but MCMC needs orders of magnitude more FEM calls than gradient methods.
Kill risk: gradient-based methods dominate by call count.
Verdict: KILL as an optimiser, <2%.

B1-09  Diverse near-optimal topology ensembles (FEM in accept, sensitivity-QUBO in U), MEDIUM
Payoff: designers get a family of structurally distinct designs within X% of optimal compliance, for manufacturability and
  aesthetic choice and as robustness hedges. Same rationale as MGA [LIT-V, arXiv:2206.08637].
Regime: MEDIUM. T is set so the mean compliance is about 1.02 to 1.10 x optimum with high diversity. This is likely near the
  "design glass crossover": too cold freezes on one topology, too hot gives grey or porous junk [SPEC].
Mapping: H_prob = linearised compliance sensitivities h_e plus pairwise J_ee' from a local second-order or factorisation-machine
  fit, plus the volume penalty. Dense penalty. Connectivity on hardware needs SWAP routing for neighbour plus penalty terms.
Energy cost: 2D FEM about 10 ms to 1 s per call [SPEC, typical for 1e4 to 1e5 DOF direct solve]; 3D minutes or more.
Spins: full 1e3 to 1e4 (2D coarse mesh). Coarse group 20 to 50 elements (a local patch), rest frozen with exact effective
  fields from the frozen state.
Mixing really the bottleneck? Plausibly yes at MEDIUM. Single-element flips create checkerboards or disconnect members, so
  topological changes such as opening a hole or moving a member are multi-element moves. This is the kind of collective move a
  quantum patch proposal can supply [SPEC].
Best classical: (i) delayed-acceptance MCMC with patch moves and a surrogate first stage [LIT-V, Christen and Fox via
  arXiv:1503.00996]; (ii) surrogate-transition with an inner classical PT on the sensitivity-QUBO [OWN, Section 0.3b];
  (iii) MGA/niching GA or MAP-Elites quality-diversity [LIT-V, Mouret and Clune, arXiv:1504.04909].
Implementation (DA-QeMCMC on a patch): random patch g; QPU prepares |s_g>, applies exp(-iHt) with H_prob = patch-QUBO surrogate
  (effective fields from the frozen rest); measure s'_g. Stage 1 accept on the surrogate ratio; stage 2 run FEM and accept on
  pi(s')pi_surr(s)/(pi(s)pi_surr(s')).
  Detailed balance: the patch proposal is symmetric [P1/P2 argument], and DA preserves reversibility [Christen and Fox], so the
  chain is exact w.r.t. the true pi. Noise breaks symmetry, so twirling is required [P1 SI].
  Device: gate-based, 20 to 50 qubits. FEM on CPU/GPU in parallel across chains.
Break-even: t_E 0.1 to 1 s gives S* about 1.1 to 2. Needs an S of about 2 or more over DA-with-classical-patch-moves. The pilot
  only shows 2.8x against local/uniform at n = 10 [PILOT].
Kill risk: (1) classical patch/cluster moves plus DA plus surrogate-transition already mix well; (2) surrogate error on compliance
  is large for topological changes (sensitivities are local), so beta sigma is greater than 1 and acceptance is below 0.5
  [OWN, Section 0.3a]; (3) designers may not want Boltzmann-weighted ensembles at all; MAP-Elites archives are more interpretable.
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (classical emulation at group size 20 to 30 is cheap), about 10% chance of a defensible advantage claim.

B1-10  Pixelated antenna / metasurface binary design ensembles with full-wave EM in the accept step (MEDIUM), strongest new idea
Payoff: RF engineers (5G/6G handset antennas, RFIDs, reconfigurable intelligent surfaces) get a set of distinct pixel layouts that
  all meet S11 and bandwidth specs. That allows choice by manufacturability, SAR and coupling. Each layout costs minutes of EM
  solver time, so cutting the expensive calls per independent design matters.
Regime: MEDIUM. The cost is a spec-violation penalty (dB above target across the band). T is chosen so that the sample sits in
  the "meets spec plus small slack" shell with diversity. LOW only if pure optimisation.
Mapping: natively binary (metal / no metal per pixel), no one-hot, no aux spins. H_prob = factorisation-machine QUBO surrogate
  fitted to previous EM calls. This is exactly the FMQA surrogate, which was successfully used with D-Wave for metamaterial
  design [LIT-V, Kitai et al., PRR 2, 013319 (2020), arXiv:1902.06573]. The FM gives dense J. A low-rank FM (k = 8 to 16) can
  be compiled as a sum of squares [SPEC].
Energy cost: expensive. Full-wave EM runs from "dozens of seconds up to several hours" per geometry [LIT-V, Koziel et al., Sci Rep
  2023/2024]. Typical pixel-antenna runs about 1 to 30 min [SPEC].
Spins: full 64 to 400 pixels (8x8 to 20x20) [SPEC, typical pixel-antenna sizes]. Full QeMCMC fits current devices at the low
  end. Coarse group 16 to 25 pixels.
Mixing really the bottleneck? Partly. EM response is highly non-local (a single pixel can short a resonant path), so the true
  landscape is rugged. But the real bottleneck is the CALL BUDGET: practitioners run 1e2 to 1e3 calls, not 1e5.
Best classical: (i) FMQA/BOCS-style Bayesian optimisation for optimisation [LIT-V, BOCS arXiv:1806.08838; FMQA]; (ii) for
  ensembles: surrogate-transition MCMC with inner classical PT on the FM surrogate (for n <= 400 a classical PT on a dense QUBO
  mixes in well under a second at MEDIUM T [SPEC]); (iii) quality-diversity (MAP-Elites) over antenna descriptors [LIT-V
  arXiv:1504.04909]; (iv) D-Wave annealer sampling the FM QUBO as a proposal (this IS FMQA).
Implementation: (a) fit FM on the current archive; (b) DA-QeMCMC: QPU proposal from exp(-iHt) with H_prob = FM, first-stage
  accept on the FM energy, second-stage accept with EM; (c) add the new EM result to the archive and refit the FM every k steps.
  Refitting between steps makes the chain ADAPTIVE: exact stationarity needs diminishing adaptation or fixed refit epochs with
  the chain restarted [LIT-M, Roberts and Rosenthal adaptive MCMC]. Within an epoch, detailed balance holds (symmetric proposal
  plus DA).
  Device: gate-based, 64 to 100 qubits (full) or 16 to 25 (coarse). EM: commercial or open FDTD (e.g., openEMS [SPEC]) on a
  cluster.
Break-even: t_E 60 s to 1 h gives S* 1.0003 to 1.02. Any real step advantage over the best classical proposal becomes
  wall-clock. But the budget arithmetic is binding: at t_E = 10 min and 1e4 calls you need 70 CPU-days; parallel chains make it
  feasible on a cluster [OWN].
Kill risk: (1) the classical surrogate-transition proposal is an independence draw from pi_FM at n <= 400 and cannot be beaten
  by a symmetric local-ish quantum proposal unless pi_FM is classically hard; at MEDIUM T and n <= 400 it is very likely easy
  [SPEC]. (2) FM residual error on EM responses is large (beta sigma > 1 means acceptance < 0.5) [OWN]. (3) Engineers buy BO,
  not ensembles.
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE. It is the cleanest testbed for the surrogate-driven hypothesis: native binary, a published FM-QUBO
  pipeline exists, and the expensive energy is real. Expect a negative or neutral result against surrogate-transition. About
  10 to 15% chance of a positive, publishable "MEDIUM-T expensive-energy" demonstration. Even a clean negative is a result.

B1-11  Heat-sink / pin-fin / microchannel cooling layout on a binary grid with CFD-in-the-loop (MEDIUM)
Payoff: thermal engineers (power electronics, data centre cold plates) trade pressure drop against peak temperature. Diverse
  layouts help manufacturability.
Regime: MEDIUM (ensembles). LOW for the single optimum.
Mapping: pin present / absent per site (binary). FM or sensitivity surrogate QUBO. Volume/count constraint penalty.
Energy cost: expensive. CFD with conjugate heat transfer runs minutes to hours; 3D cases can need "several hundreds of CPU hours"
  (car-body example) [LIT-V, search snippet, arXiv:2505.09088 / related; per-case cost varies widely].
Spins: 50 to 500 sites.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. The call budget is (1e2 to 1e3 calls at most for 3D CFD).
Best classical: Bayesian optimisation / adjoint-based CFD topology optimisation (density-based fluid topology optimisation)
  [LIT-M, Borrvall and Petersson 2003].
Implementation: same DA-QeMCMC as B1-10.
Break-even: S* about 1, but MCMC is unaffordable when t_E is hours (outside the Goldilocks band, Section 0.4).
Kill risk: no MCMC use case at that cost; adjoint gradients are better.
Verdict: KILL for 3D CFD. Keep only as a 2D coarse-CFD variant of B1-10. <3%.

B1-12  Discrete analog-circuit sizing with SPICE in the loop: yield-aware ensembles (MEDIUM)
Payoff: analog designers choose E-series component values and switch topologies. A Boltzmann ensemble over spec-violation gives
  robust, diverse candidate sizings.
Regime: MEDIUM.
Mapping: each component = Potts over 12 to 48 E-series values (one-hot) or binary-coded index (log2 bits, cheaper, but adjacency
  is distorted). Surrogate = FM over binary codes.
Energy cost: medium. One SPICE AC/transient on a small analog block about 1 ms to 1 s [SPEC]. With Monte Carlo yield
  (100 to 1000 samples per design), 0.1 s to 10 min.
Spins: 20 components x 4 to 6 bits = 80 to 120 binaries.
Mixing really the bottleneck? Unclear. The landscape is moderately rugged but low-dimensional; classical evolutionary and BO
  methods do fine.
Best classical: BO / evolutionary sizing (WEIBO-style BO for analog sizing [LIT-M]); commercial optimisers in Cadence and
  Synopsys.
Implementation: DA-QeMCMC, binary-coded. Detailed balance as in B1-10.
Break-even: plain SPICE (t_E about 1 to 10 ms) gives S* about 10 to 1000, which loses. Yield-aware (t_E 1 s or more) gives S*
  about 1.1 to 2.
Kill risk: industrial flows are BO/GA; low n means the surrogate is classically trivial to sample.
Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY, <3%.

B1-13  Bayesian-optimisation acquisition step on combinatorial domains (BOCS/FMQA Thompson sampling), MEDIUM to LOW
Payoff: fewer expensive black-box calls in combinatorial BO (materials, antenna, molecules).
Regime: Thompson sampling draws surrogate coefficients from the posterior, then MINIMISES the drawn QUBO (LOW-T optimisation of a
  cheap QUBO). A "Boltzmann acquisition" (sample from exp(-beta acq)) is a MEDIUM-T variant.
Mapping: natively QUBO (BOCS second-order model, FM) [LIT-V, arXiv:1806.08838].
Energy cost: the acquisition function is cheap (a QUBO evaluation, microseconds); only the outer black box is expensive.
Spins: 20 to 400.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. Acquisition optimisation on n <= 400 dense QUBOs is solved well by SA/tabu in milliseconds.
  The BO loop is bounded by black-box calls, not by acquisition quality.
Best classical: SA / SDP relaxation (BOCS) [LIT-V]; simulated bifurcation / digital annealer [LIT-M]; p-bit FPGA Ising machines
  (5000 p-bits, flips/s scaling linearly with size) [LIT-V, Aadit et al., Nature Electronics 2022, arXiv:2110.02481].
Implementation: QeMCMC as the inner acquisition sampler. Exactness w.r.t. the acquisition Boltzmann law holds (symmetric
  proposal), but it is irrelevant to the outer BO.
Break-even: the inner energy is cheap, so S* about 1e5 or more per acquisition step. Quantum only matters if better acquisition
  optima reduce the outer call count, which is unproven and probably weak.
Kill risk: the cheap inner problem is dominated by classical Ising machines.
Verdict: KILL (as QeMCMC). Leave FMQA-with-annealer to D-Wave-type work. <2%.

B1-14  PT-embedded quantum move for expensive-energy design ensembles (methodological core for B1-09/10)
Payoff: one machinery serves all expensive-energy binary design problems with a many-temperature ensemble: optimisation via
  the cold replica, plus diversity and robustness via warm replicas.
Regime: multi-T, from HIGH (hot replicas) through MEDIUM to LOW. The quantum move is only applied where it helps per the pilot
  (beta >= critical: 2.8x to 39x steps vs local/uniform [PILOT]). Hot replicas use cheap uniform proposals.
Mapping: as B1-10 (FM-QUBO in U).
Energy cost: every replica pays t_E per step, so M replicas cost M x t_E. Expensive.
Spins: 64 to 400 (full) or coarse groups.
Mixing really the bottleneck? In the cold replicas, yes by construction. But PT plus replica-exchange cluster moves (Houdayer /
  ICM) is the classical gold standard and speeds thermalisation by at least an order of magnitude [LIT-V, Zhu, Ochoa and
  Katzgraber, PRL 115, 077201 (2015), arXiv:1501.05630].
Best classical: PT plus ICM (Houdayer) [LIT-V]; population annealing [LIT-M, Machta 2010]; with expensive energy add a DA first
  stage.
Implementation: M replicas, quantum moves only for the coldest K replicas (K = 1 to 3), DA first stage on FM, exchange swaps
  classical. Detailed balance: each replica kernel is reversible (symmetric proposal plus MH, or DA). Swap moves are standard
  reversible PT. The composite is exact if kernels are applied in random order or as a symmetric (palindromic) sweep [OWN;
  standard PT theory].
Break-even: as B1-10. The QPU serves K replicas, so latency is shared.
Kill risk: prior art (IBM arXiv:2602.06171 QeMCMC plus PT; Ferguson and Wallden arXiv:2602.01353 QePT) [LIT-V] leaves novelty
  only in the expensive-energy/surrogate twist. ICM is very strong.
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (bundle with B1-10, not standalone), about 10%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1-15  Nested surrogate transition with a QUANTUM inner chain (quantum samples pi_surr; outer exact correction with true energy)
Payoff: the same users as B1-10. This is the only variant that directly attacks the Section 0.3b dominance argument.
Regime: the inner chain targets pi_surr at the design temperature (MEDIUM to LOW). The outer chain is an exact MH
  independence-like correction.
Mapping: FM/QUBO surrogate, natively binary.
Energy cost: expensive (outer). The inner surrogate energy is cheap, but each QPU inner step costs 0.1 to 1 s [BRIEF].
Spins: 100 to 1000 (needs pi_surr to be classically hard, so it must be large and glassy).
Mixing really the bottleneck? Only if pi_surr is classically hard. During one outer call of t_E = 1 h, classical PT does about
  1e9 to 1e12 surrogate flips on a GPU/p-bit [OWN from BRIEF rates], while the QPU does about 3e3 to 3e4 inner steps.
  The quantum inner chain needs a step advantage of about 1e5 to 1e8 against PT plus ICM on the same surrogate.
Best classical: inner PT plus ICM on the surrogate [LIT-V, arXiv:1501.05630].
Implementation: inner QeMCMC for k steps from the current s (reversible w.r.t. pi_surr, symmetric proposal plus Metropolis on
  E_surr), propose the endpoint s', outer accept min(1, pi(s')pi_surr(s)/(pi(s)pi_surr(s'))). Detailed balance: holds for ANY
  k because the inner kernel is pi_surr-reversible [OWN derivation; same structure as surrogate transition, LIT-M Liu 2001].
  Noise breaks inner reversibility, so twirling is needed, and the residual bias is NOT corrected by the outer step [OWN].
Break-even: requires the quantum inner advantage to exceed about 1e5 steps against PT plus ICM at n of hundreds. The pilot gap
  exponents (k_q 0.264 vs local 0.94 [P1]) would give 2^(0.68 n) against LOCAL, which is enormous at n = 100, but nothing is
  known against PT plus ICM, and hardware gaps were 3x below ideal at n = 10 [P1].
Kill risk: the acceptance scissor. A glassy, classically hard surrogate means LOW T, which means beta sigma is large and outer
  acceptance collapses (Section 0.3a).
Verdict: WAIT FOR HARDWARE (logical qubits, n = 200 to 1000, fast feedback). Worth one page in the review as the strongest
  conceptual form of the surrogate idea. About 3% in this decade.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1-16  Robust design under uncertainty: sampling uncertain parameters (HIGH)
Payoff: expected-performance and chance-constraint evaluation for a fixed design.
Regime: HIGH or temperature-free. You sample the input uncertainty distribution (often independent, known); this is plain
  forward Monte Carlo, not Boltzmann sampling.
Mapping: parameters are continuous (tolerances, loads, material properties), so there is no natural Ising. Discretising into bits
  gives Ising terms that encode nothing physical.
Energy cost: expensive per sample (a solver run), but samples are already independent: tau = 1.
Spins: n/a.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. Independent sampling has tau = 1, and no proposal can beat that.
Best classical: (quasi-)Monte Carlo, multilevel MC, polynomial chaos, Gaussian-process surrogates [LIT-M, Giles 2008 MLMC].
  The quantum route here would be amplitude estimation, which is out of QeMCMC scope.
Verdict: KILL, 0%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1-17  Rare-failure UQ on discrete component states: power-grid / network N-k contingency sampling near the failure boundary
  (MEDIUM)
Payoff: grid operators estimate the probability of cascading failure and find diverse critical contingency sets. Each sample is
  a power-flow solve.
Regime: MEDIUM-like. The target is the failure-conditioned distribution pi(s) proportional to prod p_i^{s_i} times
  1[failure(s)] (or a tilted soft version). The constraint boundary makes local chains sticky, which is the "mixing across hard
  constraints" case.
Mapping: s_i = component outage (binary). Prior = independent fields h_i. Failure indicator = non-quadratic. The surrogate in U is
  a pairwise "joint criticality" QUBO from DC power-flow line-outage distribution factors (LODF) [SPEC, from memory: LODFs give
  linear pairwise outage effects].
Energy cost: DC power flow about 1 ms; AC power flow plus cascade simulation about 0.1 to 10 s [SPEC].
Spins: 100 to 3000 components; in practice failure sets touch about 2 to 10 components, so states are sparse.
Mixing really the bottleneck? Yes for naive MCMC, but subset simulation and splitting already address rare events.
Best classical: subset simulation (Au and Beck 2001) [LIT-M]; cross-entropy importance sampling [LIT-M]; exact N-k enumeration
  with LODF screening for k <= 3.
Implementation: QeMCMC with H_prob = LODF pairwise QUBO (biased toward co-critical outages), accept on the true cascade
  simulator. Detailed balance holds (symmetric proposal, true-energy accept). As a subset-simulation level kernel, it plugs
  in as the MCMC step.
Break-even: AC cascade (t_E about 1 s) gives S* about 1.1 to 2. DC gives S* 100 or more.
Kill risk: sparse failure sets mean that most QeMCMC proposals (many flips) are far outside the prior mass. A Hamming-weight
  control would be needed, and the proposal can no longer be the plain transverse field [SPEC]. Classical
  enumeration plus screening is very good for small k.
Verdict: SIMULATE MORE (cheap classical emulation; niche but real expensive-energy, constraint-boundary case), about 5%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1-18  Density of states of a design space (how many designs reach each performance level): multi-T or flat-histogram (HIGH
  through LOW)
Payoff: design-space "illumination" for early-stage concept decisions (e.g., how rare are designs meeting spec X).
Regime: all T; the flat-histogram walk spends much time at HIGH and MEDIUM energies.
Mapping: any binary design QUBO.
Energy cost: cheap (if the design objective is a QUBO) or expensive (simulator).
Spins: 50 to 500.
Mixing really the bottleneck? At low-energy tails, yes. Elsewhere no.
Best classical: Wang-Landau, nested sampling, population annealing with free-energy estimates [LIT-M]. The quantum proposal is
  temperature-independent [BRIEF], so it fits a flat-histogram acceptance rule unchanged.
Implementation: QeMCMC proposal plus Wang-Landau acceptance g(E)/g(E'). Proposal symmetry makes the WL ratio correct. WL itself
  is non-Markov (adaptive); convergence follows the standard 1/t schedule [LIT-M, Belardinelli and Pereyra].
Break-even: cheap energy gives S* 1e5 or more (lose). Expensive energy: WL needs 1e6 or more calls, unaffordable.
Kill risk: there is no affordable regime.
Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY, <2%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1-19  Microstructure reconstruction (two-point / lineal-path correlation matching on a binary voxel grid), MEDIUM to LOW
Payoff: materials and geoscience engineers build statistically equivalent microstructures (porous media, composites) for
  simulation. An ensemble of reconstructions is needed for UQ.
Regime: the classical method is simulated annealing to near-zero mismatch (LOW). Ensembles at small but finite T (MEDIUM-LOW).
Mapping: binary voxels. The two-point mismatch is a quartic function of occupancies (squared difference of pair statistics), so a
  QUBO needs aux spins or a local quadratic approximation.
Energy cost: cheap with incremental updates (O(L) per swap).
Spins: 1e4 to 1e7 voxels. Coarse groups.
Mixing really the bottleneck? At LOW T, partly; classical uses swap moves (volume-preserving) and multi-grid.
Best classical: Yeong-Torquato simulated annealing reconstruction (PRE 1998) [LIT-M]; deep-learning generators [LIT-M].
Break-even: S* 1e5 or more.
Verdict: KILL, <1%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1-20  HIGH-T diverse random design generation for ML training sets (e.g., random metasurfaces / truss graphs for surrogate
  training)
Payoff: dataset diversity for neural surrogates.
Regime: HIGH (near uniform with mild bias).
Mapping: any binary grid.
Energy cost: cheap (bias term) or expensive (labelling is done after sampling anyway).
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. At HIGH T, uniform/independent proposals have a gap near 1 [P1 Fig 2a; PILOT 1.29x only].
Best classical: direct independent sampling, Latin hypercube, Sobol sequences [LIT-M].
Verdict: KILL, 0%. Included as an explicit honesty anchor for the HIGH-T cheap-energy case.

KNOWN-K4  Urban land-use zoning lens
Payoff: planners see an ensemble of zoning maps that respect adjacency compatibility (no heavy industry next to schools), demand
  quotas and accessibility, i.e., a "lens" on the space of acceptable plans rather than one optimum.
Regime: MEDIUM. Alternatives near the best compromise; MGA logic [LIT-V, arXiv:2206.08637].
Mapping: Potts, k = 5 to 10 land uses per parcel, one-hot embedding. Neighbour compatibility = pairwise Potts couplings (natural).
  Quotas = global (sum - target)^2 penalties, all-to-all within each class. Accessibility/travel-time objectives are non-local
  (shortest paths) and non-quadratic.
Energy cost: cheap for compatibility plus quotas (microseconds). Expensive if a traffic or land-use-transport model is in the loop
  (agent-based traffic simulations run minutes to hours [SPEC]), but then MCMC is unaffordable (Section 0.4).
Spins: a 100x100 parcel grid x 8 classes gives 8e4 binaries. Coarse block 4x4 parcels x 8 = 128.
Mixing really the bottleneck? Mildly. Quota penalties freeze single-parcel changes, but classical pair-swap moves (exchange the
  uses of two parcels) preserve quotas exactly.
Best classical: SA / GA / NSGA-II land-use allocation with swap moves (e.g., Santé-Riveira et al. 2008 [LIT-M]); MGA with LP/MIP.
Implementation: coarse-grained QeMCMC on one block with frozen surroundings as effective fields [P2]. Detailed balance with a
  random block and per-block accept [P2]. One-hot leakage states rejected or penalised (rejection keeps exactness since the
  proposal stays symmetric).
Break-even: S* about 1e5 or more. Lose.
Kill risk: classical swap moves; no expensive-energy angle without an unaffordable transport model.
Verdict: QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY. It is fine as a visual "lens" demo; any advantage claim is a KILL. <1%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KNOWN-K5  Redistricting ensembles (GerryChain-style outlier analysis used in US courts)
Payoff: expert witnesses show that an enacted plan is an outlier against a neutral ensemble of legal plans.
Regime: effectively HIGH or MEDIUM on a hard-constrained manifold. The target is roughly uniform, or tilted by compactness (a
  spanning-tree weight), over plans that are contiguous, population-balanced to about 1% or better, and compact. The difficulty
  is entirely the constraint manifold, not temperature.
Mapping: Potts with k districts per precinct (one-hot). Population balance = k global quadratic penalties. CONTIGUITY is a global
  connectivity constraint with no local quadratic form; it needs flow/aux-variable encodings (O(edges x k) aux spins) or must
  be checked only in the accept step.
Energy cost: cheap (a contiguity check is O(V+E), about microseconds to ms).
Spins: thousands of precincts x k = 1e4 to 1e5 binaries (from memory, Pennsylvania about 9,000 VTDs x 18 districts [LIT-M]).
Mixing really the bottleneck? YES for single-flip ("Flip walk") chains, which mix slowly. But it is solved classically by
  Recombination: merge two districts, draw a random spanning tree, cut it [LIT-V, DeFord, Duchin and Solomon, arXiv:1911.05725,
  HDSR 2021]; by reversible ReCom / merge-split (Cannon et al.; Carter et al.) [LIT-M]; and by SMC, which draws many plans in
  parallel and "can efficiently explore the relevant space ... better than the existing MCMC algorithms" [LIT-V, McCartan and
  Imai, arXiv:2008.06131, Ann. Appl. Stat. 2023].
Implementation: QeMCMC proposals flip many precincts and almost never preserve contiguity plus balance, so acceptance is about
  0 [SPEC, highly likely]. A coarse group (precincts on the boundary of two districts) is just a worse ReCom.
Legal kill: court use needs reproducible, auditable, exactly specified stationary distributions. The P1 hardware noise breaks
  proposal symmetry and needs twirling with a possible small bias [P1 SI], which would be an expert-witness liability [SPEC].
Break-even: S* 1e5 or more, and the classical competitor is not local Metropolis but ReCom/SMC.
Verdict: KILL, about 0%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KNOWN-K6  WFC-rule terrain / level layouts (the project's game)
Payoff: game designers and players get varied levels that satisfy tile-adjacency rules. Here the "payoff" is also
  demonstration and education value.
Regime: effectively HIGH (infinite T) restricted to the hard-constraint manifold (uniform over valid tilings), or MEDIUM if soft
  style weights are added. This is the brief's "mixing across hard constraints" HIGH-T case.
Mapping: Potts with k tile types per cell (one-hot). Adjacency rules = pairwise forbidden-pair penalties (natural, local,
  grid-connected: hardware friendly up to the one-hot overhead). k = 5 to 50 tiles.
Energy cost: cheap (local constraint check).
Spins: a 32x32 map x 10 tiles gives about 1e4 binaries. Coarse group 3x3 to 5x5 cells x k = 50 to 250 binaries.
Mixing really the bottleneck? YES for single-cell Glauber with tight tile sets (constraints freeze single changes), which is why
  WFC is a greedy constructive solver and not an MCMC. WFC is "non-backtracking, greedy" constraint solving [LIT-V, Karth and
  Smith, FDG 2017]. It does NOT sample uniformly over valid tilings.
Best classical: block resampling ("modify in blocks", Merrell model synthesis [LIT-M]); exact block Gibbs via transfer matrix for
  small blocks; ASP/SAT solvers with randomisation [LIT-V, Karth and Smith reformulated WFC with ASP]; WFC with backtracking
  and restarts.
Implementation: coarse-grained QeMCMC on a random 3x3 or 4x4 block with frozen boundary. H_prob = forbidden-pair penalties plus
  boundary fields. Accept only valid tilings (energy 0), so pi is uniform on valid tilings. Detailed balance: symmetric block
  proposal plus Metropolis accept gives exactness on the valid set; leakage into invalid one-hot states is rejected [P2
  argument]. At these block sizes the block proposal is classically simulable [P2: "quantum inspired"]. Exact classical block
  Gibbs (enumerate valid block fillings) is an IDEAL proposal and beats it.
Break-even: S* 1e5 or more. No wall-clock case.
Kill risk: for advantage, certain. As a game/demo feature it is fine: it is a visibly "quantum proposal" level generator with
  honest labelling (each move is a real quantum circuit sample).
Verdict: BUILD NOW as an honest demo/education feature (no advantage claim); QUANTUM-INSPIRED ONLY for performance. Advantage
  likelihood about 0%; demo value high.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KNOWN-K7  Antenna (base-station / AP) coverage placement
Payoff: network planners choose which candidate sites and sectors to activate, for coverage and interference against cost.
Regime: LOW for single plan optimisation; MEDIUM for robust or alternative plan ensembles.
Mapping: x_i = site active (binary). Coverage is a max-coverage / set-cover objective, not quadratic. QUBO via pairwise
  inclusion-exclusion truncation (overlap penalties) plus a budget penalty (sum x - K)^2. Exact set-cover needs aux slack
  spins per demand point.
Energy cost: the honest point is that per-site coverage/pathloss maps (ray-tracing or empirical models) are computed ONCE per
  candidate site (expensive, but N calls total). Evaluating any configuration is then a cheap max/union or SINR combination over
  precomputed maps (microseconds to ms) [OWN]. So the in-loop energy is CHEAP, unless joint effects (e.g., interference-coupled
  scheduling or full-network simulation) are modelled.
Spins: 50 to 2000 candidate sites.
Mixing really the bottleneck? No. Max-coverage is submodular, and greedy gives a (1 - 1/e) guarantee [LIT-M, Nemhauser, Wolsey
  and Fisher 1978]. ILP/CP-SAT solves practical sizes.
Best classical: greedy submodular plus local search; ILP (max-coverage); CP-SAT [LIT-M].
Implementation: none recommended. If anything, a surrogate-driven variant with a full system-level simulator in the accept step
  (t_E seconds or more) could be built, which reverts to the B1-10 analysis.
Break-even: cheap energy after map precomputation gives S* 1e3 to 1e6. Lose.
Kill risk: submodular greedy plus ILP.
Verdict: KILL as K7 is stated. The interesting antenna angle is element-level design (B1-10), not site placement. <1%.

SECTION 3. CROSS-CHECKS AND REQUIRED NEXT STEPS (for the team, not commitments)
======================================================================================================================

1. Measure S against the RIGHT classical baseline before any claim. For B1-09/10/14/15 the baseline is delayed-acceptance plus
   surrogate-transition with an inner classical PT on the same surrogate, using the same expensive-call budget. The pilot ratios
   (vs local/uniform, n = 10) say nothing about this.
2. Measure the acceptance scissor empirically. Fit an FM to a real pixel-antenna dataset (open FDTD), estimate the residual
   sigma, and plot outer acceptance against beta. Compare with a = 2 Phi(-beta sigma/sqrt 2) [OWN].
3. A cheap kill test for B1-10 (classical emulation, n = 64 to 100, coarse groups of 16 to 25): if surrogate-transition with
   1e6 inner PT steps reaches integrated autocorrelation time tau_int about 1 to 3 expensive calls per independent design, the
   quantum ceiling is S at most 3 and the idea is dead.
4. Noise: P1 needed twirling for symmetry. In nested schemes (B1-15) inner noise bias is NOT corrected by the outer exact step.
5. Novelty: IBM arXiv:2602.06171 and Ferguson and Wallden arXiv:2602.01353 already combine QeMCMC with PT/SA, so the only
   defensible novelty is the expensive-energy, surrogate-in-U, design-ensemble framing.
