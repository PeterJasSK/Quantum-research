A5 - CLASSICAL-BASELINE SCOUT FOR QeMCMC
=========================================
Date: 2026-11-20. Author: sub-agent A5.
Scope: for each problem class, the best classical sampler per temperature regime (LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH), its speed
(wall-clock per flip or per sweep on named hardware), and where it fails. Also typical per-step classical cost t_C
for O(n^2) dense Ising energies, and the evidence on whether PT+Houdayer/ICM or population annealing beat SK/EA
spin glasses at n ~ 10-1000.

EVIDENCE TAGS (used everywhere below)
  [V]  verified this session by reading the primary source (full text or abstract) at the URL given
  [S]  search-snippet / secondary write-up level only; check before quoting in a paper
  [M]  standard literature fact from memory, NOT re-checked this session (DOI given where known)
  [D]  derived by A5 from verified numbers (arithmetic shown)
  [E]  A5 estimate, given as a range; NOT a published number
  [B]  A5's own micro-benchmark on this machine (see section 5); a rough upper bound, not a published number

Regime definitions (from the brief):
  LOW    = below the glass/ordering transition; local moves get trapped
  MEDIUM = near the transition; critical slowing down
  HIGH   = well above it; single-spin Metropolis mixes fast


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. HEADLINE FINDINGS (read this first)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H1. HIGH T is where the classical baseline is strongest. Single-spin Metropolis or heat-bath mixes in O(n log n)
    attempted flips. Optimised codes run them at 0.15 ns (CPU, 1 thread) down to ~2-3 ps (GPU or FPGA) per flip on
    sparse lattices. A dense O(n^2) Ising costs O(1) per proposal plus O(n) per ACCEPTED flip (cached local fields).
    That is ~20-50 ns per attempt at n <= 300 and ~0.05-0.5 us at n = 1000 in plain scalar C on a laptop [B].
    Any QeMCMC step (circuit + readout) has to beat that per step times the gap in mixing. At high T there is
    no mixing gap left to exploit.

H2. MEDIUM T. For ferromagnets (and Potts with q <= 4 in 2D), Swendsen-Wang/Wolff essentially remove critical
    slowing down: z_SW ~ 0.22-0.25 (2D Ising) against z_Metropolis = 2.1665 [S]. For spin glasses near T_c, PT
    (plus ICM in 2D) equilibrates n <= ~1000 routinely [V]. For SK, rigorous fast mixing of Glauber dynamics is only
    proven at high T (beta < ~0.295; quoted in a 2026 survey [S]). Poly-time non-MCMC samplers now reach beta < 1/2
    (with TV guarantees), and there is a preprint claim for beta < 1 [S]. So "medium" for SK is where classical
    guarantees run out, but empirical PT still works at N <= 4096.

H3. LOW T. PT, PT+Houdayer/ICM and population annealing (PA) all equilibrate 3D EA up to L = 10 (n = 1000) down to
    T = 0.2 (~0.2 T_c) for the large majority of instances. Hardness is heavy-tailed: 286/4945 L=10 instances
    needed extra PA runs, and 0.6% of L=10 PT instances stayed unequilibrated after 2^30 sweeps [V]. Cost grows
    roughly exponentially in L for both PT and PA [V]. This hard tail at n ~ 500-1000 is the only place where a
    QeMCMC sampling advantage could plausibly show, and it has to be compared against PT+ICM / PA, not Metropolis.

H4. Fastest per-flip classical numbers found (all on SPARSE lattice Ising; dense SK is much slower per flip):
      CPU   : 6.65 flips/ns, 1 thread; 52.2 flips/ns, 16 threads (Xeon E5-2670, multi-spin coding, chimera,
              range-1 couplings) [V] Isakov et al. 2015.
              3D EA: ~60 ps/flip on a 16-core Sandy Bridge; 30 ps/flip on a Xeon-Phi for 128^3 [V] Janus II paper.
      GPU   : 417.5 flips/ns on one V100 and 453.6 flips/ns on one DGX-2H GPU (2D Ising, multi-spin);
              7,292 flips/ns on 16 GPUs [V] Romero et al. 2019.
              ~1.15e5 lattice updates/ns on a GB200 NVL72 rack [V] Bisson et al. 2025.
              3D EA spin glass: ~3 ps/flip on a GTX Titan; 2-3 ps/flip on K20x [V] Lulli et al. 2015.
              Population annealing on GPU: 10 ps/flip [S] Barash et al. 2017.
      FPGA  : Janus I: 16 ps/flip. Janus II: 2 ps/flip per FPGA processor (design figure); 0.125 ps per 16-FPGA box
              [V] Janus II paper. A 2D Ising FPGA reaches 614 flips/ns [V], as cited in Romero 2019.
      p-bit : 143.8 flips/ns (FPGA, 4,793 p-bits, sparse graph, max degree ~4-5 after sparsification)
              [V] Aadit et al. 2022.
              50-64 flips/ns (FPGA, deep Boltzmann machine training) [S] Niazi et al. 2024.
              1e6 flips/ns for nanodevices is a PROJECTION only [V].

H5. Ising machines (Digital Annealer, simulated bifurcation, CIM) are OPTIMISERS, not unbiased Boltzmann samplers.
    The SB paper itself says bSB and dSB are deterministic apart from random initial values [V]. They are the right
    baseline for "find low-energy states" claims, NOT for "sample pi_beta" claims. p-bit machines ARE Gibbs samplers
    (chromatic Gibbs) and are a valid sampling baseline.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. SAMPLER-BY-SAMPLER CHEAT SHEET (speed + failure modes)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Single-spin Metropolis / heat bath
  Speed: CPU multi-spin 6.65 flips/ns (1 thread), 52.2 flips/ns (16 threads), Xeon E5-2670, chimera range-1 [V].
         General-degree single-spin code: 0.30 flips/ns per thread [V] Isakov et al. CPC 192, 265 (2015),
         arXiv:1401.1084.
  Dense: no multi-spin coding; cost per accepted flip is O(n) (section 5).
  Fails: critical slowing down (z ~ 2.17 in 2D Ising), glassy trapping at low T.

Parallel tempering / replica exchange (+ Houdayer/ICM)
  Speed: overhead ~ N_T replicas (16-40 typical). ICM costs ~1 cluster construction per sweep.
  Gains: 2D ~500x (2^25 -> 2^16 sweeps), chimera ~128x, 3D ~8x [V] Zhu 2015.
  Fails: first-order transitions (replicas cannot cross the latent-heat gap); temperature chaos; ICM is useless
         where overlap clusters percolate (high dimension, SK, high T) [V for the mechanism, E for SK].

Wolff / Swendsen-Wang
  Speed: GPU SW 2.51 ns/flip on a GTX580 [S].
  Fails: frustration (clusters percolate trivially -> whole-system flips); random-sign couplings; strong fields;
         first-order transitions.

KBD / loop / worm algorithms
  Model-specific (fully frustrated square lattice, spin ice, triangular/kagome via dual worms).
  Fail for generic spin glasses [S] arXiv cond-mat/9402030.

Population annealing
  Speed: GPU 10 ps/flip including resampling [S] Barash et al. CPC 220, 341 (2017), arXiv:1703.03676.
  Equal to PT in efficiency on 3D EA [V].
  Massively parallel, but memory-heavy: R ~ 1e6 replicas.

Simulated annealing
  Optimiser only.
  Worse than PA/PT for ground states of 3D EA [V] Wang-Machta-Katzgraber PRE 92, 013303 (2015), arXiv:1412.2104.

Wang-Landau
  Gives the density of states; best at first-order transitions (alloys, HP, Potts q>4).
  Needs energy windows when the DOS is steep [S].
  Parallel form: REWL [S].

GPU multi-spin coding
  2D Ising: 417-454 flips/ns per V100-class GPU [V].
  3D EA: ~3 ps/flip [V].
  Fails: dense couplings; continuous couplings (bit-packing needs discrete J).

FPGA (Janus II)
  Speed: 2 ps/flip per FPGA processor; 16 processors per box [V].
  Hard-wired to short-range lattices (3D EA, Potts).

p-bits / p-computers
  Speed: 143.8 flips/ns (sparse, 4,793 p-bits) [V]; 50-64 flips/ns [S].
  All-to-all needs a multiplexed or sparsified embedding: ~1.7 flips/ns per replica at n=112 all-to-all [D].
  Speedup over GPU/TPU chessboard Gibbs: 5-18x [V].
  NOTE: Aadit's table lists a V100 at only 11.37 flips/ns, against 417 flips/ns in Romero 2019. Different
  implementations and graphs (irregular vs square). Always compare like-for-like.

Ising machines (DA, SB, CIM)
  Speed: DA ~100x TTS vs single-core SA/PT on dense <= 1024 [V].
         SB: K2000 in 0.26 ms (bSBM time-to-target) / ~2 ms (dSBM to best known) [V].
         CIM 100k spins: 600 us to a greedy reference [S].
  Not Boltzmann samplers. DA gives no speedup on sparse 2D [V].

Neural / flow samplers (VAN, normalising flows)
  VAN: Wu-Wang-Zhang PRL 122, 080602 (2019), arXiv:1809.10606 [S].
  Fail on hard instances: "ML-assisted MC fails at sampling computationally hard problems" [S] Ciarella et al.
  MLST 4, 010501 (2023), arXiv:2210.11145.
  Diffusion/flow samplers hit first-order transitions along the denoising path [S] Ghio et al. PNAS (2024),
  doi:10.1073/pnas.2311810121.
  Training costs dominate; useful as proposals inside MH at high/medium T.

Locally balanced / gradient-informed proposals
  Zanella 2020; GWG 2021; DLP 2022 [S].
  Cost per step = O(neighbourhood) energy differences. Wins at medium T on rugged but connected landscapes.

Tensor-network samplers
  2D: Frias-Perez et al. SciPost 14, 123 (2023) [S].
  3D EA up to 64^3: Chen et al. PRL 2026 [V].
  Large step-count reductions, but per-sweep cost 9-350x Metropolis [V].
  Acceptance of the older TNMC collapses at L=12 at T_c [V].


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DOES PT + HOUDAYER/ICM OR POPULATION ANNEALING "DEFEAT" SK/EA AT n ~ 10-1000?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Short answer: for EQUILIBRIUM SAMPLING at n <= ~1000, yes in practice, down to T ~ 0.2 (3D EA) and T ~ 0.4 (SK).
It costs 1e6-1e9 sweeps per instance with a heavy-tailed hardness distribution, and the cost grows roughly
exponentially with linear size.

Evidence:
 a) Yucesoy, Machta, Katzgraber, PRE 87, 012104 (2013), arXiv:1210.6290 [V]
    Setup: 3D EA (Gaussian), L = 6, 8, 10 (n up to 1000). PT with 16 temps, T = 0.2-2.0. ~5,000 instances per L.
    Cost: ~140 CPU-years.
    log10 tau_int (|q|) mean = 1.60 / 2.81 / 3.89, sd = 0.63-0.69 decades.
      [D] So the 99th-percentile instance is ~30-40x slower than the median (lognormal assumption).
    log10 round-trip time mean = 3.62 / 4.46 / 5.24 sweeps, sd 0.19-0.29.
      Round-trip times are essentially UNCORRELATED with tau_int (r = 0.01-0.13). Round trips alone are therefore
      not a sufficient equilibration criterion.
    0.6% (32) of L=10 instances stayed above the noise floor even after 2^30 sweeps.
 b) Wang, Machta, Katzgraber, PRE 92, 063307 (2015), arXiv:1508.05647 [V]
    PA vs PT on 3D EA, L = 4-10 (PA also L = 12, 14).
    PA "at least comparable in efficiency to PT".
    Instance hardness is strongly correlated between the two methods.
    log rho_s (PA) and log tau_int (PT) both grow ~linearly in L, i.e. exponential slowdown for both.
    Instances needing extra PA runs: 0/4941 (L=4), 5/5099 (L=8), 286/4945 (L=10), 533/5000 (L=12, at T=0.333).
    Equilibration criterion: R >= 100 rho_s (family-entropy based); rho_f ~ 1.6-2x rho_s.
 c) Wang, Machta, Katzgraber, PRE 92, 013303 (2015), arXiv:1412.2104 [V/S]
    Ground states of 3D EA: PA >> SA, PA ~ PT. Thermalisation test: e^{S_f} >= 100.
 d) Zhu, Ochoa, Katzgraber, PRL 115, 077201 (2015), arXiv:1501.05630 [V]
    ICM (Houdayer generalised). Thermalisation measured in sweeps:
      2D N=1024, T=0.212:              2^25 -> 2^16
      chimera N=1152:                  2^25 -> 2^18
      3D N=1728, T~0.42 (~0.43 T_c):   2^23 -> 2^20
    The speedup grows with system size. It works only where overlap clusters do not percolate (low T, low
    dimension).
 e) Barzegar, Pattison, Wang, Katzgraber, PRE 98, 053308 (2018), arXiv:1710.09025 [V]
    PA+ICM: clear speedup in 2D, marginal in 3D.
    3D wall time 1.3 / 12 / 75 min (N = 216 / 512 / 1000) on one 20-core node.
    Notes a growing density of "chaotic" hard instances with size.
 f) SK [S]
    Billoire & Marinari (cond-mat/0101177): PT with 38 temps over T = 0.4-1.325, 4e5 iterations.
    Billoire (arXiv:1003.6086, 1401.4341): N = 256-4096; 4e5-8e5 thermalisation sweeps. Cites Aspelmeier, Billoire,
    Marinari, Moore (2008) as the empirical basis that this equilibrates T >= 0.4 up to N = 4096.
    No Houdayer gain is expected in SK [E].
 g) Houdayer, EPJB 22, 479 (2001) [S]: 2D +-J 100x100 equilibrated down to T = 0.1.
    The moves are not ergodic alone and must be combined with SSF or PT.
 h) The Digital Annealer study (Aramon 2019) used PT+ICM as its strongest classical baseline [V/S].
Implication for QeMCMC: the honest comparison at n ~ 100-1000 and low T is the TAIL (hardest 1-10% of instances)
against PT+ICM / PA, measured in wall-clock, with tau_int (not round trips) as the metric.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. NOTES ON WHERE CLASSICAL "SUPER-SAMPLERS" DO NOT APPLY (useful for picking a QeMCMC niche)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Cluster moves (SW/Wolff) need non-frustrated (or gauge-equivalent) couplings. They fail for spin glasses, generic
  QUBO, and a strong random field.
- Houdayer/ICM needs non-percolating overlap clusters: low dimension and low T. It is useless for SK and dense QUBO.
- Loop/worm algorithms need a local constraint structure (ice rule, dimer mapping).
- Multi-spin coding (GPU, FPGA, CPU) needs short-range, discrete couplings. Dense or continuous J loses 1-3 orders
  of magnitude per flip.
- Ising machines: hardware-size limits (DA chip <= 8,192 bits; FPGA SB 2,048; p-bit FPGA ~5,000 [V/S]). They are
  biased w.r.t. Boltzmann. DA gave no gain on sparse 2D [V].
- Neural samplers: training cost; documented failures on hard (glassy/coloring) instances [S].
- Wang-Landau: needs a discrete, bounded energy range. Convergence is slow with a steep DOS [S].
- Constraints (one-hot, cardinality, redistricting contiguity) break single-flip Ising moves. They need swap moves,
  spanning-tree moves or penalties.

5. PER-STEP CLASSICAL COST t_C FOR DENSE O(n^2) ISING (n = 10 ... 1000)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Published numbers
  - No published benchmark was found that gives ns-per-flip for DENSE SK Metropolis at n = 10-1000. The published
    per-flip records are all sparse-lattice codes (section 0, H4).
  - Isakov 2015 general-degree single-spin code: 0.30 flips/ns per thread (~3.3 ns/flip) on degree-6 chimera [V].
    By O(degree) scaling, a dense degree-(n-1) graph would cost [E] ~n/6 x more if dE were recomputed each time.
    With cached local fields it costs O(1) per proposal + O(n) per accepted flip.
