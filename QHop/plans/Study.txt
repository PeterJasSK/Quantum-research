QHOP STUDY
==========


WHAT THIS STUDY IS
------------------
Generating many good solutions (maps, molecule shapes, magnet states) with Markov chain Monte Carlo is slow
because the method gets stuck in one valley of good solutions. A quantum computer can propose a jump to a distant
solution of similar quality, which a classical check still accepts or rejects, so it needs fewer steps while the
results stay correct (Layden et al., Nature 2023). QHop measures how much noise this quantum jump survives on real
IBM hardware, which kinds of noise make the results wrong instead of only slower, and shows it as a game anyone
can play.


THE 3 METRICS
-------------
1. Step advantage
   How many fewer steps the quantum jump needs than classical moves to mix (spectral gap, and its scaling with
   problem size n). Compared against one-tile flips, random jumps and stronger classical methods.

2. Noise threshold eps*
   The error rate at which the step advantage disappears, measured separately for each noise type:
   depolarizing, dephasing, energy decay (T1), readout error.

3. Bias
   How far the results drift from the correct distribution because noise makes the jump lopsided (T1 pulls
   qubits toward 0). Measured with the plain classical check and with the corrected one (Metropolis-Hastings
   using the measured proposal).


POSSIBLE RESULTS
----------------
A. Best case: the step advantage holds on Heron at small n; eps* lies just above today's error, so the next
   hardware generation is in range; T1 creates a measurable bias that the correction removes.
   Message: "it works, here is the error rate it needs, and here is how to keep it correct."

B. Likely: a clear advantage in simulation, partial on hardware (smallest boards only); eps* below today's error;
   T1 bias visible in simulation and weak on hardware.
   Message: "not yet on today's hardware; this is how far away it is."

C. Null: no advantage survives on hardware; all noise types act alike; no bias.
   Message: "noise kills it before anything else; required error rate is X." Still a measured answer to the open
   question of Ferguson & Wallden.

Every outcome also says whether the gain comes from interference (coherent circuit vs the same circuit with
interference destroyed).


HOW TO VISUALIZE IT
-------------------
1. The game (demo-quantum-leap-race.html): two generators race on a tile map the player builds; step counters,
   median steps per generator, decohered and random-jump controls. To add: noise type selector, strength slider,
   and a "distance from the correct result" meter, so players see the advantage fade and the bias appear.

2. Paper figure 1: step advantage vs board size n (log scale), quantum vs classical lines, ideal simulation and
   Heron points on the same axes.

3. Paper figure 2: step advantage vs error rate, one curve per noise type, eps* marked where each curve crosses
   the classical line.

4. Paper figure 3: bias vs error rate for T1 and depolarizing, plain check vs corrected check.


IS IT A MASTER'S THESIS?
------------------------
Yes. It has a research question from a named open problem, three measured quantities, simulation plus real
hardware experiments, and a substantial engineering part:
- exact simulator with noise channels (density matrix up to about 10 qubits, trajectories to 12)
- hardware pipeline for Heron: proposal circuits, proposal-matrix estimation, coherent vs dephased runs, repeats
- statistics: gap fits with confidence intervals, bias measurement, Hastings correction
- the interactive game with replay of real hardware data
Supervisor role (Korecko): formal proof of when the chain stays correct, and the correctness check.
