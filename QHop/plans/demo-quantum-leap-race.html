What this shows, and what it does not

Why the leap helps. The leap evolves a quantum state of the whole map under the rules plus a mixing term, then measures it. Interference sends the map to distant maps with similar quality, across the bad maps in between. One-tile flips have to climb through those bad maps and get rejected.

Decohered leap is the same leap with interference destroyed after every time slice. If it does as badly as the classical flips, interference was the reason.

Steps, not seconds. Every quantum leap here is an exact simulation in your browser. On real hardware one leap is one circuit run, roughly 10,000 times slower than a one-tile flip on a laptop. The race counts proposals, the measure used by Layden et al., Nature 2023.

Small boards. A laptop can also list all 512 to 4,096 maps and pick the best one at once, which beats both generators at this size. The leap matters only for boards far too large to list.

Try to beat it. Find a board where one-tile flips win. Hint: rules that never conflict, such as “everything wants to differ”.
