QHOP READING ORDER
==================

Read top to bottom. The first four are the science, the next four are prior art for the game, the last one is
the classical opponent. "[download]" = not in papers/ yet, link given.


THE SCIENCE

1. Layden et al. 2023 - Quantum-enhanced Markov chain Monte Carlo (Nature 619, 282)
   papers/Layden_Quantum-enhanced-MCMC_2023.pdf   https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12497
   The base of everything. A quantum circuit proposes the next state, a classical Metropolis step accepts or
   rejects it, so the samples stay correct and the quantum part only speeds up mixing. Measured on IBM hardware;
   spectral-gap exponents k quantum 0.264 vs local 0.94 vs uniform 0.948 (you reproduced them).
   Focus: the proposal circuit (H = (1-g)H_problem + g H_mix), why the proposal is symmetric, the gap figure,
   the hardware section and what they say about noise.

2. Ferguson & Wallden 2025 - QeMCMC for systems larger than your quantum computer
   papers/Ferguson-Wallden_QeMCMC-larger-than-QC_2025.pdf   https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04247
   Coarse-grains the method: the quantum computer proposes a move for only a block of the system (about sqrt(n)
   qubits) and the speedup persists in simulation. This is the "lens" in the game. Their outlook names the open
   question QHop answers: which level and types of noise keep the advantage.
   Focus: how blocks are chosen, the results figure, the outlook/open questions section.

3. Orfi & Sels 2024 - why the advantage exists and when [download]
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07881
   Theory: the gap is tied to how spread out the eigenstates are; the advantage needs an intermediate regime of
   evolution time and mixing (too short = local move, too long = random jump). This is the t and g sliders in
   the demo.
   Focus: the regime diagram; what parameters to use in your simulations.

4. Marshall et al. 2026 - QeMCMC for optimisation at 117 variables on IBM [download]
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06171
   Closest recent hardware work. Uses QeMCMC to find good solutions (optimisation), not to sample, and does not
   split noise by type. You must cite it and say clearly how QHop differs.
   Focus: their hardware setup, noise handling, what they measure.

5. Aaronson 2015 - Read the fine print (Nature Physics)
   papers/Aaronson_Read-the-fine-print_2015.pdf
   Short essay on how quantum speedup claims hide costs (input loading, output reading, comparison against weak
   classical methods). Your honesty checklist: steps vs seconds, local vs best classical, small n.
   Focus: all of it, 3 pages.


PRIOR ART FOR THE GAME (quantum map / content generation)

6. Heese 2024 - Quantum Wave Function Collapse for procedural content generation (IEEE CG&A)
   papers/Heese_Quantum-Wave-Function-Collapse_2024.pdf   https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13853
   The closest prior art to the game: a quantum version of Wave Function Collapse, a circuit prepared as a
   random generator for tile content, run on simulators and IBM devices. It is a generator, not MCMC: no
   acceptance step, no mixing measure, no step comparison. That difference is your novelty line for the game.
   Focus: how tile rules become a circuit, their IBM results, what they claim about quantum value.

7. Wootton 2020 - Procedural generation using quantum computation
   papers/Wootton_Procedural-generation-quantum-computation_2020.pdf   https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11510
   IBM's first "quantum for games" paper: Quantum Blur, interference used as a visual effect, no advantage
   claimed. Shows how the games community received quantum methods.
   Focus: introduction and the framing ("useful now" without advantage).

8. Wootton 2020 - A quantum procedure for map generation
   papers/Wootton_Quantum-procedure-map-generation_2020.pdf   https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10327
   Geopolitical maps and history generated by a small quantum decision process. Map generation on near-term
   devices, again without a speed claim.
   Focus: skim; the map encoding.

9. Wootton & Pfaffhauser 2021 - Investigating the usefulness of Quantum Blur
   papers/Wootton_Usefulness-of-Quantum-Blur_2021.pdf   https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01646
   Compares Quantum Blur with ordinary blur and asks which effects really need superposition and entanglement.
   A model for your decohered control: test which part of the effect is truly quantum.
   Focus: skim; the "which features depend on quantum phenomena" part.


THE CLASSICAL OPPONENT

10. Christmann et al. 2024 - quantum-inspired classical proposals [download, verify the ID]
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17821
    Classical (tensor-network) proposals that imitate the quantum one. If they match it, the quantum claim
    weakens. Read before fixing your baselines.
    Focus: which instances they match the quantum proposal on.
