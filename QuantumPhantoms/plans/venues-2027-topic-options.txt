QUANTUM PHANTOMS: FIVE TOPIC OPTIONS PER VENUE
================================================
Written 2026-11-14. Companion to venues-2027-three-papers.txt.

The venues and the split are locked:

  CBMS (Rome, 20 January 2027)        the RECORD      6-page paper
  ISBI (Lausanne, 1 February 2027)    the IMAGE       1-page abstract
  QCE  (Raleigh, April / June 2027)   the HARDWARE    8-10 page paper, or 2-page poster paper

Each venue has five options below. Inside one venue the options share most of the work, so a late
switch costs little. Option 1 is always the current default. Every option states what it needs,
who it depends on, and when to choose it.

Rule that never changes: the three submissions stay different. An option may not be chosen if it
repeats what another venue's chosen option already says.


1. CBMS: FIVE WAYS TO WRITE THE RECORD PAPER
--------------------------------------------

C1. The formally checked record (default)
  Title     A replayable and formally checked record for synthetic lung nodules seeded by a
            quantum processor
  Question  Can the rules of the record be proved, not only tested?
  Core      Petri net model of the workflow; five properties checked; one live case.
  Needs     Model and property results from Stefan Korecko; one live run with repeats.
  Depends   Korecko (high), hardware (medium), Baca (low).
  Choose    If the model is on track by mid-December.
  Risk      Formal part is late or too thin for 1.5 pages.

C2. From manufacturer requirements to a record design
  Title     What must a synthetic test case prove about its origin? Requirements and a
            reference record for synthetic CT
  Question  Which requirements does a medical device maker place on synthetic test data, and
            does one concrete record meet each of them?
  Core      Requirements list; table "requirement -> field in the record -> check that
            demonstrates it"; one live case as demonstration.
  Needs     A real requirements list from Michal Baca, with references to the regulations
            and standards he names.
  Depends   Baca (high), Korecko (low), hardware (low).
  Choose    If Baca delivers strong material and the formal model is late.
  Risk      Reads as an opinion paper unless every requirement has a source.

C3. An integrity and replay experiment at cohort size
  Title     Every edit is visible: an integrity and replay study on 100 synthetic CT cases
  Question  How reliably does the record expose changes, and how exactly does replay
            reproduce a case?
  Core      100 cases or more. Edit classes: one voxel, one growth parameter, the seed, the
            host scan, the code version. Detection rate per class. Replay hash match rate.
            Record size and time per case.
  Needs     Only the pipeline and compute. Runs on the dry backend; one live batch is a bonus.
  Depends   Peter only.
  Choose    If neither the formal model nor the requirements arrive in time.
  Risk      Result is "100 % detected", which is expected of a hash. The interest must come
            from the edit classes and from replay across machines and library versions.

C4. What does a certified seed add? Four seed sources compared
  Title     Pseudo-random, system, public beacon or quantum processor: what each seed source
            adds to the record of a synthetic case
  Question  For each seed source, what can a third party verify afterwards, at what cost?
  Core      Same pipeline, four seed sources. Table: what is checkable, what must be trusted,
            cost and delay per case, failure modes (a failed Bell test rejects the draw).
            Image difference between sources: none expected, and reported as such.
  Needs     Live runs with repeats and the reject gate working; a beacon source wired in.
  Depends   Hardware (high), Baca (medium, for "what must be trusted").
  Choose    If the hardware runs go well in December.
  Risk      Closest to the QCE paper. Keep physics out: no witness, no claim levels here.

C5. Reproducing published methods: a reproducibility report
  Title     Rebuilding 21 published methods for synthetic lung nodules: what reproduced and
            what did not
  Question  How much of the published tool chain for synthetic nodules can be rebuilt from
            the papers alone?
  Core      Table of the 21 methods: paper number, our number, matched or not. The three
            defects that only appeared on a real host scan. One assembled case.
  Needs     Nothing new. The material is in plans/relatedPapers/reverse-engineering/.
  Depends   Peter only. No hardware.
  Choose    If the refactored pipeline still does not run in December. This is the safety net.
  Risk      Six pages are short for 21 methods; select 8 to 10. Not a record paper, so the
            ISBI abstract must then avoid the same defects story (do not pair with I4).


2. ISBI: FIVE WAYS TO WRITE THE IMAGE ABSTRACT
----------------------------------------------

I1. Host-matched insertion (default)
  Title     Training-free lung nodule insertion that keeps the host scanner's blur and noise
  Question  Does the inserted nodule match the host scan in resolution and noise?
  Numbers   Volume error; noise inside and around the nodule against the host background.
  Needs     Rendered cases on the current pipeline, one to three hosts.
  Choose    If nothing better is ready. Always available.

I2. Detector and observer response
  Title     Do detectors see synthetic nodules as real? A training-free generator tested
            with an open detector and a model observer
  Question  Is detection performance on synthetic nodules the same as on real ones of the
            same size?
  Numbers   Sensitivity by size class, synthetic against real; model-observer detectability.
  Needs     An open nodule detector running on LIDC; 50 or more synthetic and real nodules.
  Choose    If the detector comparison is finished by mid-January. Strongest option for
            this audience, and the best one for finding an imaging co-author.
  Risk      A visible gap between synthetic and real. Report it; it is still a result.

I3. The same nodule over time
  Title     One nodule, many time points: replayable growth series inserted into a single
            host scan
  Question  Can a growth model give a controlled series with known volume doubling time
            for testing growth measurement software?
  Numbers   Set doubling time against measured doubling time; volume error per time point.
  Needs     The growth stage run to 4 to 6 time points in one host.
  Choose    If the growth player is in good shape. ISBI 2027 has a workshop on longitudinal
            medical data analysis; check whether it takes papers (deadline 26 January). (verify)
  Risk      No real follow-up scans to compare with.

I4. Three defects that only a real host scan reveals
  Title     Pitfalls when published nodule synthesis methods meet a real scan
  Question  Which errors stay hidden on phantoms and appear in real CT?
  Numbers   Enclosed holes in the grown shape; volume drift at the edge during rendering;
            noise estimate inflated by vessels. Each with size of the effect and the fix.
  Needs     Nothing new; the findings exist from the 2026-10-31 work. Re-measure on the
            current pipeline.
  Choose    If time is short in January. Cheapest option.
  Risk      Small scope. Do not pair with C5.

I5. One generator, three scanner vendors
  Title     Does training-free insertion adapt to the scanner? Nodules in GE, Toshiba and
            Siemens hosts
  Question  Does one parameter set give host-matched nodules on scans from different
            vendors and reconstruction kernels?
  Numbers   Blur and noise match per vendor; 9 to 15 hosts.
  Needs     The local LIDC hosts (GE and Toshiba are already on disk) and Siemens hosts.
  Choose    If the multi-host run works. Fits the call topic "image quality assessment and
            harmonization", and Siemens will ask this question anyway.
  Risk      One vendor matches badly. Report it.


3. QCE: FIVE WAYS TO WRITE THE HARDWARE PAPER
---------------------------------------------
Routes at QCE: technical paper (April, selective) and poster with a 2-page proceedings paper
(two rounds in June, much easier). Each option says which route it suits. Dates are the 2026
pattern. (verify)

Q1. The full ladder inside a working pipeline (default)
  Title     A quantum-seeded virtual tumour pipeline: what one Heron processor certifies today
  Question  Which quantum claims hold with repeats inside a real application, and which fail?
  Core      All claim levels, all circuits, repeats, classical bounds, design rules, cost.
  Route     Technical paper.
  Needs     Repeats of every number on one backend, better two.
  Choose    If the repeat campaign is complete by March.
  Risk      Broad. A reviewer may say it is several half-results.

Q2. Certified randomness in a real application
  Title     Certified randomness as a service step: repeated Bell tests on a 156-qubit
            processor inside an imaging pipeline
  Question  How stable is the Bell violation over days, backends and qubit pairs, and how
            many certified bits per job does the application get?
  Core      S value over many jobs; reject rate; entropy bound and extracted bits per job;
            cost per certified case; the loopholes stated plainly (this is not a
            loophole-free test).
  Route     Technical paper, or poster. The safest poster.
  Needs     Many cheap repeats of one small circuit. The entropy bound implemented in the
            pipeline (today only the S value is stored).
  Choose    If only the Bell test is reliable. Most likely to succeed.
  Risk      Narrow. Reviewers know that device-independent claims need closed loopholes;
            the wording must be exact.

Q3. What failed and why
  Title     Negative results from a 100-qubit application run: GHZ trees, process memory and
            teleport routing on Heron processors
  Question  Why do these three constructions fail on current hardware, and what rules follow?
  Core      Each failure with numbers and cause: dead qubit inside the tree, readout error
            larger than two-qubit error, memory gap below the sampling floor. The resulting
            design rules.
  Route     Technical paper, or poster.
  Needs     Repeats of the failures so that they are results and not accidents.
  Choose    If the positive claims stay marginal after repeats.
  Risk      Must show the failures are due to the hardware and not to the circuits.

Q4. A per-tile entanglement map
  Title     Where on the chip does an entangled seed survive? A per-tile witness map on a
            156-qubit processor
  Question  On how many tiles of the chip does the entangled seed state pass its witness,
            and does calibration data predict it?
  Core      Witness value per tile with error bars and multiple-test correction; link to
            calibration data; pruning rule and its effect.
  Route     Technical paper if the map is clean; poster otherwise.
  Needs     The witness to pass clearly on some tiles. Today it is marginal (one run).
  Choose    If repeats lift the witness above marginal.
  Risk      Highest physics risk of the five.

Q5. Engineering a hybrid job
  Title     Running a hybrid quantum-classical job on a budget: spend guard, calibration
            pruning and dry-run parity
  Question  How closely does a local dry run predict the hardware run, and what does each
            engineering rule save?
  Core      Dry run against hardware, number by number; QPU seconds per case before and
            after each rule; job batching; failure handling.
  Route     Technical paper in a software or systems track, or poster.
  Needs     Paired dry and live runs; logged cost per run.
  Choose    If the engineering story is stronger than the physics results.
  Risk      Overlap with the CBMS paper. Keep to run-time engineering; no record, no proofs.

venue. Decide before April whether both go to QCE or one goes elsewhere.
