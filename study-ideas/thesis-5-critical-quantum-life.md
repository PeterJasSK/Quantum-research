# Thesis Idea 5 — Life You Can Poke: a Darwinian quantum population that self-organizes to the edge of chaos under closed-loop feedback, re-stabilizes after you perturb it between hardware runs, and certifies it is *quantum*-alive with an entanglement witness

**Tag: QUANTUM · thesis (QDEP / artificial-life family) · effort: high · QC: yes (sim-first draft → hardware thesis)**

## Pitch
Take the recreated 2018 quantum-artificial-life model (already pushed to a W=24 / 48-qubit entanglement genealogy, ~6× the origin paper) and turn it from a *depth* study into a *living, interactive* one. A Darwinian population runs many generations on IBM hardware under **closed-loop feedback modeled on DishBrain** (Kagan et al. 2022): outcomes the population "predicts" are met with predictable feedback, surprising outcomes with high-entropy feedback, and — per Friston's Free Energy Principle — the population reorganizes across generations to **minimize its own surprise**. The honest, biologically-faithful target is **not "least chaotic" but criticality** (the edge of chaos, branching parameter σ≈1, neuronal-avalanche exponent α≈1.5 — Beggs & Plenz 2003): maximal order is *dead*, maximal chaos is *seizure*, "lively" means *poised at the critical point*. Between hardware batch runs **you poke it** — change the contingency/environment — and watch a transient surprise spike relax back to criticality over the next generations. It is "undeniably alive" only if three gates pass: (1) a **yoked open-loop control fails to adapt**, (2) the population **settles at criticality, not silence**, and (3) an **entanglement witness** certifies the aliveness is quantum, not classical dynamics in disguise.
**Paper strength score: 77/100** — genuinely novel (nobody has run a closed-loop, active-inference, interactively-perturbed quantum-artificial-life population), maximal wow/visual, and near-sole ownership on the author's own model/code; docked below thesis-1 because the criticality/adaptation result is a real *discovery bet* (not pre-proven like thesis-1's P0), it is engineering-heavy (the between-runs interaction loop), and it lives or dies on the honesty gates that separate science from metaphor.

## How it becomes a study
**Research question:** Under closed-loop DishBrain-style feedback, does a Darwinian quantum population self-organize across generations toward criticality (edge of chaos) by minimizing a free-energy/surprise proxy; does it re-minimize and re-reach criticality after an external poke; and is the resulting order certified as genuinely quantum (entanglement witness), with a yoked open-loop control failing all three?
**Hypothesis:** With contingent closed-loop feedback the population's surprise proxy falls over generations and its dynamics converge to criticality (σ→1, avalanche P(S)∝S^{−1.5}, entropy plateau — not H→0); a poke causes a transient surprise/entropy spike that relaxes back to the critical set-point with a measurable time constant; the off-diagonal entanglement witness stays above the classical-surrogate null throughout. Under a **yoked control** (same pokes, feedback scrambled / non-contingent) none of this happens. (Falsifiable at every gate: no adaptation gap vs control → not learning; H→0 → dead not lively; witness dead → classical, not quantum life.)
**Method — two stages, exactly as requested (small-scale draft, then thesis-scale):**

**Stage DRAFT (validate the idea cheap, simulator-first + tiny hardware).** Minimal population (small genome width W, few generations) on Aer + one small Heron run. Implement the closed-loop: encode each generation's outcome → apply predictable feedback on "expected" outcomes, high-entropy (QRNG-seeded) feedback on surprising ones → select/reproduce → mutate. Verify the three signatures exist *at toy scale*: (a) surprise proxy drops under closed loop but not under yoked control; (b) a criticality fingerprint appears (σ trending to 1, entropy plateau); (c) a single scripted poke produces spike-then-relax. Deliverable: a go/no-go kill-gate before spending hardware budget — the same discipline as the virus P0 fliptest.

**Stage THESIS (bigger and better than all prior tries, hardware, interactive).** Scale the genome/population width and generation count; run generations in **hardware batches**, persisting the inherited quantum+classical state between jobs. Between batches, the experimenter **pokes** (changes which outcomes are "predictable," alters selection pressure, or injects a stimulus) and the next batch continues from the inherited population — a true interactive session. Certify quantumness with the `⟨X^⊗W⟩` genealogical entanglement witness against a matched classical measure-and-resend surrogate. Report the criticality metrics, the post-poke adaptation time constant, and the closed-loop-vs-yoked adaptation gap.
**Baseline:** The **yoked open-loop control** (identical stimulation, feedback not contingent on the population's own state) — the DishBrain control that makes it science; plus the classical measure-and-resend surrogate for the quantum-certification arm.
**Metrics:** free-energy/surprise proxy vs generation; branching parameter σ (target →1); avalanche size-distribution exponent α (target ≈1.5); Shannon entropy of the population distribution (target: plateau, not collapse); order-parameter susceptibility peak; post-poke relaxation time constant; entanglement-witness σ-margin; closed-loop-minus-yoked adaptation gap.
**Novel contribution:** The first quantum-artificial-life population that is *interactively steerable* and *self-organizes to a certified-quantum critical state under closed-loop feedback* — a poke-and-watch-it-recover living system, with the yoked control and entanglement witness that keep "alive" from being a metaphor.

## THE VISUALIZATION
A live dashboard of one evolving population: a swarm of glowing genome-individuals, generation counter ticking. Three gauges dominate — a **criticality dial** (needle parked at the edge between a frozen-blue "dead" zone and a boiling-red "chaos" zone, hovering on σ=1), an **avalanche histogram** snapping onto the −3/2 power-law line as it self-organizes, and a **surprise meter** falling over generations. Then the user hits **POKE**: the surprise meter spikes, the criticality needle lurches, the swarm scrambles — and over the next generations you *watch it crawl back to the edge of chaos*. A ghosted "yoked control" panel beside it pokes identically and never recovers. One screen: a quantum thing you prod and see fight to stay alive.

## Bull case / Bear case / Likely outcome / Value if null
**Bull:** It is exactly the "life you can poke" dream, it is the most visually spectacular and most uniquely-owned thing in the repo, and it is scientifically anchored in three real, citable neuroscience results — so it reads as rigorous, not sci-fi, if the gates are enforced. The DRAFT stage de-risks it before hardware spend.
**Bear:** It is a discovery bet, not a pre-proven mechanism — the population may not show a clean criticality signature, the closed-loop-vs-yoked gap may be small on noisy hardware, and the between-runs interactive loop is real engineering; the "alive" framing invites metaphor-only criticism unless the yoked control + entanglement witness are foregrounded relentlessly.
**Likely outcome:** A toy-scale DRAFT that clearly shows the surprise-drop + a criticality trend + a poke-response, scaling to a hardware population where the closed-loop arm beats the yoked control and the witness certifies quantumness at modest width — a striking, honest, moderately-strong result with the best figure in the program.
**Value if null:** Even if the population fails to reach clean criticality or the poke-response washes out in noise, you deliver the first rigorous *negative* on closed-loop self-organization in a quantum-artificial-life population — with the exact width/noise budget at which it dies — plus a reusable interactive-quantum-life framework and the honest observable set (surprise proxy, σ, α, witness) that the field currently lacks.
