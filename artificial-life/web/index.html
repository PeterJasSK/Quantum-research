The model · Alvarez-Rodriguez, Sanz, Lamata and Solano · IBM machine

Artificial life on quantum computers

One group has run a full life-cycle on a real quantum computer: Alvarez-Rodriguez, Sanz, Lamata and Solano, on an IBM machine. Their model is below in plain terms, with the two places this study changes it.

Darwinian life needs three things: heredity, variation, and selection. The model builds all three from just two qubits per individual. One qubit is the gene it inherits, the other is the body it grows and shows to the world.

Heredity. A parent passes its gene to a child through a single CNOT. The no-cloning theorem forbids duplicating an unknown quantum state exactly, so the model never tries: it transmits the gene's expectation value instead, which is all inheritance needs. A cloning machine (U, ρe, X) is any unitary that leaves every observable's mean unchanged across the transfer, X̄ = X̄1 = X̄2, with no dependence on the input ρ so it works for any founder. The simplest one carries ⟨σz⟩ from an arbitrary ρ = |ψ⟩⟨ψ| into a blank ρe = |0⟩⟨0| using U = CNOT, and that single gate is the whole of heredity.

Variation. Two mutation channels feed diversity. A spontaneous single-qubit rotation by a random angle θ perturbs the gene and leaves the body alone; and replication is itself imperfect, adding its own error to gene and lifetime. Random θ per event is what keeps the population varied.

M = ⎡ cos θ   sin θ ⎤        U_M(θ) = I₄ + ½ |1⟩⟨1| ⊗ ⎡ −1  1 ⎤ (cos θ + i sin θ + 1)

Selection. Individuals move on a grid and interact when they share a cell: the gene is the control, the body the target, so when two genes differ the bodies are swapped, a shifting predator-prey exchange that favours short-lived, varied lineages. Meanwhile every body ages. Aging is a Lindbladian relaxation of the body while the gene is untouched, and death is called when the body reaches |0⟩⟨0|, i.e. ⟨σz⟩p = 1 − ε. The spent body then becomes the next blank gene, so the loop is self-sustaining. A single parameter a sets lifetime: the nearer the gene starts to |1⟩⟨1|, the longer it lives.

ρ̇ = γ( σρσ† − ½σ†σρ − ½ρσ†σ ),  σ = I⊗|0⟩⟨1|
⟨σz⟩_g = 2a − 1        ⟨σz⟩_p = 1 − 2 e^{γt} (1 − a)

That closed loop of heredity, variation and selection, running as one quantum circuit, is Darwinian evolution on real hardware. This study changes it in exactly two places.

The first change is death. Reproduced faithfully, the death operator couples the mortal body to its gene with a cx(g,p) and then dissipates it, so on hardware the body's death drags the gene down with it and the inherited quantum correlation was gone after only nine generations. So here the body dies in isolation: a separable diagonal trait relaxing by the chip's own T1 toward |0⟩, with no cx(g,p) and no engineered bath. Real physics does the dying, and it never reaches the gene.

The second change follows from it. The two qubits are split into a gene line that stays coherent and carries the one thing no classical machine can fake, the entanglement witness ⟨X⊗W⟩ read in the X basis against a separable null of ≈ 0, and a body that is classical and mortal, with a barrier so the two never share a gate. Severing that coupling carried the certified witness out to W = 50 = 100 qubits, where the limit became the number of clean qubits on the chip rather than the biology. Restoring the coupling on hardware collapses the same witness from +0.90 to +0.12: connecting the body, which feels more quantum, is exactly what destroys it.

The win · the faithful model, then the germ/soma fix · the scale ceiling

An old vs new design

The faithful reproduction drove the 2018 death channel to its limit: with real amplitude-damping death in the loop, the genealogical witness collapsed at W* = 9 = 19 qubits. The diagnosis was mechanical: the death operator couples the mortal soma to the immortal germ line (cx(g,p)) then dissipates it, so body-death irreversibly decoheres the gene-witness. A biology wall, not a hardware one.

The germ/soma fix builds the Weismann barrier: the germ line carries the GHZ witness and stays coherent; the soma is a separable diagonal trait dying in isolation (natural T1, no bath, no cx(g,p)). Severing that one coupling moved the certified ceiling to W = 50 = 100 qubits (k=3σ), and the run stopped only because the chip ran out of clean qubits (needs a 108-chain; longest clean is 107). The machine ran out before the witness did, the limiting resource flipped from entanglement to machine size.

Honest: the witness is certified, not strong, at the top (+0.058 at W=50, ~5σ over null, far below a fresh GHZ's ~0.9). Scale · faithfulness · certification, not a speedup. N=1 per width. The A/B one-liner: at W=4 the severed design reads +0.899; restoring the old cx(g,p) wound collapses it to +0.120. The coupling is the mechanism.

What is happening · the arena

They collided on real hardware, and the genealogy survived.

The run: IBM ibm_kingston (156-qubit Heron r2), 42 qubits = 2×(W12 germ + 8 body + 1 trait), 12 measured frames, 8,192 shots/frame, QRNG-certified (fail-closed), chain-quality gate passed (twoq_err_max 0.0073, readout_max 0.026), selective DD on both germ lines, ~1 min QPU.

Why they are organisms: each is a germ/soma individual, a clean 12-qubit quantum genealogy (the germ line, carrying certified entanglement inheritance) plus a moving body (the soma) on the track. Two of them.

How they collided: the bodies start at opposite ends of an 8-site track and walk toward each other; the measured occupancy overlap climbs monotonically 0.00 → 0.72 (Z-basis, off the chip), a real collision, not an if (contact) branch; one physical coupling is always on and only acts where both bodies have amplitude.

What held: the joint witness ⟨X⊗2W⟩ over both 24 germ qubits stayed alive across all 12 frames, +0.16…+0.50, mean +0.35, far above the separable null (≈0; cross-lineage cut −0.003). The Weismann barrier on a QPU: bodies collide, gene lines do not.

The inherited method

the claim

One certified witness

The sole quantum claim is an entanglement witness (⟨X⊗W⟩) with no classical surrogate, checked against a separable null and a significance gate (k=2σ headline / k=3σ reported). Alive-counts, ⟨σz⟩, occupancy, all have exact classical surrogates, so carry no quantum claim.

the discipline

Sim-first, hardware-confirm

Fix in statevector sim, then confirm live. Every hardware run is gated: certified QRNG entropy (fail-closed), and a chain-quality gate that aborts if two-qubit error > 0.05 or readout > 0.15. Selective dynamical decoupling protects the gene line only.

the honesty

No speedup claimed

The contribution is scale · faithfulness · certification, not a quantum advantage. Negatives are reported as readings: the σ frontier, the qubit wall, a witness certified, not strong at the top of a sweep. Body-body entanglement is labelled sim-only. Each width is a single N=1 snapshot.
