Quantum Artificial Life · PJ2.2 · 2D-lattice vivarium

A quantum field that moves in space — and the germ line that survives it.

PJ2.2 turns the three 2D-lattice mockups into real runs. The organism is a unary occupancy field on a small square lattice (3×3 = 9 cells, or 4×4 = 16) that walks — each generation is one Trotter layer of an excitation-conserving rxx+ryy quantum walk between von-Neumann neighbours, mass conserved, nothing scripted (position is just where amplitude flows). The single quantum claim is the germ-line genealogical witness ⟨X^⊗W⟩ (W=3). Movement and interaction only — eating / energy / budding were deliberately cut as overclaiming.

Three modes × two arms

  • Solo — one field walks; mass conserved, nothing eaten or born.
  • Replicate — at generation 2 the germ line is CNOT-cloned into a daughter (parent + daughter share one GHZ genealogical witness) and a daughter body is seeded one cell over.
  • Two organisms (duo) — two fields at opposite corners drift together; the co-located rxx+ryy interaction builds a joint two-body witness; overlap = Σ min(occ_A, occ_B).

Each mode runs in two arms: isolated germ line vs coupled (the soma→germ wound). The coupled arm is the A/B kill-switch PJ0/PJ1/PJ2 use — here proving the Weismann barrier under 2D movement.

The certified-frame protocol (the defining lock)

PJ2 baked a whole life cycle into one measured circuit and reconstructed the movie from statevector snapshots. PJ2.2 follows the mockup README's honest recipe instead: on hardware each generation is its own measured circuit — a depth scan (0 walk layers, 1, …, GENS−1), each measured for occupancy (Z) + germ witness (X) with a separable-null control. The integer generations are certified; only the smooth in-between playback the mockup interpolates is a labelled sim artifact.

The occupancy field walks with mass conserved per organism (dominant cell moves across the grid); the replicate field doubles at generation 2; the duo overlap rises from 0 as the fields meet (≈0.7 by gen 1). Noiseless sim pins the isolated witness at the ceiling (+1.0) for every mode — this is the logical-correctness check (isolation, mass conservation, the germ→germ clone, the A/B contrast), not the frontier. The isolated arm always survives in sim; the coupled arm always collapses. Figure: pj2_lattice_witness.png.

Depth is the scarce axis — the hardware datapoint

The shape of the isolated-arm decay with depth, and the generation at which it crosses into classical, are hardware effects a noiseless sim cannot find (as PJ2 found). The mockups' ~5–6-generation ceiling is the prediction; the live depth-scan measures it. On modern Heron-r2, readout error now dominates two-qubit error, and the witness (an X-basis rotation + parity read) is more fragile than the diagonal occupancy — expect the witness to be the first to go. If it dies at small gens, that boundary is the result — the richness-vs-collapse datapoint this ticket contributes to the epic's phase diagram (AC-P3.2). Selective DD on the germ line only is allowed to push the ceiling out; error is real hardware noise, never dressed as biology.
