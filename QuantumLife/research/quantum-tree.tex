\begin{abstract}
Generative art driven by quantum hardware is easy to market and hard to defend: a
108-qubit circuit run once per generation produces a striking image, but the same
image can be reproduced by a classical random-number generator wearing a quantum
label. We take a running quantum-art system, \emph{QuantumTree} --- a virtual tree
whose every growth step is one circuit executed on IBM Heron r2 superconducting
hardware, with the measured bits deciding how it branches and the outcome of each
generation fed back as the belief of the next --- and ask the smallest falsifiable
question the pipeline can actually support: does the neighbour-entangling layer
(a CX ladder plus a controlled-$R_x$ chain of angle $0.7$~rad) leave a
\emph{spatial correlation signature} in the measured 108-bit genome that a
classical, no-entanglement surrogate cannot reproduce? The system's native metric,
per-qubit diversity, is blind to this by construction, so we instrument the pipeline
with the connected two-point correlation $C(d)=\langle b_i b_{i+d}\rangle-\langle
b_i\rangle\langle b_{i+d}\rangle$ and its normalised form $c(d)$. Across three
seeded repeats of 4096 shots on \texttt{ibm\_kingston} we compare a classical
surrogate (arm A), a no-entangler hardware control (arm B0), an entangler-on
hardware run (arm B), and an entangling-depth dose--response sweep (arm C,
layers~$0,1,2,4$). The classical surrogate gives a flat $c(d)\approx0$ (floor
$\sigma_\xi\approx0.008$); the hardware entangler produces a strong, alternating
signature ($c(1)=-0.077$, $c(2)=+0.040$) that is $z=-9.9$ from the surrogate and
$z=-10.6$ from the readout control, and switches on causally --- layers $0$ and $1$
match the control, and the sign flips only at layer~$2$. This rules out the
deflationary reading: the entangling layer does real, tunable, device-produced
work that a PRNG cannot fake. It does \emph{not} yet prove \emph{entanglement}: the
serial ladder reaches ${\sim}800$ logical depth, so the signal is confounded with
decoherence and coherent crosstalk. A preliminary brick-wall (depth-reducing)
redesign is reported; the signature survives but changes shape (the weight migrates
from $c(1)$ to $c(2)$), leaving the entanglement-versus-crosstalk question open and
motivating the decisive low-depth experiment. The honest, reproducible statement is
therefore \emph{``the entangling layer imprints a measurable, causal correlation
signature absent from the classical model,''} not \emph{``we observe
entanglement.''}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Quantum computers have become a fashionable engine for generative art: a circuit is
run, its measured bits steer a drawing, and the result is presented as
``quantum-generated.'' The rhetoric is seductive and the images can be beautiful,
but the scientific content is usually left unstated. The uncomfortable question is
simple: \emph{if a classical pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) can produce a
visually indistinguishable output, in what sense is the artwork quantum at all?}
Randomness alone does not settle this --- a Mersenne-Twister stream is more than
adequate to bend branches and scatter leaves. What a classical surrogate
\emph{cannot} manufacture is genuine multi-qubit correlation created by an
entangling network~\cite{nielsen2010,horodecki2009}. If, and only if, such
correlation survives measurement and can be detected in the output, the ``quantum''
label describes a physical fact rather than a marketing choice.

This paper puts that distinction to a controlled test on a real system.
\emph{QuantumTree} is a generative-art project in which a virtual tree is
\emph{grown}, generation by generation, on IBM Heron r2 superconducting
hardware~\cite{ibmheron,kim2023}. Each generation runs exactly one 108-qubit
circuit; the measured bitstrings are decoded into branch decisions (bend, length,
fork, leaf), and --- crucially --- the per-qubit measurement statistics of
generation $N$ are fed back as the encoded ``belief'' of generation $N+1$. The
result is not a fresh random image per frame but a cumulative, inherited structure:
random variation plus heredity plus a scheduled environment, i.e.\ the shape of
directed change rather than blind noise. The art is real and runs on real hardware.
The scientific claim, however, was never measured. The pipeline's only quantitative
output was per-qubit \emph{diversity} (mean binary entropy), which is provably blind
to entanglement: 108 independent biased coins yield the same diversity as 108
maximally entangled qubits. Nothing in the art pipeline could tell ``quantum'' apart
from ``classical noise with a quantum logo.''

We therefore instrument the pipeline with a metric that \emph{can} see the
difference --- the connected two-point correlation along the qubit chain --- and
formulate the smallest honest, falsifiable question the hardware supports.

\subsection*{Research Question}

\emph{When the tree is grown on real superconducting hardware, does the
neighbour-entangling layer (a CX ladder plus a controlled-$R_x$ chain) leave a
spatial correlation signature in the measured 108-bit genome that a classical,
no-entanglement surrogate provably cannot reproduce --- and if so, is the signature
attributable to the entangling operation causally, rather than to residual
device/readout artefacts?}

\subsection*{Objectives}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Define a connected two-point correlation metric $C(d)$ on the genome bit
stream that is sensitive to inter-qubit correlation and, by construction,
insensitive to per-qubit bias.
\item Establish a classical null (a no-entanglement surrogate) and a hardware
readout floor (an encode-and-measure control with the entangling gates removed).
\item Measure the correlation signature of the entangler-on hardware run against
both baselines with a quantified significance level.
\item Test causality with an entangling-depth dose--response sweep.
\item State precisely which claim the data supports --- and which it does not.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
\item A reproducible protocol that converts a quantum-art pipeline into a
falsifiable experiment, using a bias-robust connected two-point correlation as the
discriminating statistic and paired seeds to hold the environment fixed across arms.
\item A ${\sim}10\sigma$ empirical demonstration on \texttt{ibm\_kingston} that the
entangling layer imprints a spatial correlation signature ($c(1)=-0.077$,
$c(2)=+0.040$) that the classical surrogate ($c(d)\approx0$) and the no-entangler
hardware control ($c(1)=+0.015$) cannot reproduce, and that switches on
\emph{causally} with entangling depth.
\item A deliberately bounded negative: the same data \emph{cannot} yet certify
\emph{entanglement}, because the serial entangling ladder is too deep
(${\sim}800$ logical gates) for the entangled state to survive to measurement, so
the signature is confounded with decoherence and coherent crosstalk. We report a
preliminary depth-reducing (brick-wall) redesign and explain why it is the decisive
follow-up rather than optional polish.
\item A methodological caveat of independent interest: for a sign-alternating
signature the integrated correlation length $\xi=\sum_{d\ge1}c(d)$ is a poor
headline number (the terms partially cancel); $|c(1)|$ or a staggered sum is the
correct summary.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}

\subsection{Randomness, correlation, and the deflationary worry}

A single qubit rotated into superposition and measured yields one Born-rule random
bit~\cite{nielsen2010}; a chain of such qubits, measured independently, is
statistically indistinguishable from a classical random source. Entanglement is the
resource that a product state (and any classical surrogate built from per-qubit
marginals) lacks~\cite{horodecki2009}. Entangling gates create correlations between
qubits that survive measurement as \emph{statistical dependence between bit
positions}. It is exactly this dependence --- not the presence of randomness --- that
distinguishes a genuinely quantum output from a dressed-up PRNG. The natural probe
is the connected two-point correlation function, a standard tool for detecting
spatial structure in many-body systems~\cite{sachdev2011}. Recent
utility-scale experiments on heavy-hex superconducting processors have likewise
argued their case precisely by measuring correlation observables a cheap classical
model fails to match~\cite{kim2023}, which is the template this study follows at art
scale.

\subsection{Generative art and L-systems}

Procedurally grown plants are a classical subject: Lindenmayer systems (L-systems)
generate botanical structure from rewriting rules~\cite{prusinkiewicz1990}, and
generative art more broadly studies systems where the artist specifies a process and
the output emerges from it~\cite{galanter2003}. QuantumTree is an L-system-like
growth engine in which the rewriting decisions are supplied by hardware measurement
and reinforced across generations. The contribution here is not the aesthetic but
the discipline of asking whether the quantum substrate is doing measurable work that
the classical version of the same growth engine cannot.

\subsection{NISQ hardware, decoherence, and crosstalk}

Current processors are noisy and pre-fault-tolerant~\cite{preskill2018}. On IBM
Heron r2 (156 fixed-frequency transmons on a heavy-hex-derived lattice with tunable
couplers~\cite{ibmheron,krantz2019}), single-qubit gates are high-fidelity while
two-qubit gates and readout dominate the error budget. Two mechanisms complicate any
correlation measurement. First, decoherence ($T_1/T_2$) degrades a prepared state as
circuit depth grows, so a deep circuit can wash out the very correlation it
creates~\cite{zurek2003,preskill2018}. Second, \emph{coherent crosstalk} --- unwanted
qubit--qubit coupling and spectator effects during gates --- can imprint spatial
structure that mimics genuine entanglement correlation~\cite{sarovar2020}.
Correlated readout error is a third confound and is the reason a no-entangler
hardware control is mandatory~\cite{temme2017}. This study is designed around these
three threats.

research fork (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}) reuses this engine unchanged and only adds the

\subsection{The one circuit per generation}

Each generation builds and runs a single circuit in five stages: (1)~a
\emph{belief encode}, an $R_y(\theta_i)$ on every qubit ($\theta_i$ starts at
$\pi/2$, a fair coin); (2)~the \emph{entangling network}, repeated
\texttt{layers} times --- an open CX ladder $\mathrm{CX}(i,i{+}1)$ for
$i=0\ldots106$ followed by a controlled-$R_x$ chain $\mathrm{CR}_x(0.7,i,i{+}1)$;
(3)~an \emph{environment} layer of per-slot classical bias rotations (wind, light,
season, and steady fork/leaf pushes); (4)~a \emph{self-mutation} kick carried from
the previous generation; and (5)~measurement of all 108 qubits.
Fig.~\ref{fig:circuit} shows the circuit as transpiled onto \texttt{ibm\_kingston}
at \texttt{optimization\_level=3}: the logical $R_y/\mathrm{CX}/\mathrm{CR}_x/R_x$
program is rewritten into the device's native $\sqrt{X}$, $R_z$, and CZ/ECR gates on
the chosen 108-qubit chain, which is why the entangling stages dominate the depth.
Stage~2 is the heart of the claim: $\mathrm{CX}(i,i{+}1)$ acting on a superposed
qubit creates a Bell-type state $(\lvert00\rangle+\lvert11\rangle)/\sqrt2$ ---
measuring one neighbour constrains the other --- and $\mathrm{CR}_x(0.7)$ adds a
soft, tunable partial rotation on top. Setting \texttt{layers}${=}0$ removes stage~2
entirely, leaving independent qubits: this is the on-hardware correlation floor.

\subsection{Evolution between generations: why it is not a random walk}

There are two layers of randomness and one of them has memory. \emph{Within} a
generation the 4096 shots are independent quantum coin-flips (pure noise on their
own). \emph{Across} generations the system carries memory: after measuring, it
computes $p_i$, the frequency with which qubit $i$ read~1 over all shots, and updates
$\theta_i^{\text{next}} = \mathrm{clamp}(\theta_i + \eta(2p_i-1) + \text{wiggle})$
with reinforcement rate $\eta=0.18$, while a mutation kick $\propto(2p_i-1)$ is
carried forward. This feedback turns ``repeated random measurement'' into growth: the
belief is pushed toward whatever the qubits already prefer, so the tree's branching
\emph{character} crystallises (entropy falls) as it matures, while a small wiggle
keeps individual branches alive. This is the structure of biological evolution ---
random variation, heredity, and environmental pressure --- rather than a memoryless
random walk. Note that two quantities are extracted per generation: the full
per-qubit distribution $p_i$ drives evolution, and the single most-frequent 108-bit
field is what the viewer draws. The two browser viewers (a line-art ``living'' viewer
and a pixel-art ``grove'' that auto-cycles recorded runs) replay this history so one
watches the tree grow, generation by generation, under its scheduled environment.

\subsection{Why this needs measuring, not asserting}

The full 108-qubit entangled state cannot be held classically
($2^{108}\approx3\times10^{32}$ amplitudes), so the ``impossible to simulate at
scale'' claim is true but sterile: it says nothing about whether the \emph{output}
is quantum. The \emph{visual} tree can be faked --- the classical surrogate grows one
too. What a surrogate cannot produce is genuine correlation $C(d)$. Whether that
correlation survives device noise is not a slogan; it is the hypothesis the research
fork tests.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The discriminating statistic}

For a shot record of 108-bit strings we define the connected two-point correlation
along the open chain,
\begin{equation}
C(d) = \operatorname*{mean}_{i}\bigl[\langle b_i b_{i+d}\rangle - \langle
b_i\rangle\langle b_{i+d}\rangle\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\langle\cdot\rangle$ averages over shots and the outer mean averages over
chain positions. We report the normalised form $c(d)=C(d)/C(0)$ (so $c(0)=1$) and
the integrated correlation length $\xi=\sum_{d\ge1}c(d)$. Two properties make $C(d)$
the right probe. First, it is \emph{bias-robust}: subtracting $\langle
b_i\rangle\langle b_{i+d}\rangle$ removes the effect of the deterministic per-slot
environment bias, so per-qubit imbalance alone cannot manufacture $C(d)>0$ --- only
genuine inter-qubit dependence can. Second, $C(d)=0$ for all $d>0$ is exactly the
null produced by a product state or any classical per-marginal surrogate, up to
finite-shot noise $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{\text{shots}})$.

\subsection{Experimental arms}

All hardware arms run on \texttt{ibm\_kingston} with three seeded repeats
($\text{seed}=100,101,102$) at 4096 shots per generation; the environment schedule is
seeded, so an arm run at a given seed sees an \emph{identical} environment ---
differences between arms are attributable to the circuit, not to a different random
tree.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A --- classical null (\texttt{--sim}).} A surrogate with no entangling
gates: each qubit is drawn from its own marginal with only a hand-coded 3-tap
neighbour smoothing. Predicts $c(d)\approx0$, $\xi\approx0$.
\item \textbf{B0 --- hardware control (\texttt{layers}${=}0$).} Encode and measure on
hardware with the entangling stage removed. Any $c(d)$ here is pure device/readout
artefact; this is the floor that is subtracted, so a signal is never compared against
literal zero.
\item \textbf{B --- hardware main (\texttt{layers}${=}2$).} The entangler on, same
seeds and environment as A and B0.
\item \textbf{C --- dose--response sweep (\texttt{layers}${=}0,1,2,4$).} Tests whether
the effect grows with entangling depth (causal) rather than appearing by luck.
\item \textbf{F --- shot-floor calibration (\texttt{--sim}, shots
$256\ldots8192$).} Pins the finite-shot noise level empirically.
\end{itemize}

The qubit chain is chosen per run from live calibration by a time-boxed DFS that
maximises path length while minimising total gate/readout error along it, dropping
dead qubits; the ordered \texttt{qubit\_list} is recorded so arms are never compared
across different physical chains. The evaluation is deterministic and offline: shot
records and generation metadata are stored, and the correlation analysis re-runs
without re-invoking the QPU.

\subsection{Significance bar}

The shot-floor sweep (arm F, Table~\ref{tab:shotfloor}) fixes the noise level. At
4096 shots the simulated $\xi$ scatter is $\sigma_\xi\approx0.008$, and $\xi_{\text{mean}}\to0$
as shots rise --- confirming both that the null behaves and that the metric is sound.
A hardware effect therefore counts as real only if it exceeds ${\sim}3\sigma$ over the
appropriate baseline. Per arm we compute a $z$-score
$z=(\text{signal})/\sqrt{\sigma_{\text{hw}}^2+\sigma_{\text{base}}^2}$ over the
generation samples, using B0-subtracted signals for hardware comparisons.

\subsection{Causality: the dose--response sweep}

Table~\ref{tab:dose} shows the effect switches on with entangling depth rather than
appearing by chance. Layers~0 and~1 are indistinguishable from the readout control
(weak $c(1)\approx+0.02$, dying at $d{=}2$). At \textbf{layer~2 the sign flips} to the
strong staggered pattern, reproducing arm~B on an independent run. By layer~4 the
signal is degraded and scattered (diversity rises to 0.98 and $c(1)$ collapses toward
zero while $c(2)$ swings negative), consistent with the NISQ story that deeper
circuits accumulate more two-qubit-gate error than added correlation. The structure
is thus \emph{caused} by the entangling layer and is non-monotonic in depth --- the
sweet-spot behaviour expected of a noise-limited device, not a linear systematic.

\subsection{The effect is correlation, not bias}

Diversity (mean per-qubit binary entropy) is comparable across all hardware arms
(0.93--0.98) and even the classical surrogate reaches 0.86, while $c(d)$ differs by
${\sim}10\sigma$ between the entangler-on and entangler-off arms. Because a shift in
per-qubit bias would move diversity but the connected $C(d)$ subtracts the marginal
product, this confirms that the effect lives in \emph{correlation between bit
positions}, not in a change of per-bit bias --- which was the entire motivation for
choosing $C(d)$ over the pipeline's native diversity metric.

\subsection{The $\xi$ headline is misleading for this signal}

Because the signature alternates in sign, the integrated length
$\xi=\sum_{d\ge1}c(d)$ partially cancels: for arm~B it reads a tiny $-0.031$ while the
real effect lives in $|c(1)|\approx0.077$ and the staggered $c(2)>0$ tail. A decision
rule keyed on $\xi$ alone (e.g.\ $|\xi|>0.024$) would badly understate the effect.
Future summaries should headline $|c(1)|$ or the staggered sum
$\sum_d(-1)^{d+1}c(d)$, not $\xi$. We report this as a methodological finding: the
compression of a correlation curve into a single scalar must respect the curve's
sign structure.

\subsection{Brick-wall redesign: preliminary Phase-2 result}

To separate genuine entanglement from a depth artefact, the entangling ladder must be
made shallow enough that the entangled state survives to measurement. The serial
ladder fires each neighbour bond one at a time (${\sim}107$ deep per sweep,
${\sim}800$ logical depth at \texttt{layers}${=}2$). A brick-wall schedule fires all
even bonds $(0,1)(2,3)\ldots$ in parallel (depth~1) then all odd bonds
$(1,2)(3,4)\ldots$ (depth~1): identical neighbour physics at ${\sim}50\times$ less
depth. Table~\ref{tab:brickwall} reports a best-effort brick-wall run against its own
\texttt{layers}${=}0$ control. The signature \emph{persists} and remains far above
the classical floor ($|c|\approx0.06$), but its \emph{shape changes}: the weight
migrates from $c(1)$ (serial) to $c(2)$ (brick-wall), while the brick-wall control
carries an unusually large positive $c(1)=+0.064$. Under the pre-registered decision
rule --- ``signature persists and decays cleaner $\Rightarrow$ entanglement;
signature vanishes or changes shape $\Rightarrow$ device artefact'' --- a changed
shape does \emph{not} cleanly earn the entanglement claim. The most likely reading is
that even the brick-wall circuit, at 108 qubits after transpilation, does not reach a
low enough effective depth to isolate surviving entanglement, so the result remains
consistent with a coherent-crosstalk / depth origin. This is why we report it as
preliminary and keep the entanglement claim open.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What is proven}

Two claims are established at ${\sim}10\sigma$. \emph{Not PRNG art:} the classical
surrogate produces flat $c(d)\approx0$; the hardware produces structure it cannot
reproduce ($z=-9.9$). The quantum connection does measurable work a PRNG cannot fake.
\emph{Not a readout artefact:} the no-entangler hardware control gives only a weak
$c(1)=+0.015$ that dies at $d{=}2$, and turning the entangler on flips $c(1)$ to
$-0.077$ with a staggered tail ($z=-10.6$ vs.\ control), switching on causally with
depth. The entangling gates \emph{cause} the signal. For the QuantumTree art project
this is the decisive result: it rules out the deflationary reading. The genome carries
real, device-produced, tunable spatial structure --- the ``quantum'' label describes a
physical fact, not decoration.

\subsection{What is not proven}

The stronger claim of \emph{entanglement} is not yet earned. The serial ladder
reaches ${\sim}800$ logical depth --- far beyond Heron's coherence budget --- so the
measured $c(d)$ may be a decoherence- and crosstalk-shaped pattern rather than
surviving entanglement correlation~\cite{zurek2003,sarovar2020,preskill2018}. The
alternating $c(1)<0,\,c(2)>0$ shape is as consistent with a coherent-error / spectator
pattern arising from the serial ladder's ordering as with a genuine entanglement
decay. Origin is confounded with circuit depth. The preliminary brick-wall run does
not resolve the confound: the signature survives but changes shape, which the
pre-registered rule reads as ``not clean.'' The honest statement is therefore:
\emph{the entangling layer imprints a measurable, causal correlation signature absent
from the classical model} --- not \emph{we observe entanglement}.

\subsection{Relationship to the author's QRNG work}

A companion study by the author on the same hardware family compared entanglement-based
(Bell-pair) and product-state (Hadamard) quantum random number
generators~\cite{jasqrng}, finding that on current Heron fidelities the entangling
construction bought no quality advantage at substantial extra cost. The present result
is complementary: here the entangling layer demonstrably \emph{does} imprint structure
absent from the classical model, but whether that structure is \emph{entanglement} ---
as opposed to a depth-limited coherent artefact --- is exactly the open question. Both
studies converge on the same NISQ-era caution: entangling complexity must be shown to
do useful, isolable work, not assumed to.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\emph{Finite shots.} $C(d)$ carries $\mathcal{O}(1/\text{shots})$ noise; every
hardware $c(d)$ is compared against the \texttt{--sim} floor at the same shots
(Table~\ref{tab:shotfloor}), never against literal zero.
\emph{Transpilation / SWAPs.} Correlation is nearest-neighbour only if the logical
chain maps to a physical chain; the layout picks a SWAP-free path and the
\texttt{qubit\_list} is recorded per run so arms are never compared across chains.
\emph{Bias masquerading as correlation.} The connected $C(d)$ subtracts $p_ip_j$, so
the deterministic per-slot environment bias cannot create $C(d)>0$; paired seeds keep
that bias fixed across arms.
\emph{Correlated readout.} This is precisely what arm B0 measures and what is
subtracted. \emph{Calibration drift.} The chain is re-picked per run from live
calibration; exact $c(d)$ magnitudes should be read as valid within a calibration
window, and the qualitative ordering (A $<$ B0 $\ll$ B) is the robust result.
\emph{Depth confound (the principal limitation).} As above, the serial-ladder depth
prevents attributing the signal specifically to entanglement.

\section{Future Work}

The decisive experiment is a genuine low-depth entangler: run a true brick-wall
schedule at matched \texttt{--seed} and \texttt{--layers} against the serial version,
plus its own \texttt{--sim} null and \texttt{layers}${=}0$ control, on progressively
shorter chains so the effective depth drops below the coherence cliff. If the
signature persists \emph{and decays cleanly / monotonically}, the entanglement claim
is earned; if it vanishes or continues to change shape, the serial signal is device
structure and should be reported as such. Complementary directions: a
\texttt{CROSS\_ANGLE} coupling sweep to test whether correlation strength tracks the
controlled-$R_x$ angle (a second causal handle); replication on a second Heron
processor and across calibration days to bound noise sensitivity; and a lightweight
entanglement witness on short qubit sub-chains, where state tomography is tractable,
to cross-check the correlation-function verdict against a certified measure.

\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether the entangling layer of a quantum-grown generative-art system does
real, measurable work --- whether it imprints a spatial correlation signature on the
108-bit genome that a classical, no-entanglement surrogate cannot reproduce. Across
three seeded repeats on \texttt{ibm\_kingston}, the answer to that question is
unambiguously yes: the hardware entangler produces a strong, alternating,
distance-decaying correlation ($c(1)=-0.077$, $c(2)=+0.040$) that is $z=-9.9$ from the
classical surrogate and $z=-10.6$ from the no-entangler hardware control, and that
switches on causally with entangling depth. The QuantumTree genome therefore carries
structure that is neither PRNG randomness nor readout artefact --- the deflationary
``classical noise with a quantum logo'' reading is ruled out.

The result stops precisely there. It does \emph{not} certify entanglement: the serial
entangling ladder is too deep for the entangled state to survive to measurement, so
the signature cannot be cleanly separated from decoherence and coherent crosstalk, and
a preliminary depth-reduced brick-wall run leaves the question open by producing a
signature of changed shape. The faithful conclusion is that the entangling layer
imprints a measurable, causal correlation signature absent from the classical model,
and that converting ``measurable quantum structure'' (proven) into ``measurable
entanglement'' (not yet proven) requires the low-depth experiment we have specified.
For quantum-driven generative art more broadly, the lesson is methodological: the
claim that an artwork is ``quantum'' is testable, and it should be tested --- with a
bias-robust correlation statistic, a classical null, and a hardware control --- rather
than asserted.
