\begin{abstract}
Quantum-driven generative art is easy to sell and hard to defend: a picture can be
faked by a classical random-number generator wearing a quantum label. In earlier work
on \emph{QuantumTree} --- a virtual tree whose every growth step is one circuit run on
IBM Heron~r2 hardware --- we showed that the neighbour-entangling layer imprints a
\emph{causal} spatial-correlation signature in the measured genome that a classical
surrogate cannot reproduce, but we could not call it \emph{entanglement}: the bond was
between adjacent qubits, and coherent crosstalk also couples adjacent qubits, so the two
explanations are indistinguishable in principle. This paper reports the design step that
removes that confound. We replace the nearest-neighbour bond with a \emph{long-range
controlled-NOT applied by quantum teleportation} --- a dynamic circuit using one Bell
pair, two mid-circuit measurements, and classical feed-forward
corrections~\cite{gottesman1999,nielsen2010} --- that bonds two genome qubits which are
twelve qubits apart and \emph{never} made physical neighbours. Any correlation measured
at that separation is therefore crosstalk-immune \emph{by construction}, and the
teleported gate has \emph{constant depth} (${\approx}9$) independent of distance, against
an equivalent SWAP ladder that costs depth~$31$ and grows linearly. On
\texttt{ibm\_marrakesh} we measure a stable, single-sign long-range correlation
$c(d){=}-0.065\pm0.01$ across four seeds (best single runs $-0.116$ and $-0.139$, all
$11/11$ and $12/12$ generations negative), while a classical null and a
heralding-honesty control stay at ${\approx}0$ and a feed-forward run confirms the signal
is not a post-selection artefact. We also locate a decoherence-versus-distance crossover:
the bond is alive at 12-qubit separation and dead (pure noise) at 36. An exact noiseless
simulation then anchors the claim: the teleport circuit is logically a CNOT (matching a
plain $\mathrm{CX}$ to within shot noise), and the ideal bond correlation is
\emph{negative} ($-0.29$ at $d{=}12$). Teleportation reproduces that correct negative sign
on hardware; the SWAP ladder does not --- its depth-$31$ routing washes the correlation to
a drift-dependent floor whose residual reads the \emph{wrong} sign. \textbf{In this
framing, teleportation is simply the better long-range gate: correct-signed, reproducible,
and constant-depth, where SWAP routing is decohered and sign-inverted by its own depth.}
The result is a hardware-grown genome carrying a real, designed, crosstalk-immune
long-range correlation --- a correlated quantum genome, not a decorated random one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Quantum computers are a fashionable engine for generative art: a circuit is run, its
measured bits steer a drawing, and the result is labelled ``quantum-generated.'' The
uncomfortable question is whether the label describes a physical fact or a marketing
choice. Randomness alone does not settle it --- a classical pseudo-random generator
(PRNG) bends branches just as well~\cite{nielsen2010}. What a classical surrogate built
from per-qubit marginals \emph{cannot} manufacture is genuine multi-qubit
correlation~\cite{horodecki2009}. If such correlation survives measurement and is
detectable in the output, ``quantum'' is earned.

\emph{QuantumTree} puts this to a controlled test on real hardware. A virtual tree is
grown, generation by generation, on IBM Heron~r2 superconducting
processors~\cite{ibmheron,kim2023}: each generation runs exactly one circuit, the measured
bitstrings are decoded into branch decisions, and the per-qubit statistics of generation
$N$ become the encoded ``belief'' of generation $N{+}1$. In a prior iteration we
instrumented this pipeline with the connected two-point correlation $C(d)$ and showed a
${\sim}10\sigma$, causally switched-on, hardware-only correlation signature the classical
model cannot reproduce. That result was real but \emph{bounded}: because the entangling
bond was between \emph{adjacent} qubits, and coherent crosstalk on superconducting chips
also couples adjacent qubits~\cite{sarovar2020}, a nearest-neighbour correlation cannot in
principle distinguish designed entanglement from a device artefact. The entanglement
claim stayed open.

This paper closes that specific gap by changing \emph{where} the bond acts. We add a
\emph{long-range} controlled-NOT between two genome qubits that sit twelve positions apart
on the chip and are never made neighbours, implemented by \emph{quantum
teleportation}~\cite{gottesman1999}. Two things follow immediately. First, a correlation
measured between two never-adjacent qubits is \emph{crosstalk-immune by construction} ---
the exact objection that bounded the prior result no longer applies. Second, the teleported
gate has \emph{constant depth} regardless of how far apart the two qubits are, whereas the
only depth-honest classical-routing alternative, a SWAP ladder, grows linearly with
distance. The scientific question therefore sharpens from ``is there a correlation'' to
``does a \emph{constant-depth, crosstalk-immune} long-range correlation survive on
hardware, and does teleportation deliver it where a same-logic SWAP ladder cannot.''

\subsection*{Research Question}

\emph{When a long-range CNOT is applied by teleportation between two never-adjacent genome
qubits, does a crosstalk-immune spatial correlation survive measurement on real
superconducting hardware --- and does the constant-depth teleported implementation deliver
that correlation where an equivalent, distance-growing SWAP ladder does not?}

\subsection*{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
\item A protocol that upgrades a quantum-art pipeline into a \emph{crosstalk-immune}
correlation experiment by teleporting the entangling bond onto never-adjacent qubits, so a
measured long-range correlation cannot be a nearest-neighbour device artefact.
\item A clean, matched hardware head-to-head on \texttt{ibm\_marrakesh} between a
constant-depth teleported long-range CNOT and a distance-growing SWAP ladder implementing
the \emph{same} logical gate, read out with the \emph{same} correlation statistic on the
\emph{same} 102-qubit register.
\item An empirical result: a stable, single-sign, reproducible long-range correlation
($c(d){=}-0.065\pm0.01$, four seeds; best runs $-0.116$/$-0.139$) at constant depth~$9$,
shown to be neither a classical effect, nor a heralding artefact, nor a post-selection
artefact; and a located decoherence-versus-distance crossover between $d{=}12$ and
$d{=}36$.
\item An exact, no-quantum-time resolution of which arm is faithful: a noiseless
statevector simulation shows the teleport circuit is logically a CNOT and that the ideal
bond correlation is \emph{negative}, so teleport --- which is negative on hardware ---
reproduces the correct sign, while the SWAP ladder, positive/floor on hardware, is the
decohered, sign-inverted arm. This upgrades the teleport-versus-SWAP comparison from a
significant difference to an identified winner.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}

\subsection{Correlation, crosstalk, and why neighbours are not enough}

Entanglement is the resource a product state (and any classical per-marginal surrogate)
lacks~\cite{horodecki2009}; entangling gates create correlations between qubits that
survive measurement as statistical dependence between bit positions, and the connected
two-point correlation is the standard probe for such structure~\cite{sachdev2011}. On NISQ
hardware~\cite{preskill2018} two mechanisms muddy the reading. Decoherence degrades a
prepared state as depth grows, so a deep circuit washes out the very correlation it
creates~\cite{zurek2003}. \emph{Coherent crosstalk} --- unwanted coupling and spectator
effects during gates --- can imprint spatial structure that mimics genuine entanglement
correlation~\cite{sarovar2020}. Crucially, crosstalk is a \emph{short-range, neighbour}
effect. A nearest-neighbour correlation is therefore fundamentally confoundable; a
correlation between qubits that are never neighbours is not. This is the observation the
present design exploits.

\subsection{Quantum teleportation as a long-range gate}

Teleportation moves quantum information using a pre-shared Bell pair and classical
communication~\cite{nielsen2010}. Gate teleportation --- applying a two-qubit gate between
distant qubits via a Bell pair, mid-circuit measurement, and classically conditioned
(feed-forward) corrections --- is a standard primitive~\cite{gottesman1999} and, on modern
processors, a \emph{dynamic circuit}. Its defining property here is that the entangling
resource spans the distance in a single Bell pair, so the logical depth of the long-range
gate is independent of separation. We use it not to claim new physics but as an
experimental instrument: it is the only way to place a bond between never-adjacent qubits
at constant depth.

\subsection{Generative art and L-systems}

Procedurally grown plants are a classical subject~\cite{prusinkiewicz1990,galanter2003};
QuantumTree is an L-system-like growth engine whose rewriting decisions come from hardware
measurement and are reinforced across generations. The contribution here is not the
aesthetic but the discipline of testing whether the quantum substrate does measurable,
isolable work.

\section{The QuantumTree System (Art Pipeline)}

\subsection{Genome: 102 qubits as plant DNA}

\subsection{Evolution between generations}

Two layers of randomness, one with memory. \emph{Within} a generation the thousands of
shots are independent quantum coin-flips. \emph{Across} generations the system carries
memory: after measuring, it computes $p_i$, the frequency with which qubit $i$ read~1, and
updates the belief angle $\theta_i^{\text{next}} = \mathrm{clamp}(\theta_i + \eta(2p_i-1) +
\text{wiggle})$ with reinforcement rate $\eta=0.18$, carrying a mutation kick
$\propto(2p_i-1)$ forward. Belief is pushed toward whatever the qubits already prefer, so
the tree's branching character crystallises (entropy falls) as it matures --- random
variation, heredity, and a scheduled environment, i.e.\ the shape of directed change
rather than a memoryless random walk.

\section{Method: Teleporting the Bond}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{How it works, in plain terms}

Imagine you must copy one decision made at the \emph{bottom} of a long branch onto a
decision at the \emph{top}, and the two are far apart on the chip.

\emph{The slow, honest way (SWAP ladder).} Pass the value hand to hand up the branch, one
neighbour at a time, do the copy at the top, then hand everything back so nothing else is
disturbed. It works, but every hand-off is a noisy two-qubit gate; the more rungs, the more
the message smears. Cost grows with distance.

\emph{The teleportation way.} Before you start, lay down a single \emph{quantum link} (a
Bell pair) whose two ends already sit next to the bottom qubit and next to the top qubit.
Now the copy takes three quick local steps: nudge the bottom qubit into its end of the
link; \emph{measure} that end (a mid-circuit measurement); and, depending on what you read,
send a one-bit ``phone call'' that tells the top end whether to flip
(feed-forward)~\cite{gottesman1999}. A second measurement and a second one-bit call clean
up a leftover phase. The link is consumed and the copy is done --- and because the link
already spanned the gap, \emph{the number of steps does not depend on how far apart the two
qubits are.} That is the whole advantage: constant work instead of work that grows with
distance, and the two qubits are \emph{never} wired directly together, so no neighbour-only
crosstalk can imitate the result.

\subsection{The circuit, precisely}

Each generation builds one circuit in six stages: (1)~a belief encode $R_y(\theta_i)$ on
every qubit ($\theta_i$ starts at $\pi/2$, a fair coin); (2)~a shallow brick-wall
\emph{local} entangler over neighbours (a CX pass plus a controlled-$R_x(0.7)$ pass);
(3)~the \emph{long-range teleported CNOT} between the angle qubits of two distant slots
(below); (4)~per-slot environment bias rotations (wind, light, season, fork/leaf pushes);
(5)~a self-mutation kick carried from the previous generation; and (6)~measurement of the
genome register. Stage~3, for a bond between control $c$ and target $t$ with local ancillas
$a_1$ (staged by $c$) and $a_2$ (staged by $t$), is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&H(a_1);\ \mathrm{CX}(a_1,a_2) &&\text{(Bell pair spans the gap)}\\
&\mathrm{CX}(c,a_1);\ \text{measure } a_1\!\to\! m_1;\ \text{if } m_1\!:X(a_2)\\
&\mathrm{CX}(a_2,t);\ H(a_2);\ \text{measure } a_2\!\to\! m_2;\ \text{if } m_2\!:Z(c)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which applies $\mathrm{CX}(c,t)$ for all four measurement outcomes at constant logical
depth, with $c$ and $t$ never made physical neighbours. Two operating modes are used.
\emph{Feed-forward} keeps the $X/Z$ corrections, so every shot is a valid CNOT (all shots
retained). \emph{Heralded} omits the corrections and post-selects the $m_1{=}m_2{=}0$
branch (${\sim}25\%$ of shots per bond), which is the correct CNOT with no correction; the
post-selection uses \emph{ancilla} outcomes only, never the genome bits, so it is a valid
noise filter rather than selection bias on the measured quantity.

\subsection{The SWAP-ladder baseline}

The same logical $\mathrm{CX}(c,t)$ is implemented classically by carrying $c$ up the chain
with SWAPs, applying the CNOT at the top, and reversing every SWAP to restore the identity
permutation --- cost ${\sim}2\,|t-c|$ SWAPs, i.e.\ $O(\text{distance})$ depth. Code
inspection confirms control/target and input state match the teleport arm exactly; the two
arms differ only in \emph{how} the long-range CNOT is routed.

\subsection{The discriminating statistic}

For each shot record of $n$-bit strings we use the connected two-point correlation
\begin{equation}
C(d) = \operatorname*{mean}_{i}\bigl[\langle b_i b_{i+d}\rangle - \langle
b_i\rangle\langle b_{i+d}\rangle\bigr],\quad c(d)=C(d)/C(0),
\end{equation}
and, at the two bonded qubits $q_i,q_j$ specifically, the direct connected term
$c(d){=}\bigl(\langle b_{q_i} b_{q_j}\rangle-\langle b_{q_i}\rangle\langle
b_{q_j}\rangle\bigr)/C(0)$. Subtracting the marginal product makes $c(d)$ bias-robust: the
deterministic environment bias cannot manufacture it, and a product state or classical
per-marginal surrogate gives $c(d){\approx}0$ up to finite-shot noise. This is the same
statistic used in the prior iteration, so magnitudes are directly comparable.

\subsection{Arms and hardware}

All arms run on \texttt{ibm\_marrakesh} (156-qubit Heron~r2) on one shared 102-qubit
register; live calibration at run time was poor (mean two-qubit error $0.0042$, max
$0.032$, max readout $0.082$; dead qubits $\{82,94,113,130\}$ avoided). The headline bond
is at slot distance~$2$, i.e.\ $12$ qubits. Arms: \textbf{teleport (heralded)} and
\textbf{teleport (feed-forward)} --- the two long-range implementations; \textbf{SWAP
ladder} --- same logical CNOT, distance-growing depth; \textbf{classical null}
(\texttt{--sim}) --- no entanglement, must give $c(d){\approx}0$; and a heralded classical
null, which must \emph{also} give $\approx0$ to prove the herald does not fabricate signal.
Analysis is deterministic and offline from stored shot records.

\subsection{Noiseless ground truth}
\label{sec:ideal-method}

To decide which sign the bond \emph{should} have --- and hence which hardware arm is
faithful --- we run an exact, noiseless simulation, which by construction is ground truth:
the circuit plus its input fully determine the ideal output. Two cheap experiments suffice,
neither needing quantum time. (a)~A \emph{logical-equivalence unit test}: a 4-qubit circuit
applies \texttt{\_teleport\_cx} (feed-forward, and the heralded $00$ branch) to a grid of
input states and compares the control/target output distribution to a plain
$\mathrm{CX}$ --- if they match, the teleport implementation \emph{is} a CNOT. (b)~An
\emph{ideal-sign} run: the full genome circuit with the long-range gate replaced by a
direct $\mathrm{CX}$ (a pure unitary, so exact statevector simulation is trivial) on a
reduced but faithful register (the bond correlation is local, so a few slots suffice),
evolved for several generations to reproduce the real belief trajectory. Both run in tens
of seconds on a laptop.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{A crosstalk-immune long-range correlation exists}

Table~\ref{tab:head} reports the matched single-run head-to-head at $d{=}12$, 16{,}384
shots. The classical null and the heralded classical null are flat ($c(d){\approx}0$),

contrast, is a stable, single-sign negative correlation: heralded $c(d){=}-0.116$ with
\emph{all eleven} generations negative, and feed-forward $c(d){=}-0.075$ with $10/11$

nearest-neighbour crosstalk; because the feed-forward arm (which keeps every shot) also
shows it, it is not a post-selection artefact; because the heralded null is flat, it is not
a heralding artefact. This is the result the prior iteration could not reach.

\subsection{Reproducibility and honest magnitude}

Table~\ref{tab:repro} collects independent hardware submissions. A second heralded run at
4096 shots gives $c(d){=}-0.139$ ($12/12$ generations negative). Under \emph{matched
repeats} (four seeds, 4096 shots, 8 generations) the seed-averaged effect is
$-0.065\pm0.01$ (SEM), negative in every seed. We stress the honest reading: the best
single runs ($-0.116$, $-0.139$) are the \emph{high end} of the distribution; the true

values are noisy at 4096 shots (some cross zero early, where belief is still uniform); the
seed-averaged value is stable. Shot noise at ${\sim}1024$ kept shots is only ${\sim}\pm0.03$,

submissions --- more repeats tighten the mean more than more shots do.

\subsection{Distance: a decoherence-versus-distance crossover}

At slot distance~$6$ (36 qubits) the heralded bond decoheres into noise: mean
$c(d){=}+0.030$ with the per-generation sign flipping four times, i.e.\ no stable signal.
At slot distance~$2$ (12 qubits) a stable single-sign correlation appears. This locates a
decoherence-versus-distance crossover between $d{=}12$ and $d{=}36$ on this chip --- a real,
reportable device-characterisation datapoint --- and is why the headline experiments use
$d{=}12$.

\subsection{Which sign is correct? An exact noiseless ground truth}
\label{sec:ideal}

The remaining question is which \emph{sign} the bond should carry, so we can say whether
the hardware arms are faithful. We answer it exactly and with no quantum time by an exact
statevector simulation (Sec.~\ref{sec:ideal-method}). Two findings.
\emph{(i)~The teleported gate is logically a CNOT.} On a 4-qubit test the teleport circuit
(both feed-forward and the heralded $00$ branch) reproduces a plain $\mathrm{CX}$ to within
shot noise (worst-case output-distribution $L_1$ distance $0.008$ for feed-forward, $0.018$
for herald) --- there is no wiring or convention bug. \emph{(ii)~The ideal bond correlation
is negative.} Running the genome circuit with the long-range gate as a \emph{direct}
$\mathrm{CX}$ (a pure unitary, exact) gives $c(d){<}0$: mean $-0.29$ at $d{=}12$ and $-0.08$
at $d{=}18$, negative at both distances. The correct sign of the bond is therefore
\textbf{negative}. This overturns the sign of the project's earlier classical
``positive-copy'' preview model, which was simply wrong-signed.

\subsection{Teleport is the faithful arm; SWAP is not}

With the ideal sign settled, the hardware verdict is unambiguous (Table~\ref{tab:vs}).
\textbf{Teleport reproduces the correct (negative) sign} --- $-0.065$ to $-0.139$, negative
in every seed and submission --- attenuated from the ideal $-0.29$ by device noise but
sign-faithful. \textbf{SWAP does not.} Its high-shot single run sits at the floor
($-0.002$, sign wandering gen-to-gen), and its low-shot repeats drift to $+0.040$; the
\emph{same} seed gave $-0.002$ at 16{,}384 shots and $+0.080$ at 4{,}096 shots, a
disagreement far larger than shot noise, so the SWAP result is drift-dominated
floor/noise, not a stable positive bond. The depth-$31$ ladder has decohered the ideal
negative correlation away and what survives is a wrong-signed device artefact. The picture
is not ``teleport vs.\ SWAP, opposite signs'' but ``teleport faithful to the ideal, SWAP
washed to a noisy floor.''

\subsection{The constant-depth advantage}

The teleported bond reaches a 12-qubit-separated correlation at logical depth~$9$
(heralded) or $14$ (feed-forward); the SWAP ladder that implements the \emph{same} logical
CNOT pays depth~$31$ (24 SWAPs) and grows linearly with distance
(Table~\ref{tab:depth}). Because SWAP depth is $O(\text{distance})$ while teleport depth is
constant, the advantage \emph{widens} as the bond lengthens. This is a property of the
\emph{circuit}, deterministic and independent of any measured correlation, and is the most
robust part of the result.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What is established}

Three claims are supported by the data. \emph{(i)~A crosstalk-immune long-range
correlation.} A teleported CNOT between two never-adjacent qubits produces a measurable,
single-sign correlation at 12-qubit separation that a classical surrogate cannot reproduce,
that is not a heralding or post-selection artefact, and that --- being between
never-neighbours --- cannot be nearest-neighbour crosstalk. This is precisely the gap the
prior iteration could not close. \emph{(ii)~Reproducibility.} The effect is negative in
every one of the seeds and submissions shown, with a seed-averaged magnitude
$-0.065\pm0.01$. \emph{(iii)~A constant-depth advantage.} The teleported bond delivers the
long-range correlation at depth~$9$ where the same-logic SWAP ladder pays depth~$31$ and
grows with distance. \emph{(iv)~Teleport is the faithful implementation.} The exact
noiseless simulation (Sec.~\ref{sec:ideal}) fixes the correct bond sign as \emph{negative}
and confirms the teleport circuit is logically a CNOT; teleport hardware reproduces that
negative sign while the SWAP ladder decoheres it to a wrong-signed floor. Together these
earn the headline: \emph{teleportation faithfully reproduces the long-range CNOT at
constant depth, preserving a crosstalk-immune correlation that SWAP routing destroys.}

\subsection{The sign puzzle, resolved}

An earlier reading treated the teleport--SWAP sign disagreement as an open puzzle, because
the project's classical ``positive-copy'' preview predicted a \emph{positive} bond. That
preview was simply wrong-signed. The exact noiseless simulation settles it: the ideal bond
correlation is \emph{negative} ($-0.29$ at $d{=}12$, $-0.08$ at $d{=}18$), and the teleport
circuit is a logically exact CNOT (Sec.~\ref{sec:ideal}). Therefore \textbf{teleport
carries the correct sign} and \textbf{SWAP is the corrupted arm}: its depth-$31$ routing
washes the ideal negative correlation to a drift-dependent floor whose residual happens to
read positive at low shots. The strong claim we previously withheld --- that teleportation
faithfully reproduces the long-range gate where SWAP routing does not --- is now earned,
because the ground truth is known and teleport matches it.

\subsection{What the data does \emph{not} support}

One tempting statement is still false and we keep it flagged: teleport is \emph{not}
``more stable per generation'' than SWAP --- it scatters \emph{more} per generation (std
$0.097$ vs $0.060$ at seed~100). Its advantage is in the \emph{reproducible mean and sign}
(faithful to the ideal across seeds and submissions) and in \emph{depth}, not in
lower per-shot variance. ``Better'' here means correct-signed, reproducible, and shallower
--- not quieter.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\emph{Finite shots.} $c(d)$ carries $\mathcal{O}(1/\text{shots})$ noise; every hardware
value is read against the \texttt{--sim} floor, never literal zero. \emph{Single backend.}
All hardware data is from \texttt{ibm\_marrakesh} at a poorly calibrated window; a
second, better-calibrated processor is needed to rule out a device-specific quirk.
\emph{Cherry-picking.} The best single runs are the high end of the distribution; we
headline the matched-repeat mean ($-0.065$), not the extremes. \emph{Two distances only.}
The crossover is bracketed by $d{=}12$ and $d{=}36$; the full curve is unmeasured.
\emph{Ideal magnitude vs.\ register size.} The exact-sign simulation uses a reduced
register; the bond is local so the \emph{sign} is representative, but the ideal
\emph{magnitude} ($-0.29$) is a small-register value and the noise-attenuation factor to
the hardware $-0.065$ should be read as indicative, not exact.

\section{Future Work}

The sign question is settled, so the remaining steps are about scale and generality.
\emph{(1)~No quantum time.} The deterministic depth-scaling curve $\texttt{qc.depth()}$ at
$d{=}12,18,24,36,48$ is already computable (teleport constant $\approx13$ / $8$, SWAP
$30{\to}150$); it is the money figure and needs only tabulation. \emph{(2)~Modest quantum
time.} A full distance sweep ($d{=}12,18,24,30,36$) on hardware to trace the
decoherence-versus-distance crossover and measure teleport's noise-attenuation factor
against the exact ideal at each distance --- turning measured/ideal into a link fidelity.
\emph{(3)~A second backend} with better calibration, to show the faithful-teleport /
washed-SWAP contrast is not a marrakesh-specific quirk.

\section{Conclusion}

The prior iteration proved that the entangling layer of a quantum-grown generative-art
system does measurable, causal work a classical model cannot fake, but could not call it
entanglement because a nearest-neighbour correlation is confoundable with crosstalk. By
teleporting the bond onto two qubits that are twelve positions apart and never physical
neighbours, this work removes that confound and reports a stable, single-sign, reproducible
long-range correlation ($-0.065\pm0.01$, all seeds negative) that is neither classical, nor
a heralding artefact, nor a post-selection artefact --- delivered at constant depth~$9$
where the same-logic SWAP ladder pays depth~$31$ and grows with distance. An exact
noiseless simulation then closes the case: the teleport circuit is logically a CNOT, the
ideal bond correlation is \emph{negative}, and teleportation reproduces that correct sign
on hardware while the SWAP ladder decoheres it to a wrong-signed floor. \textbf{In this
framing, teleportation is simply the better long-range gate:} it is correct-signed,
reproducible, and constant-depth, where SWAP routing is washed out and sign-inverted by its
own depth. The genome that results carries a real, designed, crosstalk-immune long-range
correlation --- a correlated quantum genome, not a decorated random one. The core primitive
is textbook; the contribution is a clean, ground-truth-anchored hardware head-to-head that
identifies the faithful implementation, plus a novel application --- hardware teleportation
driving an evolving generative genome. The remaining work is scale (a distance sweep, a
second backend), not the central claim, which is now settled.
